# How to practise

<span class="tag tag-other">Practice</span>

After this lesson you can run a 30-minute practice session with a warm-up, a new skill and a review, keep time with a metronome, and record yourself to check your own playing.

Before this: [How to use this book](../00-start-here/index.md). You need: your piano, your guitar, and a phone with a metronome app and a camera ([free apps and gear](../00-start-here/free-apps-and-gear.md) lists some).

## The idea

The page on [how to use this book](../00-start-here/index.md) explains the stages, the lesson layout and the practice tools. This lesson covers what happens at the instrument: how a 30-minute session is built and how to practise so that it sticks.

### Why every day, and why repeat

Playing an instrument is a physical skill, like typing or juggling. Your hands learn it by repetition, and some of the learning happens in the hours between sessions as well as while you play. A pattern that feels clumsy on Monday is often smoother on Wednesday even if you did nothing in between.

So 30 minutes on each of five days usually works better than one long session on Sunday. This is why every routine in this book ends with "Repeat this routine on at least three days", and why the "Move on when" boxes come after the repeats. One good session does not mean the skill is learned. Three days of the same routine get it much closer.

### The shape of a session

Each routine fills 30 minutes in four parts. The names are the same in every lesson, so you always know where you are.

| Part | Minutes | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| Warm-up | 5 | Something easy you already know, to wake up your hands and ears |
| New skill | 15 | The thing this lesson teaches, in small pieces |
| Reading or ear training | 5 | Short reading, listening or flashcards |
| Other instrument | 5 | A quick turn on the instrument this lesson is not about |

Both instruments get played every day. Five minutes is short, but it keeps the other instrument from going cold while you work on the first.

### Slow practice and three clean in a row

Play new material slower than feels necessary, at a speed where you make no mistakes. Speed comes from repeating correct playing, not from rushing and hoping. A mistake played ten times is a habit you then have to undo.

To check yourself, use the rule of **three clean in a row**: play the passage three times in a row with no stops and no wrong notes. One mistake sends the count back to zero. If the count resets twice in a row, slow down by 10 beats per minute and start again.

### The metronome and beats per minute

A **metronome** is a device or app that clicks at a steady speed. Your digital piano has one built in, and phone apps are free. The speed is the **tempo**, and it is counted in **beats per minute (bpm)**: at 60 bpm there is one click each second, and at 120 bpm there are two. Lesson 7 explains beats and tempo properly. For now you only need to follow the click.

1. Open the metronome and set the tempo number to 60.
2. Start it and listen for four clicks.
3. Tap one finger on a table with each click.
4. Listen for a single sound. If you hear a tap and a click as two sounds, you are early or late; adjust until they merge.

### Recording yourself

Your ears are busy while you play, so some problems are only audible on a recording and others only visible on video. Prop your phone against something, record 30 seconds, and watch it twice: once with the sound off to check how you sit and move, and once with your eyes closed to listen. Choose one thing to change before the next session. Keep the videos; comparing day 1 with day 7 is encouraging and shows what changed.

### The tags and the two paths

Every lesson has a coloured tag under its title, such as Piano, Guitar, Theory or Practice. This lesson is tagged Practice because it is about both instruments. The how-to-use page explains how to follow only the piano or only the guitar tags if you want to focus for a while.

### Pain is a stop sign

Tired fingers are normal. Pain in a hand, wrist, arm, shoulder or back is not. If something hurts, stop, shake your hands out, check how you are sitting, and play for a shorter time. If the pain keeps coming back, see a teacher or a doctor.

## Try it

Exercise 1. At 60 bpm, how many clicks are there in 30 seconds? At 90 bpm, how many in one minute? At 120 bpm, how long is the gap between two clicks?

<details class="solution" markdown>
<summary>Answer</summary>

30 clicks; 90 clicks; half a second (120 clicks in 60 seconds means 60 / 120 = 0.5 seconds each).

</details>

Exercise 2. You play a two-bar passage cleanly, cleanly, then make a mistake on the third try. What is your count, and what do you do next?

<details class="solution" markdown>
<summary>Answer</summary>

The count goes back to zero. Play the passage again from the start. If it resets a second time in a row, slow the metronome down by 10 bpm.

</details>

### Practice routine (30 minutes): your first session

| Minutes | What to do | Tempo | Listen for |
|---|---|---|---|
| 5 | Set up: open the metronome app, set a place for your phone to stand, decide on a daily time | None | Nothing yet; make it easy to start tomorrow |
| 5 | Metronome: tap a finger on a table with the click for one minute at 60 bpm, then 80, then 50 | 60, 80, 50 bpm | The tap and the click sound as one |
| 5 | Piano: sit at the piano, rest your hands in your lap, then on the keys. Film 20 seconds from the side | None | Where your back, shoulders and wrists are |
| 5 | Guitar: sit with the guitar in your lap in a way that feels natural. Film 20 seconds from the front | None | Where the guitar rests and where your arms go |
| 5 | Watch both videos, then write one thing to change for each instrument | None | One change per instrument, not five |
| 5 | Metronome again at 60 bpm, with your eyes closed this time | 60 bpm | Whether you still tap with the click |

Repeat this routine on at least three days. On days 2 and 3, record new videos and compare them with day 1.

</div>

### Move on when

- [ ] You can tap with the metronome at 60 bpm for one minute and 80 bpm for one minute, without drifting away from the click.
- [ ] You have two videos saved, one at each instrument, and a written note of one thing to change in each.
- [ ] You have a fixed daily time, and your first three practice days are marked on a calendar.
- [ ] You can explain "three clean in a row" to someone else in one sentence.

</div>

## Common mistakes

Playing too fast. A tempo where you stumble practises the stumble. Slow the metronome until three clean in a row is easy.

Skipping the metronome because it feels strict. The click is the thing that shows you when you rush or drag, so keep it on for every exercise that gives a bpm.

Practising once for a long time instead of often for a short time. If you miss a day, play the next day at the normal 30 minutes. Do not try to catch up with an hour.

## Key terms

- **Practice routine**: a timed plan for a session, with a warm-up, a new skill, reading or ear training, and time on the other instrument.
- **Metronome**: a device or app that clicks at a steady speed.
- **Tempo**: the speed of the music.
- **Beats per minute (bpm)**: the unit for tempo; 60 bpm is one click per second.
- **Three clean in a row**: playing a passage three times with no stops and no wrong notes; one mistake resets the count.
