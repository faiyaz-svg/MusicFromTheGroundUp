# Beat, tempo and note values

<span class="tag tag-theory">Theory</span>

After this lesson you can feel and count a steady beat, set a tempo in beats per minute, and clap rhythms made of quarter notes, half notes and whole notes.

Before this: [How to practise](../01-first-sounds/01-how-to-practise.md). You need: a metronome (on your piano or your phone). You can also use your piano and guitar for the routine.

## The idea

### The beat

Put on any song you know and tap your foot. The even pulse you are tapping is the **beat**. It is like a heartbeat under the music, and most music has one you can tap along to.

### Tempo and beats per minute

The **tempo** is how fast the beat goes. We count it in **beats per minute (bpm)**: the number of beats in one minute. At 60 bpm there is one beat each second. At 120 bpm there are two beats each second, so the music is twice as fast.

The time between beats is 60 divided by the bpm, in seconds. At 60 bpm that is 1 second, at 120 bpm it is 0.5 seconds, and at 80 bpm it is 0.75 seconds.

To feel this, set the metronome to 60 bpm and tap or clap with every click. Then try 80 and 100, and notice how the same tapping hand needs to move faster.

### Note values

Music writes down how long each sound lasts. A **note value** says how many beats a note lasts. Three note values are enough for now.

| Note | Name | Length | Count |
|---|---|---|---|
| Filled head with a stem | Quarter note | 1 beat | 1 |
| Open head with a stem | Half note | 2 beats | 1 2 |
| Open head, no stem | Whole note | 4 beats | 1 2 3 4 |

A half note is as long as two quarter notes, and a whole note is as long as four. The notes also tell you when each sound starts. In a half note, you play on "1" and hold through "2".

### Counting aloud

Count the beats aloud as "1 2 3 4" over and over, in time with the metronome. When you play a note, play it on the number where it starts. The numbers tell you how long to hold it.

Listen first. These are four quarter notes in a row, twice. Count "1 2 3 4" on each group and clap on every number. The 4/4 at the start and the vertical lines are explained in lesson 8. For now, each vertical line means "start counting at 1 again".

```abc
X:1
M:4/4
L:1/4
Q:1/4=60
K:C
C C C C | C C C C |]
```

Next, half notes. Clap on 1 and 3, and hold through 2 and 4 by keeping your hands apart (don't clap). Count all four numbers aloud.

```abc
X:1
M:4/4
L:1/4
Q:1/4=60
K:C
C2 C2 | C2 C2 |]
```

Now a mix, with a whole note at the end. Count every beat aloud. The whole note is played on 1 and held for four counts.

```abc
X:1
M:4/4
L:1/4
Q:1/4=60
K:C
C C C2 | C2 C C | C4 |]
```

To clap a rhythm, clap only at the start of each note. Don't clap during the held part of a half or whole note, but keep counting aloud so your hands stay in time.

### Playing the rhythms

You can play the same rhythms on your instruments. On the piano, play any single key. On the guitar, pluck any open string. Play on the number where each note starts, and let the note ring through the counts.

## Try it

Exercise 1. How many beats are in one half note, one quarter note and one whole note?

<details class="solution" markdown>
<summary>Answer</summary>

Half note: 2. Quarter note: 1. Whole note: 4.

</details>

Exercise 2. The pattern is quarter, quarter, half. How many beats is that in total?

<details class="solution" markdown>
<summary>Answer</summary>

1 + 1 + 2 = 4 beats.

</details>

Exercise 3. How long does one whole note last at 60 bpm, and at 120 bpm? And a half note at 80 bpm?

<details class="solution" markdown>
<summary>Answer</summary>

A whole note lasts 4 beats. At 60 bpm that is 4 seconds, and at 120 bpm it is 2 seconds. A half note lasts 2 beats, and at 80 bpm each beat is 0.75 seconds, so it lasts 1.5 seconds.

</details>

<div class="practice" markdown>

### Practice routine (30 minutes)

| Minutes | What to do | Tempo | Listen for |
|---|---|---|---|
| 5 | Warm-up: tap the beat on a table with the metronome | 60 bpm, then 80 | Tap and click sounding as one |
| 10 | Clap and count the three examples, one after another, each three times | 60 bpm | Hands exactly on the clicks |
| 5 | Piano: play the three examples on one key (right hand finger 3) | 60 bpm | The held notes ringing for their full length |
| 5 | Guitar: play the three examples on an open string | 60 bpm | The same count as on the piano |
| 5 | Ear: close the book, clap back after listening to an example, then check | 60 bpm | Hearing the long and short notes |

Repeat this routine on at least three days. On day 3, try the clapping at 72 bpm.

</div>

### Move on when

- [ ] You can clap the three examples at 60 bpm, three times each with no stops, while counting aloud.
- [ ] You can tap 30 beats at 60 bpm with the click off, and a clock shows you finished between 27 and 33 seconds.
- [ ] You can say how many beats each of the three note values lasts without looking.
- [ ] You can play all three examples on both instruments at 60 bpm without losing the count.

## Common mistakes

Rushing the held notes. Half and whole notes feel long, so beginners cut them short. Keep counting aloud until the next note.

Clapping every beat instead of every note. Clap only where a note starts.

Counting faster when the music gets harder. The count must stay on the click. If you are speeding up, slow the metronome and start again.

## Key terms

- **Beat**: the steady pulse of the music.
- **Tempo**: how fast the beat goes.
- **Beats per minute (bpm)**: the number of beats in one minute.
- **Note value**: how many beats a note lasts.
- **Quarter note**: a note lasting 1 beat.
- **Half note**: a note lasting 2 beats.
- **Whole note**: a note lasting 4 beats.
