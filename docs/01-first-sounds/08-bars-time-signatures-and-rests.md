# Bars, time signatures and rests

<span class="tag tag-theory">Theory</span>

After this lesson you can read bars and bar lines, read the time signatures 4/4 and 3/4, and count the quarter, half and whole rests as silent beats.

Before this: [Beat, tempo and note values](../01-first-sounds/07-beat-tempo-and-note-values.md). You need: a metronome, and your piano and guitar for the routine.

## The idea

### Bars

In lesson 7 you counted "1 2 3 4" again and again. Music groups the beats in the same way. A group of beats is a **bar** (also called a measure), and a thin vertical line marks the end of each bar: the **bar line**. The first beat of a bar is the strongest, and the count restarts at 1 in every bar.

A thin line followed by a thick one marks the end of a piece: the **final bar line**. Two thin lines form a **double bar line**, which marks a new section. Every example here ends with the final bar line.

### The time signature

The **time signature** is two numbers stacked at the start of the music. It says how the beats are grouped.

- The top number says how many beats are in each bar.
- The bottom number says which note value is one beat. A 4 on the bottom means the quarter note is one beat.

In **4/4**, each bar has four quarter-note beats. You count "1 2 3 4". In **3/4**, each bar has three quarter-note beats, and you count "1 2 3". A waltz is in 3/4. A third common one is **2/4**: two beats per bar, counted "1 2", as in a march.

Listen to the difference. Both examples play the same C on every beat. The first is in 4/4 and the second is in 3/4. Count aloud and feel where the bar starts.

```abc
X:1
M:4/4
L:1/4
Q:1/4=60
K:C
C C C C | C C C C |]
```

```abc
X:1
M:3/4
L:1/4
Q:1/4=60
K:C
C C C | C C C | C C C |]
```

A note's length must fit in the bar: in 4/4 the note values in a bar add up to 4 beats, and in 3/4 they add up to 3. If a bar adds up to anything else, it is wrong.

### Rests

A **rest** is a silence that lasts a set number of beats. You keep counting through a rest, but you play nothing. Each note value has a matching rest.

| Rest | Looks like | Length |
|---|---|---|
| Quarter rest | A squiggly line | 1 beat |
| Half rest | A small block sitting on top of a line | 2 beats |
| Whole rest | A small block hanging below a line | A whole bar of silence; 4 beats in 4/4 |

The lines they sit on are the lines of the staff, which lesson 9 explains. To tell the two blocks apart: the half rest sits on the line like a hat, and the whole rest hangs from the line like a bag. In ABC, which the examples use, a rest is written with `z`.

Count rests aloud, so you do not lose your place. Put the rest number in brackets in your head: "1 (2) 3 4" for a quarter note, a rest, then two quarter notes. Clap on the notes only, and keep your hands apart on the rest. In this example, bar 1 has a quarter rest on beat 3, and bar 2 has a half rest on beats 3 and 4.

```abc
X:1
M:4/4
L:1/4
Q:1/4=60
K:C
C C z C | C2 z2 | C z C C | C4 |]
```

Now a bar of silence. In bar 2, the whole bar rests, so you count "1 2 3 4" and play nothing.

```abc
X:1
M:4/4
L:1/4
Q:1/4=60
K:C
C C C C | z4 | C2 C2 | C4 |]
```

In 3/4, the same idea. Each bar holds three beats.

```abc
X:1
M:3/4
L:1/4
Q:1/4=60
K:C
C C C | C2 z | C z C | C C z |]
```

## Try it

Exercise 1. In 4/4, how many beats are in a bar made of a half note, a quarter note and a quarter rest?

<details class="solution" markdown>
<summary>Answer</summary>

2 + 1 + 1 = 4 beats. The bar is complete.

</details>

Exercise 2. In 3/4, the bar has a half note and a quarter note. How many beats are in the bar, and is it complete?

<details class="solution" markdown>
<summary>Answer</summary>

2 + 1 = 3 beats, so the bar is complete in 3/4.

</details>

Exercise 3. A 4/4 bar has a quarter note, a quarter rest and a half rest. How many beats of sound does it have, and how many of silence?

<details class="solution" markdown>
<summary>Answer</summary>

One beat of sound and three beats of silence (1 + 1 + 2 = 4 beats in total).

</details>

### Practice routine (30 minutes)

| Minutes | What to do | Tempo | Listen for |
|---|---|---|---|
| 5 | Warm-up: count "1 2 3 4" aloud with the metronome, then "1 2 3" | 60 bpm | Saying 1 exactly on the first click of every bar |
| 10 | Clap and count the five examples, saying "(2)" for silent beats | 60 bpm | Hands apart on every rest |
| 5 | Piano: play the 4/4 examples with a rest on one key | 60 bpm | Silence for the full length of each rest |
| 5 | Guitar: play the 3/4 example on an open string; lay a finger of your picking hand on the string to stop it on a rest | 60 bpm | A silent rest that stops the sound |
| 5 | Ear: someone claps a bar of 4/4 with a rest, you clap it back, then say where the rest was | 60 bpm | The gap in the pattern |

Repeat this routine on at least three days. Rests are easy to rush until the count is automatic.

## Common mistakes

Skipping the rest. A rest is a beat you count and do not play. If you drop it, the next note comes early.

Mixing up the time signature numbers. The top is how many beats, the bottom is the kind of note that gets one beat.

Counting 1 2 3 4 in a 3/4 bar. Count the beats the time signature gives you, so that the "1" lands on the first beat of every bar.

## Key terms

- **Bar** (also called a measure): a group of beats between two bar lines.
- **Bar line**: the vertical line that marks the end of a bar.
- **Double bar line**: two thin lines marking a new section.
- **Time signature**: two numbers at the start that say how many beats are in a bar and which note gets one beat.
- **Rest**: a silence with a set length in beats.
- **Quarter rest**, **half rest**, **whole rest**: rests lasting 1 beat, 2 beats and a full bar.
