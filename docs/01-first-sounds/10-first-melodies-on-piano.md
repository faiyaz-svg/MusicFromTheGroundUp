# First melodies on piano

<span class="tag tag-piano">Piano</span>

After this lesson you can play five short melodies with your right hand in C position, from the page, at 60 bpm.

Before this: [The treble staff and C position](../01-first-sounds/09-treble-staff-and-c-position.md). You need: your piano and a metronome.

## The idea

Every melody in this lesson uses only the five notes of C position. Keep your right hand still, with the thumb on middle C, and let each finger play its own key: finger 1 plays C, 2 plays D, 3 plays E, 4 plays F, 5 plays G. The finger numbers are printed at the start of each line, and you can work out the rest from the notes.

### How to learn a melody

Use these steps for every melody, in this order.

1. Read it away from the piano. Say the name of the first note of each bar, and notice where the melody goes up, down or repeats.
2. Clap and count the rhythm aloud, with the metronome at 60 bpm.
3. Put your hand in C position and play the first two bars at 50 bpm.
4. Play bars 3 and 4 the same way, then the first four bars together.
5. Do the same with the second half, then put the halves together.
6. Count three clean in a row at 60 bpm, with the metronome on.

Common mistake at step 3: stopping to look for the next key. Keep your eyes on the page. If a bar is hard, say the note names aloud and play it four times slowly.

### Hot Cross Buns

This is a traditional tune, with the rhythm simplified to quarter notes. It uses only three notes, E, D and C, and is the easiest of the five. Listen for the pattern: bars 1 and 2 are the same, bar 3 repeats each note, and bar 4 brings back the first phrase.

```abc
X:1
T:Hot Cross Buns
C:Traditional
M:4/4
L:1/4
Q:1/4=60
K:C
!3!E D C2 | E D C2 | C C D D | E D C2 |]
```

### Mary Had a Little Lamb

Another traditional tune, with eight bars. It starts on finger 3. The notes move by step (to the next key) and repeat, and in bar 4 the melody makes one skip up from E to G. Listen for the three repeated notes in bars 2 and 6.

```abc
X:1
T:Mary Had a Little Lamb
C:Traditional
M:4/4
L:1/4
Q:1/4=60
K:C
!3!E D C D | E E E2 | D D D2 | E G G2 |
!3!E D C D | E E E E | D D E D | C4 |]
```

### Ode to Joy

The theme from the last movement of Beethoven's Ninth Symphony, a public-domain piece. The rhythm is simplified here: the original has a dotted note in bars 4 and 8, which is covered in a later stage. Listen to how the second half answers the first, and how bars 4 and 8 differ: the first ends on D, which sounds unfinished, and the second ends on C, which sounds finished.

```abc
X:1
T:Ode to Joy (theme, simplified rhythm)
C:Ludwig van Beethoven
M:4/4
L:1/4
Q:1/4=60
K:C
!3!E E F G | G F E D | !1!C C D E | E D D2 |
!3!E E F G | G F E D | !1!C C D E | D C C2 |]
```

### Morning in C

An original melody for this book. It rises through the first bar, rests for two beats in bar 4, and comes home to C. Bars 5 to 8 come back down by step. Count the rest in bar 4.

```abc
X:1
T:Morning in C
C:Original, for this book
M:4/4
L:1/4
Q:1/4=60
K:C
!1!C D E G | E2 D2 | C D E F | G2 z2 |
G F E D | E2 C2 | D E D C | C4 |]
```

### Lantern Walk

An original melody in 3/4, so you count "1 2 3". It has a rest in bars 4 and 8. Count "1 2 3" and keep the count going through each rest.

```abc
X:1
T:Lantern Walk
C:Original, for this book
M:3/4
L:1/4
Q:1/4=60
K:C
!1!C E G | G2 F | E D C | D2 z |
E E F | G2 E | D E D | C2 z |]
```

## Try it

Exercise 1. In Mary Had a Little Lamb, which bar has the skip from E to G, and which finger plays G?

<details class="solution" markdown>
<summary>Answer</summary>

Bar 4 (E G G2). Finger 5 plays G.

</details>

Exercise 2. In Morning in C, how many beats of silence are in the whole piece?

<details class="solution" markdown>
<summary>Answer</summary>

Two beats, in bar 4 (a half rest, written z2).

</details>

Exercise 3. In Lantern Walk, which finger plays the first note, and what is the first bar's total in beats?

<details class="solution" markdown>
<summary>Answer</summary>

Finger 1 plays C, and the bar has 3 beats (three quarter notes).

</details>

### Practice routine (30 minutes)

| Minutes | What to do | Tempo | Listen for |
|---|---|---|---|
| 5 | Warm-up: five-finger pattern C D E F G and back, then the first line of Hot Cross Buns | 60 bpm | Even volume on every finger |
| 15 | New skill: learn Mary Had a Little Lamb, then Ode to Joy, with the six steps in "How to learn a melody", two bars at a time | 50 bpm, then 60 | A steady beat through bar lines |
| 5 | Reading: [Reading drill](../practice/reading-drill.md), notes C4 to G4 | Untimed | Naming notes at once |
| 5 | Guitar: tune, then play string 1 open, fret 1 and fret 3 on each beat | 60 bpm | No buzz |

Repeat this routine on at least three days. When you are ready for another melody, put it in the 15 minutes: Morning in C one week, Lantern Walk the next.

</div>

## Common mistakes

Moving the hand out of C position. If you find yourself reaching, check that your thumb is still on middle C. The tune never leaves the five keys.

Rushing the long notes. Half notes need two beats. Count them aloud until the length is automatic.

Playing the rest as a short note. In a rest, lift your fingers and sit still. Count "1 2" of silence in bar 4 of Morning in C.

## Key terms

- **Melody**: a line of single notes that makes a tune.
- **Phrase**: a short musical idea within a melody, like a line of a sentence.
- **Traditional**: a tune from folk tradition, with no known composer, free to use.
