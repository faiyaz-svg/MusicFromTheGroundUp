# Hands together

<span class="tag tag-piano">Piano</span>

After this lesson you can play a right-hand melody over a held left-hand note, and take simple two-hand pieces from the grand staff at 60 bpm after practising each hand alone.

Before this: [Left hand in C position](02-left-hand-c-position.md), [The bass clef and the grand staff](01-bass-clef-and-grand-staff.md). You need: your piano and a metronome.

## The idea

### Sound first: the hands take turns

Playing two hands at once is a new skill, because each hand has to do something different while you read two staves. Start with a version that is hard to get wrong: the hands take turns. Listen to this, and play it with the right hand in the first bar, the left hand in the second, and so on. Only one hand plays at a time, so you can practise changing your attention from the treble staff to the bass staff.

```abc
X:1
M:4/4
L:1/4
Q:1/4=60
K:C
%%score {RH LH}
V:RH clef=treble
V:LH clef=bass
[V:RH] !1!C D E C | z4 | E F !5!G2 | z4 |]
[V:LH] z4 | !5!C, D, E, C, | z4 | !1!G,2 C,2 |]
```

The right hand plays bars 1 and 3, and the left hand answers in bars 2 and 4. The rests in each hand are counted like any other bar: four beats of silence in 4/4.

### Reading two staves

In a grand staff, notes that line up vertically are played at the same moment. Read straight down from one staff to the other to see what happens on each beat. A note in the treble staff and a note in the bass staff that stand above each other sound together.

Your eyes move between the two staves, so read in this order. Look at the clef of each staff. Look at both time signatures (they are the same). Read the rhythm of the hand that has the harder rhythm. Then read the other.

### Hands separately, then together

Every two-hand piece in this book is learned in the same order.

1. Play the right hand alone, two bars at a time, until it is smooth at 60 bpm.
2. Play the left hand alone the same way.
3. Play two bars with both hands at half speed (about 40 bpm). Play very slowly. Slow practice is the way to make the hands independent.
4. Add the next two bars. When all the bars work, speed up by 5 bpm at a time.
5. Count three clean in a row before you raise the tempo.

Common mistake at step 3: the left hand copies the right hand's rhythm. When this happens, stop and clap the rhythms, one hand on each knee. Then go back to the keys.

### A held left-hand note

The easiest two-hand texture is a melody in the right hand over one long left-hand note. Press the left-hand note, keep your finger on the key, and play the melody on top. The left hand has almost no work to do, so your attention can go to the right hand. Mary Had a Little Lamb (traditional) is below, with a whole note C3 in the left hand for two bars and then G3 in bar 3.

```abc
X:1
T:Mary Had a Little Lamb
C:Traditional
M:4/4
L:1/4
Q:1/4=60
K:C
%%score {RH LH}
V:RH clef=treble
V:LH clef=bass
[V:RH] !3!E D C D | E E E2 | D D D2 | E G G2 |]
[V:LH] !5!C,4 | C,4 | !1!G,4 | C,4 |]
```

The left-hand notes are whole notes, so they last the whole bar. Hold each key down for four beats while the right hand plays four notes. In bar 3, the left hand moves from finger 5 to finger 1 at the barline; look at the bass staff and move your hand before the bar starts.

### A short piece with two moving hands

Here is an original piece. The right hand plays a quarter-note melody, and the left hand plays two half notes in every bar, so both hands move but the left hand moves half as often.

```abc
X:1
T:Two Lanterns
C:Original, for this book
M:4/4
L:1/4
Q:1/4=60
K:C
%%score {RH LH}
V:RH clef=treble
V:LH clef=bass
[V:RH] !3!E G G E | F E D2 | E G G E | D D C2 |]
[V:LH] !5!C,2 C,2 | !1!G,2 G,2 | C,2 C,2 | G,2 C,2 |]
```

The left hand sounds on beats 1 and 3, and the right hand sounds on every beat. When the two hands start a bar together, you hear one clear beat 1.

## Try it

Exercise 1. In bar 4 of Two Lanterns, how many notes do the two hands play together on beat 1?

<details class="solution" markdown>
<summary>Answer</summary>

Two: D4 in the right hand and G3 in the left hand.

</details>

Exercise 2. In the turn-taking example, how many beats of rest does the left hand count before it plays?

<details class="solution" markdown>
<summary>Answer</summary>

Four beats, a whole bar, in bar 1.

</details>

Exercise 3. In Mary Had a Little Lamb, which left-hand finger plays in bar 3?

<details class="solution" markdown>

### Practice routine (30 minutes)

| Minutes | What to do | Tempo | Listen for |
|---|---|---|---|
| 5 | Warm-up: five-finger pattern C D E F G and back, right hand, then left hand | 60 bpm | Even volume on every finger |
| 15 | New skill: the turn-taking example, then Mary Had a Little Lamb with the steps above. Add Two Lanterns on day 3: hands separately, then two bars together | 50 bpm, then 40 for both hands | The left-hand note held for the full bar |
| 5 | Reading: read the first two bars of Two Lanterns aloud as "right hand E, left hand C" for each beat | Untimed | Reading across the two staves |
| 5 | Guitar: Em chord, four down strums per bar for 4 bars | 60 bpm | Every string ringing |

Repeat this routine on at least three days.

</div>

- [ ] You can play the turn-taking example at 60 bpm, three times in a row with no stops.
- [ ] You can play Mary Had a Little Lamb hands together at 60 bpm, three times in a row with the left-hand notes held the full bar.
- [ ] You can play each hand of Two Lanterns alone at 60 bpm, then hands together at 40 bpm with no stops.
- [ ] You can say which note in the left hand sounds on beat 3 of each bar of Two Lanterns.

## Common mistakes

Skipping the hands-separate step. Hands together is slower to learn when each hand is still uncertain.

Letting the held left-hand key come up early. Keep the finger on the key until the bar is over.

Looking down at the left hand. Your left hand knows C position now. Keep your eyes on the page.

## Key terms

- **Hands together**: playing the right-hand and left-hand parts at the same time.
- **Hands separately**: practising one hand alone before combining them.
- **Held note**: a note whose key stays down for its full length.
