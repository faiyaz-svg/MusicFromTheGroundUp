# Ties, dots and eighth notes

<span class="tag tag-theory">Theory</span>

After this lesson you can count and play tied notes, dotted half notes, dotted quarter notes and eighth notes, and say how many beats each one lasts.

Before this: [Beat, tempo and note values](../01-first-sounds/07-beat-tempo-and-note-values.md), [Bars, time signatures and rests](../01-first-sounds/08-bars-time-signatures-and-rests.md), [First melodies on piano](../01-first-sounds/10-first-melodies-on-piano.md). You need: your piano and a metronome.

## The idea

So far every note has been 1, 2 or 4 beats. Real music also needs 3 beats, 1.5 beats and half a beat. Three tools make them: the tie, the dot and the eighth note.

### The tie

A **tie** is a curved line joining two notes of the same pitch. Play the first note and hold it through the second; do not play the second. The tied notes sound as one note whose length is the two lengths added together.

A tie can cross a bar line, which a single note cannot. In this melody the E in bar 1 is a half note tied to the half note E at the start of bar 2, so it sounds for 4 beats, and you press it once.

```abc
X:1
M:4/4
L:1/4
Q:1/4=60
K:C
!3!E E E2- | E2 D2 | C D E D | C4 |]
```

Look for the curved line between the two E notes, and notice that the second one has no new attack when you listen.

### The dot

A **dot** written after a note makes it longer by half of its own value. A half note is 2 beats, half of that is 1, so a dotted half note lasts 3 beats. A quarter note is 1 beat, half of that is half a beat, so a dotted quarter note lasts 1.5 beats.

| Note | Value | Dot adds | Total |
|---|---|---|---|
| Dotted half note | 2 beats | 1 beat | 3 beats |
| Dotted quarter note | 1 beat | 0.5 beat | 1.5 beats |

A dotted half note fills a bar of 3/4 by itself. In 4/4 it takes three beats and is followed by one more beat, so count "1 2 3 4" and play on "1" and "4".

```abc
X:1
M:4/4
L:1/4
Q:1/4=60
K:C
!1!C3 D | E3 C | D3 E | C4 |]
```

### Eighth notes

An **eighth note** lasts half a beat, so two of them fit in one beat. It looks like a quarter note with a flag on the stem. When two or more eighth notes are next to each other, a thick line called a **beam** joins their stems and replaces the flags. Beamed eighth notes are usually grouped one beat at a time.

Count eighth notes by saying the beat number and "&" between the beats: "1 & 2 & 3 & 4 &". The numbers are on the beats, and the "&" is halfway between them. Two eighth notes on beat 1 are played on "1" and "&".

An **eighth rest** is half a beat of silence. It looks like a small slanted stroke with a flag, and you count it like a note you don't play.

Count the first example aloud, then play it with the right hand. Bar 1 has an eighth-note pair on beats 1 and 2. In bar 3 each beat has an eighth note and an eighth rest, so you play on the number and rest on the "&".

```abc
X:1
M:4/4
L:1/4
Q:1/4=60
K:C
!1!C/D/ E/F/ G2 | G/F/ E/D/ C2 | E/z/ E/z/ G2 | C4 |]
```

### Dotted quarter and eighth

A dotted quarter note is often followed by an eighth note. Together they fill 2 beats: 1.5 plus 0.5. Count "1 & 2 &". The dotted quarter is played on "1" and held through the "&" and the "2". The eighth note is played on the last "&", right before beat 3.

You already know a tune with this rhythm. Ode to Joy (Beethoven) in stage 1 had a simplified rhythm in bars 4 and 8. Here is the theme with its real rhythm. Listen for the long-short pair in bars 4 and 8, where the melody leans on the first note and then steps quickly to the next one.

```abc
X:1
T:Ode to Joy (theme)
C:Ludwig van Beethoven
M:4/4
L:1/4
Q:1/4=60
K:C
!3!E E F G | G F E D | !1!C C D E | E3/ D/ D2 |
E E F G | G F E D | C C D E | D3/ C/ C2 |]
```

Set the metronome to 60 bpm and clap bar 4 first. The claps fall on "1", on the last "&" of beat 2, and on beat 3.

## Try it

Exercise 1. How many beats does each last: a dotted half note, a dotted quarter note, and an eighth note?

<details class="solution" markdown>
<summary>Answer</summary>

3 beats, 1.5 beats and 0.5 beat.

</details>

Exercise 2. A half note tied to a quarter note of the same pitch lasts how many beats?

<details class="solution" markdown>
<summary>Answer</summary>

3 beats (2 + 1). It sounds like a dotted half note.

</details>

Exercise 3. Count a bar of 4/4 that has a dotted quarter, an eighth, and a half note. On which counts are notes played?

<details class="solution" markdown>
<summary>Answer</summary>

Count "1 & 2 & 3 & 4 &". Notes are played on "1", on the "&" after 2, and on "3". The half note is held through "4".

</details>

### Practice routine (30 minutes)

| Minutes | What to do | Tempo | Listen for |
|---|---|---|---|
| 5 | Warm-up: five-finger pattern C D E F G and back, each hand | 60 bpm | Even volume on every finger |
| 15 | New skill: clap and count each example aloud ("1 & 2 &"), then play it with the right hand. Finish with the Ode to Joy rhythm in bars 4 and 8 | 50 bpm, then 60 | Eighth notes exactly twice as fast as the beat |
| 5 | Reading: clap the rhythm of the eighth-note example from the page, then say it as "1 & 2 &" | 60 bpm | The same pulse in every bar |
| 5 | Guitar: Em to E and back, one strum per bar | 60 bpm | Every string ringing |

Repeat this routine on at least three days.

## Common mistakes

Playing the second note of a tie. Hold the first note and don't play the second.

Rushing the eighth note after a dotted quarter. It is short, but it still waits for the last "&". Count aloud.

Forgetting that the dot is half of the note's own value. A dotted half is 3 beats and a dotted quarter is 1.5 beats; other dotted notes come in later stages.

## Key terms

- **Tie**: a curved line joining two notes of the same pitch, played as one longer note.
- **Dot**: a dot after a note that adds half of the note's value.
- **Dotted half note**: 3 beats.
- **Dotted quarter note**: 1.5 beats.
- **Eighth note**: half a beat; two fit in one beat.
- **Beam**: the thick line joining the stems of two or more eighth notes.
- **Eighth rest**: half a beat of silence.
