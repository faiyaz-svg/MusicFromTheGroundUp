# Legato, staccato and dynamics

<span class="tag tag-piano">Piano</span>

After this lesson you can play a line legato or staccato, read slurs, staccato dots and the dynamics p, mf and f, and play louder and softer on purpose.

Before this: [Meet your piano](../01-first-sounds/02-meet-your-piano.md), [Hands together](03-hands-together.md), [Ties, dots and eighth notes](05-ties-dots-and-eighth-notes.md). You need: your piano and a metronome.

## The idea

### Sound first: smooth and short

Play the notes C D E F G with the right hand twice. The first time, hold each key down until the next key is already pressed, so the notes run into each other without a gap. The second time, release each key as soon as you play it, so there is a short silence after every note. The notes and the rhythm are the same, but the first version sounds smooth and the second sounds crisp.

### Legato and the slur

**Legato** (Italian for "tied together") means smooth and connected, with no gap between notes. In notation it is shown by a **slur**, a curved line over or under a group of different notes. Play the first note of a slur, and for the others change smoothly from one finger to the next so each note sounds until the next one begins.

A slur joins different pitches. A tie, from the last lesson, joins two notes of the same pitch. Both are curved lines, so check the pitches under the curve.

To play legato on the piano:

1. Press the first key and keep your finger on it.
2. Play the next key, and release the first key only when the second is down.
3. Keep the wrist loose, and let the weight of the arm pass from finger to finger.

Common mistake at step 2: lifting the old key too early, which leaves a gap. Slow down until every note joins the next one.

### Staccato

**Staccato** (Italian for "detached") means short and separate. It is written as a dot above or below the note head, on the side away from the stem. A staccato note sounds for about half of its written length, and the rest is silence. A staccato quarter note at 60 bpm is a short sound on the beat followed by a gap, and the beat still lasts a full second.

To play staccato, let the finger bounce off the key: drop the weight into the key and release it at once. Keep the wrist loose and don't poke with the finger alone.

### Dynamics

**Dynamics** are the loudness of the music. They are written as letters under the staff.

| Sign | Italian | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| p | piano | soft |
| mf | mezzo forte | medium loud |
| f | forte | loud |

Here "piano" means soft. The instrument's name is a short form of the older word pianoforte, "soft-loud", because the instrument could play both. A dynamic sign applies from where it is written until the next one.

A **crescendo** is a gradual increase in volume, drawn as a hairpin that opens to the right. A **diminuendo** is a gradual decrease, drawn as a hairpin that closes.

On your digital piano, loudness comes from touch sensitivity (stage 1, lesson 2): play softer by pressing the keys with less speed, and louder by pressing them with more arm weight. Don't use the volume knob to make dynamics; set it once and leave it.

### Hear and see

Listen to a line with legato slurs. The first bar is p, and a slur covers each group of notes.

```abc
X:1
M:4/4
L:1/4
Q:1/4=60
K:C
!p!(!3!E F G F) | (E D C2) | !mf!(D E F E) | (D C D2) |]
```

Now staccato notes, with a rest in the last bar. Listen for the gap after each note.

```abc
X:1
M:4/4
L:1/4
Q:1/4=60
K:C
!mf!.G .G .G .G | .E .F .G .E | .D .D .D .D | C2 z2 |]
```

A crescendo and a diminuendo. The hairpin opens over bar 1 as the line goes up and closes over bar 2 as it comes down.

```abc
X:1
M:4/4
L:1/4
Q:1/4=60
K:C
!p!!crescendo(!C D E !crescendo)!F | !f!G F !diminuendo(!E !diminuendo)!D | C4 |]
```

### An original piece

"Rain and Sun" uses everything in this lesson. Bars 1 to 4 are soft and legato, bars 5 and 6 are staccato and get louder, and bars 7 and 8 come back down.

```abc
X:1
T:Rain and Sun
C:Original, for this book
M:4/4
L:1/4
Q:1/4=60
K:C
!p!(!3!E F G F) | (E D C2) | !mf!(D E F E) | (D C D2) |
!crescendo(!.G .G .G .G | .E .E .E !crescendo)!.E | !f!G F E D | !diminuendo(!C2 !diminuendo)!C2 |]
```

## Try it

Exercise 1. What is the difference between a tie and a slur?

<details class="solution" markdown>
<summary>Answer</summary>

A tie joins two notes of the same pitch into one long note. A slur covers different pitches and means play them legato.

</details>

Exercise 2. Put these in order from softest to loudest: f, p, mf.

<details class="solution" markdown>
<summary>Answer</summary>

p, mf, f.

</details>

Exercise 3. What does a hairpin that closes to the right tell you to do?

<details class="solution" markdown>
<summary>Answer</summary>

Get gradually softer (diminuendo).

</details>

### Practice routine (30 minutes)

| Minutes | What to do | Tempo | Listen for |
|---|---|---|---|
| 5 | Warm-up: C D E F G and back, each hand, once legato and once staccato | 60 bpm | Smooth versus detached, same volume |
| 15 | New skill: the legato line, the staccato line and the crescendo line, then Rain and Sun in four-bar halves | 50 bpm, then 60 | A clear difference between p, mf and f |
| 5 | Reading: read Rain and Sun aloud, naming the dynamic and articulation of each bar before you play | Untimed | Seeing the signs before the notes |
| 5 | Guitar: Am to E and D to A, one strum per bar | 60 bpm | Every string ringing |

Repeat this routine on at least three days.

## Common mistakes

Playing softly by playing hesitantly. Soft playing needs the same steady rhythm and an even tone; use less speed in the key, not a slower beat.

Making staccato notes too short. Aim for about half the written length, and keep the count steady in the silence.

Using the volume knob for dynamics. The knob changes everything at once. The change should come from your touch.

## Key terms

- **Legato**: smooth and connected, with no gap between notes.
- **Slur**: a curved line over different notes meaning play them legato.
- **Staccato**: short and detached, marked by a dot.
- **Dynamics**: the loudness of the music.
- **p** (piano): soft. **mf** (mezzo forte): medium loud. **f** (forte): loud.
- **Crescendo**: gradually louder, drawn as an opening hairpin.
- **Diminuendo**: gradually softer, drawn as a closing hairpin.
