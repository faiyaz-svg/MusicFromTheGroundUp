# Sight-reading basics

<span class="tag tag-reading">Reading</span>

After this lesson you can scan a short piece before you play it, keep a steady beat through a first reading without stopping for mistakes, and read easy lines in both clefs at sight.

Before this: [The bass clef and the grand staff](01-bass-clef-and-grand-staff.md), [Hands together](03-hands-together.md), [Ties, dots and eighth notes](05-ties-dots-and-eighth-notes.md). You need: your piano and a metronome.

## The idea

### What sight reading is

**Sight reading** is playing music you have never seen before, at the first attempt, in time. It is a different skill from learning a piece. When you learn a piece you stop, fix and repeat. When you sight-read you play on, with the beat going, and accept the mistakes.

Your goals include playing pieces from sheet music, and sight reading is the skill underneath that. The aim is to take in a line of notation in groups and shapes instead of naming every note. The skill grows from short, daily reading of music that is easy for you.

### Scan before you play

Spend 10 to 20 seconds with the music before you touch the keys. Look at these things, in order.

1. The clef or clefs. Treble, bass, or both.
2. The time signature. Count the beats in a bar.
3. The highest and lowest notes. They show where each hand goes and whether it needs to move from its position.
4. The rhythm. Find the long notes, the rests, the ties, the dots and any eighth notes, and tap them in your head.
5. Anything unusual: a dynamic, a slur, a repeat sign. All the music so far has no key signature; in stage 3 a key signature joins the list.

Then choose a tempo. Set the metronome slower than feels necessary. If the line is in 4/4 and has eighth notes, 40 to 50 bpm is a good start.

### Keep the beat going

The rule of sight reading is to keep the beat. A wrong note passes in a moment, but a stopped beat breaks the whole line.

1. Count aloud or in your head, one bar of the click first: "1 2 3 4".
2. Play through to the end without stopping.
3. If you play a wrong note, let it go and play the next note on time. If you lose your place, rejoin at the next bar line on the next downbeat, even if you have to leave out notes.
4. Play once through. Then, if you like, scan again and play a second time.

Common mistake at step 3: stopping to correct, which wipes out the beat. Missing a note costs you one note, but stopping costs you the bar.

### Look ahead

The eyes should be one beat or one bar ahead of the hands. Beginners look at the note they are playing and then find the next one. A reader looks at the next note while the current one is sounding.

Two habits build this. Keep your eyes on the page and never look down at the keys: your hand knows the position from the scan. And read in groups: see "up by step" and "same note twice" as shapes instead of naming each note.

### Read easy music every day

Read music that is easier than the pieces you are learning, so that you can keep the beat going. Five minutes every day builds the habit better than an hour once a week. The reading slot in each practice routine in this book is for this.

## Hear and see: four lines to read

Scan each line first, then play it once through at 50 bpm with the metronome on, without stopping. The first line is the right hand in C position. Listen for the shape: it goes up in steps, then back down.

```abc
X:1
M:4/4
L:1/4
Q:1/4=50
K:C
!1!C D E2 | F E D2 | E G F D | C4 |]
```

The second line is the left hand in 3/4. Count "1 2 3" and notice the dotted half note in bar 3.

```abc
X:1
M:3/4
L:1/4
Q:1/4=50
K:C clef=bass
!3!E, D, C, | D, E, F, | G,3 | G, F, E, | D,2 C, | C,3 |]
```

The third line puts the hands together. The left hand holds a whole note while the right hand plays four.

```abc
X:1
M:4/4
L:1/4
Q:1/4=50
K:C
%%score {RH LH}
V:RH clef=treble
V:LH clef=bass
[V:RH] !3!E E F G | G F E D | C D E2 | D4 |]
[V:LH] !5!C,4 | G,4 | C,2 E,2 | G,4 |]
```

The fourth line has eighth notes. Count "1 & 2 &" in bars 1 and 2, and keep it steady into the half note.

```abc
X:1
M:4/4
L:1/4
Q:1/4=50
K:C
!1!C/D/ E/F/ G2 | G/F/ E/D/ C2 | E G/F/ E D | C4 |]
```

### Practice routine (30 minutes)

| Minutes | What to do | Tempo | Listen for |
|---|---|---|---|
| 5 | Warm-up: C D E F G and back, each hand, then both hands an octave apart | 60 bpm | Even volume on every finger |
| 15 | New skill: scan and sight-read the four lines, one at a time, once through each, then read each one again. Then read lines from the [Reading drill](../practice/reading-drill.md) | 50 bpm | The beat going through every wrong note |
| 5 | Reading: name the highest and lowest note and the longest rest of each line, in under 20 seconds | Untimed | A scan that finds the position |
| 5 | Guitar: Em to A with four strums per bar, or rest on beat 4 for the change | 60 bpm | Steady beat across the change |

Repeat this routine on at least three days. Use new lines when you can; the Reading drill gives a fresh line every time.

</div>

### Move on when

- [ ] You can scan a new line and say its clef, time signature, highest and lowest notes and rhythm in under 20 seconds.
- [ ] You can sight-read the first line at 50 bpm without stopping, with fewer than three wrong notes.
- [ ] You can sight-read the third line at 50 bpm without stopping.
- [ ] You can keep the beat through a wrong note without stopping, in any of the four lines.

## Common mistakes

Playing too fast. Pick a tempo at which you can read everything, and play at that tempo even if it feels slow.

Looking at the hands. Your eyes belong on the page. Learn the position by feel.

Stopping to fix mistakes. This turns a reading exercise into a practice exercise. Fix the line afterwards, in a separate pass.

## Key terms

- **Sight reading**: playing music you have not seen before, in time, without stopping.
- **Scan**: looking over a piece before playing to find the clef, time signature, range and rhythms.
- **Look ahead**: reading the notes that come next while the current notes sound.
