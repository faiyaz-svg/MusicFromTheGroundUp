# First chord pieces

<span class="tag tag-guitar">Guitar</span>

After this lesson you can read chord symbols above a melody line and strum three short pieces with two or three chords, four down strokes per bar, along with the melody.

Before this: [Strumming with down strokes](08-strumming-down-strokes.md), [Sight-reading basics](09-sight-reading-basics.md), [One-minute chord changes](11-one-minute-changes.md). You need: your guitar, a pick, a tuner and a metronome.

## The idea

### Chord symbols above a melody

A piece for chords is often written as a melody on a staff with **chord symbols** above it. The symbol is the chord name: A means A major, Am means A minor, and so on. A chord symbol is played from the point where it is written until the next symbol. Most of the time that is one symbol per bar, and a bar with no symbol uses the chord before it.

The melody is written on the staff for whoever plays it (a singer, a piano or another guitar). You strum the chords while the melody sounds. In this lesson the play bar plays the melody, and you strum the chords along with it. Later you can play the melody on the piano in G position (lesson 10) while someone else, or a recording of yourself, plays the chords.

### How to learn a chord piece

Follow these steps for each piece.

1. Scan the chord symbols. Name the distinct chords (two or three) and find where each one changes.
2. Check the time signature for the number of strums per bar. In 4/4, strum four down strokes. In 3/4, strum three.
3. Say the chords aloud in time, one bar each, with the metronome: "Em, Em, Am, Am".
4. Play the first two bars with the metronome at 50 bpm. Use the rest on beat 4 from lesson 8 if you need time for a change.
5. Add two bars at a time. When the whole piece works, strum it with the play bar.
6. Count three clean in a row at 60 bpm before you move on.

Common mistake at step 4: stopping the arm at the chord change. The arm keeps swinging; a missing strum is better than a stopped beat.

## Hear and see

### A piece with A, D and E

Harbour Lights is an original piece in 4/4 with three chords: A, D and E. Strum four down strokes in each bar. In bar 6 the chord changes to E, and in bar 7 it returns to A. Listen for the melody staying on A and B, which fit all three chords.

```abc
X:1
T:Harbour Lights
C:Original, for this book
M:4/4
L:1/4
Q:1/4=60
K:C
"A"A A B A | B2 A2 | "D"d d A A | A G A2 |
"A"A A B A | "E"B B A B | "A"A B A2 | A4 |]
```

The chords in order: A, A, D, D, A, E, A, A. The A chord starts its stroke on string 5, and D starts on string 4. This chord progression sounds settled in bar 8 because the music comes back to the chord it began on, A.

### A piece in 3/4 with Em, Am and D

Night Market is an original piece in 3/4 with three chords: Em, Am and D. Strum three down strokes in each bar. Count "1 2 3" and keep the arm swinging while you change in bars 3 and 5.

```abc
X:1
T:Night Market
C:Original, for this book
M:3/4
L:1/4
Q:1/4=60
K:C
"Em"G A B | B2 G | "Am"A c B | A3 |
"D"d B A | A2 G | "Em"G A B | G3 |]
```

The chords in order: Em, Em, Am, Am, D, D, Em, Em. Because each chord lasts two bars, you have 6 beats to prepare every change.

### A traditional tune with Em and D

Au clair de la lune is a traditional French tune. The melody is the one you played in G position, with chords added by this book: Em and D. Strum four down strokes per bar. The only change is between Em and D, so this is the easiest piece to start with.

```abc
X:1
T:Au clair de la lune
C:Traditional
M:4/4
L:1/4
Q:1/4=60
K:C
"Em"G G G A | "D"B2 A2 | "Em"G B A A | G4 |
"Em"G G G A | "D"B2 A2 | "Em"G B A A | G4 |]
```

The chords in order: Em, D, Em, Em, Em, D, Em, Em. Notice that the melody ends on G, which is a note of the Em chord, so the last chord fits.

## Try it

Exercise 1. In Harbour Lights, how many different chords are there, and in which bar does each change happen?

<details class="solution" markdown>
<summary>Answer</summary>

Three chords (A, D and E). The chord changes at bar 3 (A to D), bar 5 (D to A), bar 6 (A to E) and bar 7 (E to A).

</details>

Exercise 2. How many strums do you play in all of Night Market?

<details class="solution" markdown>
<summary>Answer</summary>

24: eight bars with three strums each.

</details>

Exercise 3. In Au clair de la lune, which bar should you start preparing the change to D in, and on which beat?

<details class="solution" markdown>
<summary>Answer</summary>

The change to D happens at bar 2, so start moving on beat 4 of bar 1.

</details>

| Minutes | What to do | Tempo | Listen for |
|---|---|---|---|
| 5 | Warm-up: one-minute changes on one pair from the last lesson, one round | Fastest clean speed | Clean chords |

## Common mistakes

Reading the melody notes as chords. The notes on the staff are for the melody player; you read the symbols above them.

Rushing the strums to catch up after a change. Hold the beat; play the strum you missed as a rest.

Forgetting the starting string: A starts on string 5, D starts on string 4, and Em and E use all six.

## Key terms

- **Chord symbol**: the name of a chord written above a bar, such as Em or A.
- **Chord piece**: a piece in which you strum the chords written above a melody.
