## The idea

### The piece

The Minuet in G (catalogue number BWV Anh. 114) is a **minuet**, a dance. It is in G major, in 3/4 time, and is written for both hands. It comes from the Anna Magdalena Bach Notebook of 1725, a notebook of keyboard pieces that belonged to J. S. Bach's second wife. It was long thought to be by J. S. Bach, and is now credited to Christian Petzold.

### 3/4 as a dance

A minuet is built for dancing in steps, three beats to a bar. Count 1 2 3 and lean on beat 1: it is the strong beat, and the bar feels like one step with a lift on 2 and 3. Play 3/4 with the same weight on beat 1 each time, and keep beats 2 and 3 lighter.

Listen to the count first. The right hand plays a G major pattern, and the left hand plays one low note on each beat 1.

```abc
X:1
M:3/4
L:1/4
Q:1/4=80
K:G
%%score {RH LH}
V:RH clef=treble
V:LH clef=bass
[V:RH] !mf!G A B | c2 B | A B A | G3 |]
[V:LH] !p!G,3 | G,3 | D,3 | G,3 |]
```

Say "ONE two three" as you listen, and tap the strong beat. A dance step is steady, so keep the tempo level; start at 60 bpm and move toward 80 over several days as it becomes secure.

### Learning a piece in sections

A **phrase** is a musical sentence: a short stretch of music that rises and falls and then comes to a rest, like a spoken sentence ending with a breath. Phrases are often 2 or 4 bars long. A piece is easier to learn when you cut it into phrases and learn one at a time. Follow these steps.

1. Listen to the whole piece twice with the score in front of you.
2. Scan the page before playing. Find the key signature (one sharp, so every F is F#), the time signature (3/4), the highest and lowest notes in each hand, and any bar where the hand has to move. Mark the hard bars with a pencil.
3. Cut the piece into sections of 2 to 4 bars. Look for the ends of phrases, a longer note or a change in the pattern, and use those as your cut points. Draw a short line on the score at each cut.
4. Learn section 1 with the right hand alone, slowly, with fingering written in. Play it three times in a row with no stops.
5. Learn section 1 with the left hand alone the same way.
6. Put the hands together at a slow tempo, 50 bpm or lower. Play section 1 three clean in a row.
7. Do the same for section 2. Then play sections 1 and 2 as one piece, and continue like this to the end.

Common mistake at step 6: speeding up when the hands go together. If the hands fall out of time, slow down by 10 bpm and start again.

Common mistake at step 7: always starting from the beginning. The first bars become polished and the last bars stay rough. Sometimes start at the last section and work backwards.

### Exercises in the same style

The music below is not the Minuet. It is written for this book, in the same style, to practise the steps above. Learn it as if it were section 1 of a piece.

Right hand alone. The fingers 1 to 5 sit on G, A, B, C and D:

```abc
X:1
M:3/4
L:1/4
Q:1/4=60
K:G
!1!G2 !2!A | !3!B2 !4!c | !5!d2 !3!B | !1!G3 |]
```

Left hand alone. Each bar is one held note, a dotted half note, which lasts the three beats of the bar:

```abc
X:1
M:3/4
L:1/4
Q:1/4=60
K:G clef=bass
G,3 | G,3 | D,3 | G,3 |]
```

Both hands together. The left hand holds a note under the right-hand tune:

```abc
X:1
M:3/4
L:1/4
Q:1/4=60
K:G
%%score {RH LH}
V:RH clef=treble
V:LH clef=bass
[V:RH] !1!G2 !2!A | !3!B2 !4!c | !5!d2 !3!B | !1!G3 |]
[V:LH] G,3 | G,3 | D,3 | G,3 |]
```

Once this is steady, write a second 4-bar section of your own in G major, keeping each bar at three beats, and learn it the same way.

If a hand, wrist or arm aches, stop and rest. If it keeps happening, see a teacher or a doctor.

## Try it

Exercise 1. How many beats are in one bar of the Minuet, and which beat is the strongest?

<details class="solution" markdown>
<summary>Answer</summary>

Three beats (3/4). Beat 1 is the strongest.

</details>

Exercise 2. The key signature of the Minuet has one sharp. Which note is it, and which major key is it?

<details class="solution" markdown>
<summary>Answer</summary>

F#, and the key is G major. G major has one sharp.

</details>

Exercise 3. You play section 1 with both hands at 60 bpm and make a mistake in the third bar. What do you do?

<details class="solution" markdown>
<summary>Answer</summary>

Slow down by 10 bpm or go back to hands separately for that bar, then build up again until you play three clean in a row.

</details>

### Practice routine (30 minutes)

| Minutes | What to do | Tempo | Listen for |
|---|---|---|---|
| 5 | Warm-up: G major scale, one octave, each hand alone | 60 bpm | Even notes |
| 15 | New skill: the Minuet, one or two sections at a time as the plan above says. Right hand alone, left hand alone, then together | 50 bpm, then 60 | Beat 1 a little stronger than 2 and 3 |
| 5 | Reading: the exercise pieces above, hands together | 60 bpm | Keeping on after a wrong note |
| 5 | Guitar: C, G and Dm, one strum per bar | 60 bpm | Every sounding string ringing |

Repeat this routine on at least five days. A whole piece takes more than three days.

</div>

### Move on when

- [ ] You can play each section of the Minuet with both hands at 60 bpm, three times in a row with no stops.
- [ ] You can play the whole Minuet hands together at 60 bpm without stopping, even if it has a few small slips.
- [ ] You can count 1 2 3 aloud while you play and keep beat 1 stronger than beats 2 and 3.
- [ ] You can record yourself playing the Minuet, and name the one bar you want to improve next.

</div>

## Common mistakes

Learning the right hand fast and the left hand never. Give both hands the same number of repeats.

Starting every practice session at the beginning. Vary where you start.

Playing the three beats evenly. A minuet has a lean on beat 1.

## Key terms

- **Minuet**: a dance in 3/4 time.
- **Phrase**: a musical sentence, often 2 or 4 bars long.
- **Section**: a few bars of a piece learned as one unit.
- **Hands separately**: practising each hand alone before playing them together.
