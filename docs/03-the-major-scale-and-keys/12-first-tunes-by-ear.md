# First tunes by ear

<span class="tag tag-ear">Ear</span>

After this lesson you can find the first note of a tune you know, tell whether the next note moves by step or by leap, and work out a simple traditional tune on the piano without sheet music.

Before this: [Singing the scale by number](04-singing-the-scale-by-number.md), [The major scale](02-the-major-scale.md), [The C major scale and thumb-under](03-c-major-scale-and-thumb-under.md). You need: your piano and your voice.

## The idea

### Sound first: you already know the tune

Hum "Twinkle, Twinkle, Little Star" (traditional). You know every note, even if you cannot name one. Playing a tune by ear means finding those notes on an instrument by listening, with no page to read. It uses the same skills as singing the scale by number: you match pitch, and you hear where each note sits in the scale.

### Step and leap

Between two notes, the melody either moves by step or by leap. A **step** moves to the next note of the scale, up or down: 1 to 2, or 5 to 4. A **leap** skips over at least one scale note: 1 to 3, or 1 to 5. The first notes of Twinkle (1 1 5 5) are a leap, then the 5 to 6 is a step. A tune that moves mostly by step is easy to follow, and a leap is easy to hear because it is big.

Listen to the opening of the tune. The play bar shows it in C, with 1 as C.

### A method

Follow these steps for any tune.

1. Hum the tune and find where it comes home. The last note of a simple tune is usually 1, the tonic.
2. Play the C major scale and find which note matches that last note. If you hum the tune and your voice settles on C, the key is C.
3. Find the first note. Hum it, then try the white keys near it until one matches. Call out the number (1, 3, 5), not the name.
4. Work one phrase at a time. After each note, decide if the next one is the same, a step up, a step down or a leap. Try it on the piano, listen, and correct.
5. Write the numbers down. When the whole tune is on the page as numbers, play it again from the numbers.

Common mistake at step 4: trying to find a whole phrase at once. Find one note, check it, then add the next.

### Numbers carry to any key

A tune written as numbers is the same in every key. Here is Mary Had a Little Lamb (traditional) as numbers: 3 2 1 2 | 3 3 3 | 2 2 2 | 3 5 5. In C, 1 is C, so the tune begins on E. In G, 1 is G, so it begins on B. Compare the two. Listen: they are the same tune, with the whole thing moved.

```abc
X:1
M:4/4
L:1/4
Q:1/4=70
K:C
E D C D | E E E2 | D D D2 | E G G2 |]
```

```abc
X:1
M:4/4
L:1/4
Q:1/4=70
K:G
B A G A | B B B2 | A A A2 | B d d2 |]
```

If you know the numbers, you can play a tune in any key you know the scale of.

### Your tunes to work out

Choose one and find it by ear first. Do not look at the answers until you have tried.

- Twinkle, Twinkle: 1 1 5 5 6 6 5 | 4 4 3 3 2 2 1.
- Frere Jacques (traditional): 1 2 3 1 | 1 2 3 1 | 3 4 5 | 3 4 5.
- Au Clair de la Lune (traditional): 1 1 1 2 3 2 | 1 3 2 2 1.
- Ode to Joy: 3 3 4 5 | 5 4 3 2 | 1 1 2 3 | 3 2 2.

Try each tune once by ear before you read its numbers, so the numbers are a check and not a crutch.

## Try it

Exercise 1. In Twinkle, is the move from the first note to the third note a step or a leap? What about the third to the fifth note?

<details class="solution" markdown>
<summary>Answer</summary>

The notes are 1 1 5 5 6. First note to third (1 to 5) is a leap. Third to fifth (5 to 6) is a step.

</details>

Exercise 2. Write the first three notes of Mary Had a Little Lamb (numbers 3 2 1) in the key of G.

<details class="solution" markdown>
<summary>Answer</summary>

B, A, G. In G major, 3 is B, 2 is A and 1 is G.

</details>

Exercise 3. Play Frere Jacques in C using the numbers above, then play it again in G.

<details class="solution" markdown>
<summary>Answer</summary>

In C: C D E C | C D E C | E F G | E F G. In G: G A B G | G A B G | B C D | B C D.

</details>

### Practice routine (30 minutes)

| Minutes | What to do | Tempo | Listen for |
|---|---|---|---|
| 5 | Warm-up: C and G major scales, right hand | 60 bpm | Even notes |
| 15 | By ear: pick one tune from the list. Hum it, find the last note, then find the first note, then work through one phrase at a time. Write down the numbers, then play from your numbers | Untimed, then 70 bpm | Hearing a wrong note before you check |
| 5 | Same tune again in G, from the numbers | 70 bpm | The same tune in a new key |
| 5 | Guitar: C, G and Em, one strum per bar. Sing the numbers of a tune over the chords | 60 bpm | Your voice matching the notes |

Repeat this routine on at least three days, with a new tune each day.

## Common mistakes

Looking for the first note by name. Look for its number: is it the tonic, or a leap above it?

Trying the whole tune at once. Work one phrase at a time.

Giving up on the key. If the tune does not sound right in C, hum the last note and try another tonic.

## Key terms

- **Step**: a move to the next note of the scale.
- **Leap**: a move that skips at least one scale note.
- **Playing by ear**: finding the notes of a tune by listening, without a score.
