# Eighth-note strumming

<span class="tag tag-guitar">Guitar</span>

After this lesson you can strum eight even strokes to the bar with a steady down-up motion, play the pattern D D U U D U on one chord and on a four-chord loop, and put an accent on beat 1.

Before this: [Strumming with down strokes](../02-two-hands-and-first-chords/08-strumming-down-strokes.md), [Ties, dots and eighth notes](../02-two-hands-and-first-chords/05-ties-dots-and-eighth-notes.md), [Chord diagrams and your first chord](../01-first-sounds/12-chord-diagrams-and-e-minor.md). You need: your guitar, a pick, a tuner and a metronome.

## The idea

### Sound first: down and up

Until now you strummed down once per beat. Eighth notes are twice as fast: two strokes to each beat. You count them "1 & 2 & 3 & 4 &", saying "and" on the second half of each beat. Play down on every number and up on every "&".

An **up stroke** moves the pick from the thin strings to the thick strings, string 1 toward string 6. It is lighter than a down stroke, and it usually sounds only the top three or four strings, so it does not need to reach the bass.

Listen to a bar of eight strokes on the Em chord: down, up, down, up, and so on. The letters D and U above the music mark each stroke. The up strokes use the top three strings only.

```abc
%%opts tab=guitar
X:1
M:4/4
L:1/8
Q:1/4=60
K:C clef=treble-8
"Em""^D"[E,B,EGBe] "^U"[GBe] "^D"[E,B,EGBe] "^U"[GBe] "^D"[E,B,EGBe] "^U"[GBe] "^D"[E,B,EGBe] "^U"[GBe] |
"^D"[E,B,EGBe] "^U"[GBe] "^D"[E,B,EGBe] "^U"[GBe] "^D"[E,B,EGBe] "^U"[GBe] "^D"[E,B,EGBe] "^U"[GBe] |]
```

### The constant motion

The most important idea is that your strumming hand never stops. It swings down on the numbers and up on the "&" at all times, like a pendulum. Whether a stroke makes a sound depends on whether the pick touches the strings on that swing. The hand keeps moving through the strokes you do not play, so the timing stays even.

Follow these steps to build the motion.

1. Hold the pick as in [Holding the guitar, tuning and the pick](../01-first-sounds/06-holding-tuning-and-the-pick.md) and rest your forearm on the guitar body.
2. Lay your fretting hand lightly across all six strings, without pressing, so the strings are muted.
3. Set the metronome to 60 bpm and strum down on each click and up halfway between clicks, counting "1 & 2 & 3 & 4 &" aloud. Swing from the wrist and the forearm, with a loose grip.
4. When the count is steady for two bars, play an open Em chord and do the same.

Common mistake at step 3: the hand tenses and the up strokes come out loud and late. Keep the up stroke light, and let the pick brush only the top strings.

### A first strumming pattern

A **strumming pattern** says which strokes of the bar you play. The pattern D D U U D U has six strokes in a bar of eight slots. The hand swings through all eight slots and plays on six of them. The other two, the "&" after beat 1 and beat 3, are swung but not played.

| Count | 1 | & | 2 | & | 3 | & | 4 | & |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Hand | down | up | down | up | down | up | down | up |
| Strum | D | - | D | U | - | U | D | U |

Strum on beat 1, beat 2, the "&" after 2, the "&" after 3, beat 4 and the "&" after 4. In the music, the first note is held for a full beat (beat 1 and the "&" after it), and a rest marks the missed stroke on beat 3.

```abc
%%opts tab=guitar
X:1
M:4/4
L:1/8
Q:1/4=60
K:C clef=treble-8
"Em""^D"[E,B,EGBe]2 "^D"[E,B,EGBe] "^U"[GBe] z "^U"[GBe] "^D"[E,B,EGBe] "^U"[GBe] |
"^D"[E,B,EGBe]2 "^D"[E,B,EGBe] "^U"[GBe] z "^U"[GBe] "^D"[E,B,EGBe] "^U"[GBe] |]
```

Say the count aloud while you strum: "1 (and) 2 and (3) and 4 and". The brackets show the swings with no sound. Learn it on the muted strings first, then on Em.

### Accents

An **accent** is a note played louder than the ones around it. A mark like > above a note shows it. In strumming, you accent a stroke by hitting a little harder, not by moving faster. The hand moves at the same speed, and the pick goes a little deeper into the strings.

The example below plays the pattern over four chords, Em, Am, D and G, with an accent on the first stroke of each bar.

```abc
%%opts tab=guitar
X:1
M:4/4
L:1/8
Q:1/4=60
K:C clef=treble-8
"Em"!>!"^D"[E,B,EGBe]2 "^D"[E,B,EGBe] "^U"[GBe] z "^U"[GBe] "^D"[E,B,EGBe] "^U"[GBe] |
"Am"!>!"^D"[A,EAce]2 "^D"[A,EAce] "^U"[Ace] z "^U"[Ace] "^D"[A,EAce] "^U"[Ace] |
"D"!>!"^D"[DAd^f]2 "^D"[DAd^f] "^U"[Ad^f] z "^U"[Ad^f] "^D"[DAd^f] "^U"[Ad^f] |
"G"!>!"^D"[G,B,DGBg]2 "^D"[G,B,DGBg] "^U"[GBg] z "^U"[GBg] "^D"[G,B,DGBg] "^U"[GBg] |]
```

Change chords on beat 1 of each bar, and keep the hand moving through the change. If the change is not ready, play the old chord and take the new one on the next bar. The count never stops.

## Try it

Exercise 1. In the pattern D D U U D U, which counts have a sounding stroke?

<details class="solution" markdown>
<summary>Answer</summary>

Beat 1, beat 2, the "&" after 2, the "&" after 3, beat 4 and the "&" after 4.

</details>

Exercise 2. In the pattern, on which counts does the hand move without hitting the strings?

<details class="solution" markdown>
<summary>Answer</summary>

The "&" after 1 (an up swing) and beat 3 (a down swing).

</details>

Exercise 3. At 60 bpm, how long is one eighth note, in seconds?

<details class="solution" markdown>
<summary>Answer</summary>

One beat is 1 second, and an eighth note is half a beat, so it is 0.5 seconds. Eight strokes fill a bar of 4 seconds.

</details>

### Practice routine (30 minutes)

| Minutes | What to do | Tempo | Listen for |
|---|---|---|---|
| 5 | Warm-up: Em, Am, D and G, four down strokes on each chord, changing on the beat | 60 bpm | Every string ringing, no gap in the change |
| 15 | New skill: muted-string down-up count for two minutes; then eight even strokes on Em; then the pattern D D U U D U on Em; then the four-chord example | 50 bpm, then 60 | The hand never stopping, even spacing of the strokes |
| 5 | Rhythm: tap or clap the pattern on your knee while saying the count | 60 bpm | The two missed swings |
| 5 | Piano: blocked C, F, G, C with the right hand using the nearest inversions (lesson 8) | 60 bpm | All three notes starting together |

Repeat this routine on at least three days.

</div>

## Common mistakes

Stopping the hand on the missed strokes. The hand moves down and up all the time. Only the pick touching the strings changes.

Strumming the up stroke across all six strings. Keep it light and aim for the top three or four strings.

Gripping the pick and the neck too hard. A tight grip makes the strum choppy and tires the hand. If your wrist or forearm hurts, stop and rest, and see a teacher or doctor if the pain returns.

## Key terms

- **Up stroke**: a strum from the thin strings toward the thick strings.
- **Strumming pattern**: the plan for which strokes of a bar to play.
- **Accent**: a note played louder than those around it, marked with >.
