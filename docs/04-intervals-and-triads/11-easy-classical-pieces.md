# Easy classical pieces: a musette and a minuet

<span class="tag tag-piano">Piano</span>

After this lesson you can survey a short classical piece before you play it, split it into sections, learn it in a planned order, and play two short exercises in the style of 18th-century teaching pieces.

You need: your piano.

## The idea

### What an easy classical piece asks of you

Many composers wrote short pieces for students. The pieces in this lesson are of that kind: about 16 to 32 bars, a melody in the right hand, a plain accompaniment in the left, and a few marks for dynamics and articulation. They are built from the skills you already have: steps and skips, a five-finger position or two, triads, and a steady pulse.

Learning a piece is different from playing a drill. A piece has a shape, and you learn it by understanding that shape first and practising the notes second. This lesson gives the method. The exercises below are written for this book so you can use the method before you meet the real pieces.

### Survey the piece first

Before you play a note, look through the whole page for about two minutes. Ask these questions and write the answers in pencil on the score.

1. What are the key signature and the time signature? Name the key, and say which notes are sharp or flat.
2. Where does each hand start? Which position is it, and where does the hand need to move?
3. Where are the phrases? A **phrase** is a musical sentence, usually 2 or 4 bars long, that ends at a point of rest. Slurs often mark them. Bracket each one.
4. Which bars repeat or nearly repeat? Look for the same rhythm, the same pattern moved up or down by step, and the left-hand triad shapes from lesson 8.
5. Where are the hard spots? Circle each leap, hand shift, accidental or tricky rhythm.
6. Is there a word at the start that tells the speed or mood? If so, look it up in the glossary, and start at 60 bpm or slower. Stage 6<!-- link: ../06-meter-terms-and-rhythm/03-tempo-and-dynamics-terms.md --> teaches these terms.

### Split it into sections

Choose sections of 2 to 4 bars, and cut at the end of a phrase. Never end a section in the middle of a phrase. A 16-bar piece in four sections is a good size.

Learn each section in this order: right hand alone, left hand alone, hands together slowly, then hands together up to tempo. Join a new section to the one before it as soon as both are steady. Use these rules.

- Slow is the speed. Play at half the final speed (for example 40 bpm when the target is 80).
- Three clean in a row, then move on. A mistake resets the count.
- Repeat the hard spots in small pieces, 1 to 2 bars at a time.
- Keep going when you slip. Stopping at every mistake teaches stopping.

### A learning plan over seven days

This plan fits a 16-bar piece with four sections of 4 bars (A, B, C, D). Each day is one 15-minute block of the practice routine.

| Day | New work | Review |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Survey the whole piece. Section A, right hand, then left hand | None |
| 2 | Section A, hands together. Section B, hands separately | Section A |
| 3 | Section B, hands together. Join A and B | A and B |
| 4 | Section C, hands separately, then together | A and B joined |
| 5 | Section D, hands separately, then together. Join C and D | C and D joined |
| 6 | Join all four sections slowly | Whole piece at half tempo |
| 7 | Whole piece, three clean in a row at half tempo, then 5 bpm faster | Hard spots only |

## Hear and see

These two exercises are short, original pieces in the style of 18th-century teaching pieces: a melody in two phrases, a plain accompaniment, and gentle dynamics. Use them to practise the survey and the plan. Play Exercise A first.

### Exercise A: a question and an answer

C major, 4/4. The first phrase (bars 1 to 4) ends on D and sounds unfinished, like a question. The second phrase (bars 5 to 8) ends on C and sounds finished, like an answer. Both hands stay in C position: the right thumb on C4 and the left little finger on C3. The slurs mark the phrases.

```abc
X:1
M:4/4
L:1/4
Q:1/4=66
K:C
%%score {RH LH}
V:RH clef=treble
V:LH clef=bass
[V:RH] !mf!(!1!C !3!E !5!G !3!E | !4!F !2!D !3!E2) | (!5!G !4!F !3!E !4!F | !2!D4) |
[V:LH] !5!C,2 !1!G,2 | !5!C,2 !1!G,2 | !5!C,2 !1!G,2 | !1!G,4 |
[V:RH] !p!(!3!E !4!F !5!G !3!E | !4!F !5!G !4!F !2!D) | (!3!E !2!D !1!C !2!D | !1!C4) |]
[V:LH] !5!C,2 !1!G,2 | !2!F,2 !1!G,2 | !5!C,2 !1!G,2 | !5!C,4 |]
```

### Exercise B: a minuet-style dance

G major, 3/4, with one sharp (F#), though no F appears in this tune. The right hand uses G position: the thumb on G4 and fingers 2, 3, 4 and 5 on A, B, C and D. The left hand plays one note per bar. Count "1 2 3" and lean slightly on beat 1.

```abc
X:1
M:3/4
L:1/4
Q:1/4=80
K:G
%%score {RH LH}
V:RH clef=treble
V:LH clef=bass
[V:RH] !mf!(!1!G !2!A !3!B | !4!c2 !2!A) | (!3!B !2!A !1!G | !2!A3) |
[V:LH] !5!G,,3 | !1!D,3 | !5!G,,3 | !1!D,3 |
[V:RH] !p!(!1!G !2!A !3!B | !5!d2 !3!B) | (!4!c !3!B !2!A | !1!G3) |]
[V:LH] !5!G,,3 | !3!B,,3 | !1!D,3 | !5!G,,3 |]
```

## The two pieces

The two pieces come from the notebook Johann Sebastian Bach and his wife Anna Magdalena kept in 1725, a collection of short pieces for the family to learn and play. The Musette in D is by an unknown composer: a musette is a dance over a held drone bass, so the left hand jumps between low and high D. The Minuet in G minor is by Christian Petzold, the composer of the Minuet in G from stage 3, and is its companion piece in a minor key (minor keys are stage 5; for now, read the two flats in the key signature as Bb and Eb). Both are public domain, and the source of each score is printed under it. Use the survey, the sections and the seven-day plan above on each one, and start the second only when the first is steady.

## Try it

Exercise 1. Survey Exercise A: name the key, the time signature, the number of phrases, and the bar where each phrase ends.

<details class="solution" markdown>
<summary>Answer</summary>

C major, 4/4. There are two phrases of 4 bars each. The first ends in bar 4 (on D) and the second in bar 8 (on C).

</details>

Exercise 2. Survey Exercise B: how many sharps does the key signature have, which note does it change, and which bars end on a long note?

<details class="solution" markdown>
<summary>Answer</summary>

G major has one sharp, F#, so every F is F#. The tune does not use F. The long notes are the dotted half notes in bars 4 and 8.

</details>

Exercise 3. You have 4 bars of music at 80 bpm as the target. At what tempo do you start, and when do you speed up?

<details class="solution" markdown>
<summary>Answer</summary>

Start at about 40 bpm, half the target. Speed up by about 5 bpm only after three clean plays in a row at the current tempo.

</details>

### Practice routine (30 minutes)

| Minutes | What to do | Tempo | Listen for |
|---|---|---|---|
| 5 | Warm-up: one-octave C arpeggio with each hand alone, then C, F, G, C blocked chords with the right hand | 60 bpm | An even sound, all chord notes together |
| 15 | New skill: follow the seven-day plan on Exercise A, then Exercise B, then Piece 1 and Piece 2 once they are printed; one section at a time, hands separately, then together | 40 bpm, then 50 | Three clean in a row, and no stops at the bar lines |
| 5 | Reading: survey a new 4-bar line from the [reading drill](../practice/reading-drill.md) (key, time, first note, moves), then play it | 50 bpm | Playing the shape and not hunting for names |
| 5 | Guitar: D D U U D U on Em, Am, D and G, one bar each | 60 bpm | The hand never stopping |

Repeat this routine on at least three days for each piece.

## Common mistakes

Playing through the piece from the start every time. The first bars become smooth and the later bars stay weak. Start sometimes from section C or D.

Practising at full speed. Mistakes you play fast become habits. Use half speed until three plays in a row are clean.

Skipping the survey. A leap or a shift you did not see coming causes the stop. Two minutes of looking saves ten minutes of repeats.

## Key terms

- **Phrase**: a musical sentence, usually 2 or 4 bars, that ends at a point of rest.
- **Survey**: reading through the whole piece before you play, to find the key, the phrases, the patterns and the hard spots.
- **Section**: a group of 2 to 4 bars that you learn as one unit.
