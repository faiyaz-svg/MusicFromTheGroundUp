# Hearing chord quality

<span class="tag tag-ear">Ear</span>

After this lesson you can tell a major triad from a minor one and from a diminished one by ear, in block form and in broken form, even when the chord is inverted.

Before this: [Triads](04-triads.md), [Hearing intervals](03-hearing-intervals.md), [Inversions](07-inversions.md). You need: your piano and your guitar.

## The idea

### Sound first: three colours

Play C, E and G together. Then lower the E to Eb and play the chord again. Then lower the G to Gb as well and play it a third time. You played the C major, C minor and C diminished triads in a row. The root stayed the same, and only the 3rd and the 5th moved by a half step.

```abc
X:1
M:4/4
L:1/4
Q:1/4=60
K:C
[CEG]2 [C_EG]2 | [C_E_G]2 z2 |]
```

The major triad sounds bright and settled. The minor triad sounds darker, but still settled. The diminished triad sounds tense, as if it needs to move to another chord. You do not need to name a feeling for each one. Learn what each sounds like by comparing them on the same root, many times.

### What to listen for

Two questions find the answer for most chords.

1. Is it bright or dark? Bright is major and dark is minor. The 3rd decides this: a major 3rd gives the bright sound, and a minor 3rd the dark one. You trained the two 3rds in lesson 3, with When the Saints Go Marching In for the major 3rd and Greensleeves for the minor 3rd.
2. If it is dark, is it settled or tense? Settled is minor. Tense, with a pinched sound, is diminished. The lowered 5th causes this.

An augmented triad, which you also met in lesson 4, sounds neither bright nor dark. It sounds open and unsettled, because both 3rds are major. You will hear it less often.

The chord quiz in Practice tools has a "Broken" setting that plays the notes one after another, so the intervals are easier to pick out. Use it when block chords feel hard to tell apart.

Listen to the same three triads as broken chords, one note at a time, and hear the 3rd change.

```abc
X:1
M:3/4
L:1/4
Q:1/4=60
K:C
C E G | C _E G | C _E _G |]
```

### Inversions keep the quality

Turning a chord upside down does not change its quality. The notes are the same letters, so the same two 3rds are inside the chord. A major triad in first or second inversion is still major, and a minor one is still minor. The inversion changes the feel of the bass, not the colour.

Listen to C major in root position, first inversion and second inversion, then C minor in the same three positions.

```abc
X:1
M:4/4
L:1/4
Q:1/4=60
K:C
[CEG]2 [EGc]2 | [Gce]2 z2 | [C_EG]2 [_EGc]2 | [Gc_e]2 z2 |]
```

### On the guitar

The Em chord and the E chord are the same except for one note, G against G#. Strum Em, then E, and hear the dark chord turn bright. Do the same with Am and A. This is the same change as C minor to C major on the piano.

## Try it: the self-test

For each test, cover the staff with a sheet of paper, press play, and write down major, minor or diminished for each bar. Then uncover the music and check your answers.

Test 1: eight block chords in root position.

```abc
X:1
M:2/4
L:1/4
Q:1/4=60
K:C
[EGB]2 | [FAc]2 | [Bdf]2 | [D^FA]2 |
[^Fac]2 | [Ace]2 | [CEG]2 | [G_Bd]2 |]
```

<details class="solution" markdown>
<summary>Answer</summary>

Minor (Em), major (F), diminished (Bdim), major (D), diminished (F#dim), minor (Am), major (C), minor (Gm).

</details>

Test 2: five broken chords.

```abc
X:1
M:3/4
L:1/4
Q:1/4=60
K:C
A c e | G B d | B d f | F A c |
E G B |]
```

<details class="solution" markdown>
<summary>Answer</summary>

Minor (Am), major (G), diminished (Bdim), major (F), minor (Em).

</details>

Test 3: five inverted block chords.

```abc
X:1
M:2/4
L:1/4
Q:1/4=60
K:C
[EGc]2 | [cea]2 | [Adf]2 | [dfb]2 |
[Gce]2 |]
```

<details class="solution" markdown>
<summary>Answer</summary>

Major (C/E), minor (Am/C), minor (Dm/A), diminished (Bdim/D), major (C/G).

</details>

Then use the [chord quiz](../practice/chord-quiz.md) in Practice tools for as many rounds as you like.

<div class="practice" markdown>

### Practice routine (30 minutes)

| Minutes | What to do | Tempo | Listen for |
|---|---|---|---|
| 5 | Warm-up: blocked triads C, F, G, Am, Dm and Em with the right hand, then name each as major or minor | 60 bpm | The bright chords and the dark ones |
| 15 | New skill: play major, minor and diminished triads on C, then on G, F and D, comparing them; then the three self-tests | Untimed | The 3rd, then the 5th |
| 5 | Ear training: the [chord quiz](../practice/chord-quiz.md), level 1, then level 2 when level 1 is calm | Untimed | Bright against dark against tense |
| 5 | Guitar: Em, E, Am and A, one strum each, naming each as major or minor before the strum | 60 bpm | G against G# in the E chords |

Repeat this routine on at least three days. Ear training grows in short daily sessions.

## Common mistakes

Listening only to the top note. In an inverted chord the top note can be the root, the 3rd or the 5th. Listen to the whole chord.

Calling every dark chord diminished. A minor chord is dark but settled. A diminished chord is tense and sounds pinched.

Guessing from the notation. In the self-tests, the staff gives the answer away. Cover it first.

## Key terms

- **Chord quality**: the kind of a chord: major, minor, diminished or augmented.
