## The idea

### The two pieces

Robert Schumann (1810 to 1856) published the Album for the Young, Op. 68, in 1848. It is a collection of short piano pieces for young players. This lesson uses the first two. No. 1, Melody, is in C major. No. 2, Soldiers' March, is in G major. Both are short, and both are in the public domain.

Before you play from the scores, build the two skills they need with the short exercises below, which are written for this book.

### Melody over accompaniment

In most piano music one part carries the tune and the other parts support it. The tune is the **melody** and the supporting part is the **accompaniment**. A listener should hear the melody first. That means the melody is played louder, and the accompaniment is played softer, even when the same hand plays both or when the accompaniment has more notes.

Follow these steps.

1. Find the melody. It is usually the highest line, and it moves in longer, singing shapes.
2. Play the melody alone and shape it: connect the notes (legato, from stage 2) and let it rise and fall in volume with the line.
3. Play the accompaniment alone, softly.
4. Play both, and listen to the balance. If the melody is hard to hear, play the accompaniment softer rather than the melody harder.

Common mistake at step 4: pressing both hands with the same weight. Keep the accompanying hand light.

This first exercise has the melody in the right hand, slurred, and the left hand holding quiet chords. The right hand is marked mf and the left hand p. Listen for the tune on top.

```abc
X:1
M:4/4
L:1/4
Q:1/4=60
K:C
%%score {RH LH}
V:RH clef=treble
V:LH clef=bass
[V:RH] !mf!(E F G A) | (G F E2) | (D E F D) | C4 |]
[V:LH] !p![C,E,]4 | [C,E,]4 | [G,,B,,]4 | [C,E,]4 |]
```

### Dotted rhythms

A dot after a note adds half of its length. A dotted quarter note lasts 1.5 beats, which is a quarter plus an eighth. It is often followed by an eighth note, to fill the beat: the pair adds up to two beats.

Count in eighths: "1 & 2 & 3 & 4 &". A dotted quarter lasts three eighths. Starting on beat 1, it holds through "1 &" and "2", so the eighth note after it falls on the "&" of beat 2. Count the bar as "1 (&) (2) &, 3, 4", where the counts in brackets are held, not played.

Clap this rhythm first: dotted quarter, eighth, quarter, quarter. Say "long, short, step, step". The mistake is to play the short note too long, which turns the rhythm into an even pattern. The eighth note is quick and leans into the next note.

```abc
X:1
M:4/4
L:1/4
Q:1/4=60
K:G
!1!G3/ !2!A/ !3!B !1!G | !2!A3/ !3!B/ !4!c2 | !3!B3/ !4!c/ !5!d !3!B | !1!G4 |]
```

### Learning the pieces

When you have the scores, learn each piece in sections of 2 to 4 bars. Look for the end of each phrase: a rest, a long note or a slur ending. A **phrase** is a musical sentence, a group of bars that belong together.

| Step | What to do |
|---|---|
| 1 | Read the key signature (none for No. 1, one sharp for No. 2) and the time signature, and find the highest and lowest notes |
| 2 | Clap the rhythm of the first section, counting aloud, and mark any dotted rhythm |
| 3 | Play the right hand alone, slowly, with the fingering you plan |
| 4 | Play the left hand alone, softly |
| 5 | Put the hands together at half speed, melody louder than accompaniment |
| 6 | Move to the next section, then join the two sections |

## Try it

Exercise 1. How many beats does a dotted quarter note last, and what note value completes a two-beat pair with it?

<details class="solution" markdown>
<summary>Answer</summary>

A dotted quarter lasts 1.5 beats. An eighth note (half a beat) completes the pair to two beats.

</details>

Exercise 2. In the G major march above, in which bars does the dotted rhythm appear?

<details class="solution" markdown>
<summary>Answer</summary>

Bars 1, 2 and 3. Each has a dotted quarter on its first note, followed by an eighth.

</details>

Exercise 3. In the first exercise, which hand has the melody, and which dynamic sign is under it?

<details class="solution" markdown>
<summary>Answer</summary>

The right hand has the melody, marked mf. The left hand has the accompaniment, marked p (softer).

</details>

### Practice routine (30 minutes)

| Minutes | What to do | Tempo | Listen for |
|---|---|---|---|
| 5 | Warm-up: G major scale, one octave, each hand alone | 60 bpm | Even volume |
| 15 | New skill: the melody exercise, then the dotted exercise right hand alone, then the march exercise hands together; then the first section of Melody (No. 1) from the score | 50 bpm, then 60 | Melody louder than accompaniment; the eighth note short |
| 5 | Reading: clap the rhythm of the next section of the piece you are learning, counting aloud | 60 bpm | The dotted notes held their full length |
| 5 | Guitar: G, Em, C, D loop, four down strokes per bar | 60 bpm | A steady strum through each change |

Repeat this routine on at least three days.

- [ ] You can play the melody exercise hands together at 60 bpm, three times in a row, with the right hand louder.

## Common mistakes

Playing every note at the same volume. Choose the melody and play the rest softer.

Making the eighth after a dot too long. Say "long, short" aloud and clap it before you play.

Learning the whole piece at once. Learn two to four bars at a time, hands separately, and join them.

## Film yourself

Record a view of your hands from the side playing the melody exercise. Watch it and check:

- Does the left hand stay lighter than the right?
- Do the wrists stay level and relaxed?
- Do the dotted rhythms keep a steady beat with the short note quick?

## Key terms

- **Melody**: the tune, the part a listener follows.
- **Accompaniment**: the part that supports the melody.
- **Dotted note**: a note with a dot after it, lasting 1.5 times its normal length.
- **Phrase**: a group of bars that forms one musical sentence.
- **Album for the Young**: Schumann's Op. 68, a set of short piano pieces published in 1848.
