# 6/8 on piano

<span class="tag tag-piano">Piano</span>

After this lesson you can play a left-hand lilt in 6/8, play a right-hand melody over it with the beat falling on counts 1 and 4, and play a short 6/8 piece, "Slow Boat", with both hands.

Before this: [Compound meter](01-compound-meter.md), [Hands together](../02-two-hands-and-first-chords/03-hands-together.md), [G and F positions](../02-two-hands-and-first-chords/10-g-and-f-positions.md). You need: your piano.

## The idea

### The lilt

Play any bass note on count 1 of a 6/8 bar and a chord on count 4, and you get a gentle sway, two beats to a bar. This is the **lilt**: a long note (the bass), then a lighter chord, over and over. Nothing plays on counts 2, 3, 5 and 6, so the left hand has long, quiet gaps.

Listen to the left hand alone on a C chord. The bass note is C3 and the chord above it is E3 and G3.

```abc
X:1
M:6/8
L:1/8
Q:3/8=56
K:C
V:LH clef=bass
V:LH
!1!C,3 [!3!E,!1!G,]3 | C,3 [E,G,]3 | C,3 [E,G,]3 | C,6 |]
```

Count "1 2 3 4 5 6" in your head. The bass lands on 1, the chord on 4, and you wait on the other counts.

### Hands, fingers and positions

The left hand shapes match the three chords of the piece. For the C chord, put LH finger 5 on C3: the bass is finger 5, and the chord is fingers 3 and 1 on E3 and G3. For the F chord, move finger 5 to F3 and play A3 and C4 with fingers 3 and 1. For the G chord, finger 5 goes to G3 and fingers 3 and 1 play B3 and D4.

| Chord | Bass (LH 5) | Chord notes (LH 3 and 1) |
|---|---|---|
| C | C3 | E3, G3 |
| F | F3 | A3, C4 |
| G | G3 | B3, D4 |

The right hand stays in C position (thumb on middle C, fingers 1 to 5 on C D E F G) for the whole piece, so you only watch the left hand move.

### Putting the hands together

Follow these steps.

1. Count aloud "1 2 3 4 5 6" at 3/8 = 50, tapping your foot on 1 and 4.
2. Play the left hand alone for the whole piece until it sways without stops.
3. Play the right hand alone, saying the counts.
4. Put the hands together one bar at a time. Land the bass note with the first right-hand note of each bar.
5. Play the whole piece without stopping.

Common mistake at step 4: playing the chord on count 3 instead of count 4. Say the numbers out loud so the chord does not creep early.

Here is "Slow Boat", an original piece. The melody has long notes on the beats and short notes between them. Play it as one sway with two beats to a bar.

```abc
X:1
T:Slow Boat
M:6/8
L:1/8
Q:3/8=56
K:C
%%score {RH LH}
V:RH clef=treble
V:LH clef=bass
V:RH
!mp!E2 F G2 E | G2 F E3 | F2 E D2 C | E3 C3 |
D2 E F2 D | D3 G3 | E2 D C2 E | C6 |]
V:LH
"C"!5!C,3 [E,G,]3 | C,3 [E,G,]3 | "F"F,3 [A,C]3 | "C"C,3 [E,G,]3 |
"G"G,3 [B,D]3 | G,3 [B,D]3 | "C"C,3 [E,G,]3 | C,6 |]
```

Bars 1 and 2 are C, bar 3 is F, bar 4 is C, bars 5 and 6 are G, and bars 7 and 8 are C. Listen for the sway: the bass and the melody note land together on count 1 of every bar.

### Another left-hand pattern

A second pattern spreads the same notes across the six counts, one eighth note each. This is a **broken chord** in 6/8, played from the bottom up and back.

```abc
X:1
M:6/8
L:1/8
Q:3/8=50
K:C
V:LH clef=bass
V:LH
C, E, G, E, G, E, | F, A, C A, C A, | G, B, D B, D B, | C,6 |]
```

The first pattern waits on counts 2, 3, 5 and 6, and this one moves on every count. Try "Slow Boat" with this pattern instead, once the first is steady.

## Try it

Exercise 1. In "Slow Boat", on which counts does the left hand sound?

<details class="solution" markdown>
<summary>Answer</summary>

On counts 1 (the bass note) and 4 (the chord) in every bar except the last. The last bar holds one note.

</details>

Exercise 2. How many bars use the G chord in the left hand, and what are the two notes the left hand plays above the bass?

<details class="solution" markdown>
<summary>Answer</summary>

Two bars, bars 5 and 6. The chord notes are B3 and D4 above the G3 bass.

</details>

| Minutes | What to do | Tempo | Listen for |
|---|---|---|---|
| 5 | Warm-up: left-hand lilt on C, F and G (bass then chord), each three times | 3/8 = 50 | The chord lighter than the bass |
| 15 | New skill: "Slow Boat" in steps 1 to 5 above | 3/8 = 44, then 50, then 56 | Bass and melody together on count 1 |
| 5 | Reading: clap the melody of "Slow Boat" from the page, then the first line of a 6/8 piece in your own music | 3/8 = 50 | Counts 1 and 4 clear |

Repeat this routine on at least three days before you check the boxes below.

## Common mistakes

Pressing the chord as hard as the bass. The chord is a lighter push on count 4.

Moving the right hand when the left changes chords. The right hand stays in C position for this piece.

Playing three even beats per bar. 6/8 has two beats. Keep the lean on 1 and 4.

## Key terms

- **Lilt**: a swaying pattern in 6/8: a bass note on count 1 and a chord on count 4.
- **Broken chord**: the notes of a chord played one after another.
