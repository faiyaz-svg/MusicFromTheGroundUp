# Burgmüller: two easy studies

<span class="tag tag-piano">Piano</span>

After this lesson you can learn a piece in sections, play two real studies by Burgmüller (Op. 100 No. 1 in 4/4 and No. 3 in 6/8), and use a study to fix one technical problem at a time.

Before this: [6/8 on piano](05-six-eight-on-piano.md), [Tempo and dynamics terms](03-tempo-and-dynamics-terms.md), [Articulation and repeat signs](04-articulation-and-repeat-signs.md), [The G and D major scales](../03-the-major-scale-and-keys/08-g-and-d-major-scales.md). You need: your piano.

## The idea

### What a study is

A **study** (also called an **etude**) is a piece written to practise one technical problem, such as an even run of notes, a steady left-hand pattern or a soft accompaniment under a melody, while still sounding like music. Johann Friedrich Burgmüller (1806 to 1874) wrote a set of 25 easy studies, Op. 100. This lesson uses two of them, No. 1 "La candeur" (Candour) and No. 3 "La pastorale" (The Pastoral).

Bar numbers below count the bars as printed on the page. A repeated section is not counted twice. The source edition and licence are printed under each score.

Hard spots in No. 1:

- Bars 1 to 8: the four-note slurs must be even, with a small lift at the end of each slur.
- Bars 9 to 12: the left hand has eighth notes while the right hand plays. Learn the left hand alone first.
- Bar 13 (F#) and bar 14 (sfz): watch for the accidental, then land the accent without banging.
- Bars 15 to 20: the left hand is written in the treble clef, and it returns to the bass clef in bar 21. Read the clef before the notes.
- Bars 21 to 23: the poco rit. and the soft ending (pp).

Hard spots in No. 3:

- Bars 1 and 2: three-note groups in the right hand, each group slurred. Keep them smooth and quiet.
- Bars 3, 5 and 7: the grace notes before the main note.

- Bar 23: the left hand has an F natural (a chord with F and not F#) before the tune settles back into G.
- Bar 28: the rising staccato line, which ends on a high D.

### Order to learn them in

For each study, follow these steps.

1. Play the piece through once slowly, hands together, in small parts if you need them, to hear it.
2. Learn the sections in this order: the section that is repeated (A), then the ending, then the middle (B).
3. Practise each section hands apart, the harder hand first.
4. Put the hands together for 2 bars at a time, then 4 bars.
5. Add the dynamics and the tempo words last.

Start No. 1 at a quarter note = 60 and raise it by 8 bpm on each day you play three clean in a row. Start No. 3 at a dotted quarter = 40 and raise it by 4.

## Common mistakes

Starting with the easier hand and leaving the harder one for last. Learn the harder hand alone at half tempo, then add the other.

Playing the left-hand chords as loudly as the melody. In No. 3 the chords are a quiet cushion. Keep them below the right hand.

Skipping the clef change. When the left hand moves into the treble clef, say "treble clef" aloud before the notes.

## Key terms

- **Study (etude)**: a piece written to practise a technique.
- **Grace note**: a small, quick note played right before a main note.
- **Dolce, cantabile**: sweetly, in a singing style.
- **Allegro moderato, andantino**: moderately fast, close to andante.
