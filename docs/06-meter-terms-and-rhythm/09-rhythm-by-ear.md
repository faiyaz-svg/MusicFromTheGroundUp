# Rhythm by ear

<span class="tag tag-ear">Ear</span>

After this lesson you can tap back a short rhythm you have heard a moment ago, write down a one- or two-bar rhythm from hearing it, and tell 6/8 from 4/4 by ear.

Before this: [Compound meter](01-compound-meter.md), [Triplets and syncopation](02-triplets-and-syncopation.md), [Ties, dots and eighth notes](../02-two-hands-and-first-chords/05-ties-dots-and-eighth-notes.md). You need: your piano or any surface to tap on, and a pencil.

## The idea

### Rhythm is the pattern of long and short

The **rhythm** of a tune is the pattern of long and short notes, apart from the pitches. If you tap the rhythm of "Twinkle, Twinkle, Little Star" (traditional) on a table, a friend can often name the tune, even with no pitches at all. Rhythm is the easiest part of music to copy by ear, because it has no notes to find. It needs only a steady beat and a good memory for patterns.

### Step 1: feel the beat first

Before you tap anything, find the beat. Listen to the first example: a steady pulse on one note, with the first beat of each bar louder. Count with it, "1 2 3 4", and tap your foot.

```abc
X:1
M:4/4
L:1/4
Q:1/4=72
K:C
!f!G !mf!G !mf!G !mf!G | !f!G !mf!G !mf!G !mf!G |]
```

If you can keep the foot going while the rhythm plays, you can hear where each note falls against the beat. If the foot stops, start again with the count only.

### Step 2: tap it back

Follow these steps.

1. Listen to the whole rhythm once without moving. Count the beats with your foot.
2. Play it again and tap it with one hand while the foot keeps the beat.
3. Play it a third time and check each bar: where does it start, where does it end?
4. Say the rhythm in counts, "1 & 2" and so on, before you write it.

Common mistake at step 2: tapping the notes only, with no beat. The beat is what makes the answer checkable, so keep the foot going.

### Step 3: write it down

Write one count for each eighth note, "1 & 2 & 3 & 4 &", and mark each place where a note starts. Then turn the marks into note values: a note lasts until the next one starts.

Listen to this bar and write the counts on which notes start. Then check with the answer.

```abc
X:1
M:4/4
L:1/8
Q:1/4=72
K:C
G2 G G G2 G2 |]
```

<details class="solution" markdown>
<summary>Answer</summary>

Notes start on 1, 2, the "&" after 2, 3 and 4. The rhythm is a quarter note on 1, two eighth notes on 2 and its "&", then quarter notes on 3 and 4.

</details>

### 6/8 against 4/4

6/8 sounds like a rock or a skip: two beats of three notes. 4/4 with eighth notes sounds like a march: four beats of two. If you can feel the beat in two (counting "1 2 3, 4 5 6"), the music is probably compound. If you feel it in four, with "&" between, it is simple.

## Try it: the self-test

Cover the staff with a sheet of paper, press play, and tap the rhythm back. Then write down the counts on which notes start. Then uncover the staff and check.

Test 1.

```abc
X:1
M:4/4
L:1/4
Q:1/4=72
K:C
G G G2 | G G2 G |]
```

<details class="solution" markdown>
<summary>Answer</summary>

Bar 1: quarter, quarter, half note. Bar 2: quarter, half note, quarter. Notes start on 1, 2, 3 in bar 1 and on 1, 2, 4 in bar 2.

</details>

Test 2.

```abc
X:1
M:4/4
L:1/8
Q:1/4=72
K:C
G2 G G G2 G2 | G G G G G2 z2 |]
```

<details class="solution" markdown>
<summary>Answer</summary>

Bar 1: notes start on 1, 2, the "&" after 2, 3 and 4. Bar 2: notes start on 1, &, 2, & and 3, with a quarter rest on beat 4.

</details>

Test 3.

```abc
X:1
M:4/4
L:1/8
Q:1/4=72
K:C
G3 G G2 G2 | G G G3 G3 |]
```

<details class="solution" markdown>
<summary>Answer</summary>

Bar 1: a dotted quarter note on 1, an eighth on the "&" after 2, then quarter notes on 3 and 4. Bar 2: eighth notes on 1 and the "&" after 1, a dotted quarter on 2, and a dotted quarter on the "&" after 3.

</details>

Test 4.

```abc
X:1
M:4/4
L:1/4
Q:1/4=72
K:C
G z G G | G G z G |]
```

<details class="solution" markdown>
<summary>Answer</summary>

Bar 1: notes on 1, 3 and 4, with a quarter rest on 2. Bar 2: notes on 1, 2 and 4, with a quarter rest on 3.

</details>

Test 5. This one is in 6/8.

```abc
X:1
M:6/8
L:1/8
Q:3/8=50
K:C
G3 G G G | G2 G G3 |]
```

<details class="solution" markdown>
<summary>Answer</summary>

Bar 1: a dotted quarter on count 1, then eighth notes on counts 4, 5 and 6. Bar 2: a quarter note on count 1, an eighth on count 3, and a dotted quarter on count 4.

</details>

Test 6. This one is syncopated.

```abc
X:1
M:4/4
L:1/8
Q:1/4=72
K:C
G G2 G2 G2 G | G2 G2 G4 |]
```

<details class="solution" markdown>
<summary>Answer</summary>

Bar 1: notes start on 1 and on the "&" after 1, 2, 3 and 4. Bar 2: notes start on 1, 2 and 3 (a quarter, a quarter and a half note).

</details>

### Practice routine (30 minutes)

| Minutes | What to do | Tempo | Listen for |
|---|---|---|---|
| 5 | Warm-up: tap a steady beat with the foot while clapping eighth notes | 72 bpm | The foot never stops |
| 15 | New skill: tests 1 to 6, covering the staff, tapping back, writing the counts, then checking | 72 bpm; 6/8 at 3/8 = 50 | Where each note falls against the beat |
| 5 | Reading: clap one line of a piece you are learning, then play it | 60 bpm | The clap matching the page |
| 5 | Guitar: pattern 3 on Em from lesson 6, counting aloud | 3/8 = 50 | A steady hand |

Repeat this routine on at least three days before you check the boxes below.

- [ ] You can tap back tests 1, 2 and 4 after hearing each once or twice, with the foot on the beat.
- [ ] You can write down the counts for tests 1 to 4 correctly without looking at the staff.
- [ ] You can say whether a short example is in 6/8 or 4/4 by ear, four times out of five.

## Common mistakes

Writing the notes before the beat is settled. Always count the beat first.

Forgetting rests. A gap between notes is not always a rest: a long note sounds until the next one. Listen for silence, for gaps as well as new starts.

Tapping the rhythm as even beats. Rhythm is long and short, so exaggerate the long notes by holding your hand down.

## Key terms

- **Rhythm**: the pattern of long and short notes in time.
- **Tap back**: repeat a rhythm you heard by tapping it.
- **Start (attack)**: the moment a note begins.
