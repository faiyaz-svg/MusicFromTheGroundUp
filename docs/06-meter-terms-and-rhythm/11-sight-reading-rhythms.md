# Sight-reading rhythms

<span class="tag tag-reading">Reading</span>

After this lesson you can read and clap a new rhythm at sight in 4/4, 3/4 and 6/8, including eighth notes, dotted rhythms and rests, and keep going without stopping.

Before this: [Sight-reading basics](../02-two-hands-and-first-chords/09-sight-reading-basics.md), [Ties, dots and eighth notes](../02-two-hands-and-first-chords/05-ties-dots-and-eighth-notes.md), [Compound meter](01-compound-meter.md), [Rhythm by ear](09-rhythm-by-ear.md). You need: your piano and a metronome app.

## The idea

### Rhythm first

Many mistakes in sight reading are rhythm mistakes: the right notes at the wrong time. A player who keeps a steady beat and misses a note sounds better than one who plays the notes in a lurching rhythm. So when you read a new piece, look at the rhythm first and the pitches second.

The skill is separate from pitch reading. You practise it on a single note, or by clapping alone, so that you can give the whole attention to the counting.

### The rhythm drill

Follow these steps for each line.

1. Read the time signature. Decide the beat: a quarter note in 4/4 and 3/4, a dotted quarter in 6/8.
2. Scan the whole line. Find the hardest bar: the one with rests, dots or a pair of eighth notes in an odd place.
3. Set the beat. Count one empty bar aloud at the tempo.
4. Clap the line, counting aloud, and do not stop for a mistake. Keep the beat going.
5. Play it again with your foot tapping, and check the hard bar.
6. Play the line on a single key of the piano. The pitch is the same, so the rhythm is all you think about.

Common mistake at step 4: stopping to fix a bar. In a real piece the beat does not wait for you. If you slip, find the next beat and rejoin.

### Eighths, dots and rests: what to look for

Group the notes by the beat. In 4/4, the beam of two eighth notes sits inside one beat, so a pair of eighths is one beat of "1 &". A dotted quarter followed by an eighth fills two beats, counted "1 & 2 &": the first note starts on 1 and lasts through the 2, and the eighth starts on the last "&". A rest is counted, not skipped: say its number out loud and stay silent.

In 6/8, group by threes: a dotted quarter fills one beat, and three eighths fill one beat. A quarter note followed by an eighth fills one beat.

### Three drills

Drill 1 is in 4/4. Set the beat at 66 bpm, count one empty bar, then clap. Beat 4 of bar 2 is a rest.

```abc
X:1
M:4/4
L:1/8
Q:1/4=66
K:C
G2 G2 G4 | G G G G G2 z2 | G3 G G2 G2 | G2 G G G4 |]
```

Drill 2 is in 3/4. Count "1 2 3" and lean on 1.

```abc
X:1
M:3/4
L:1/8
Q:1/4=66
K:C
G2 G G G2 | G3 G G2 | z2 G2 G2 | G6 |]
```

Drill 3 is in 6/8. Set the beat at 3/8 = 50, count "1 2 3 4 5 6" or "1 2" for the two beats, then clap.

```abc
X:1
M:6/8
L:1/8
Q:3/8=50
K:C
G3 G G G | G2 G G3 | G G G G3 | G6 |]
```

## Try it

Exercise 1. In Drill 1, which bar has a rest, and on which beat is it?

<details class="solution" markdown>
<summary>Answer</summary>

Bar 2, on beat 4. The bar is four eighth notes (beats 1 and 2), a quarter note (beat 3) and a quarter rest.

</details>

Exercise 2. In Drill 3, how many notes start in bar 3, and on which counts?

<details class="solution" markdown>
<summary>Answer</summary>

Four notes, on counts 1, 2, 3 and 4. The last one is a dotted quarter note that lasts through counts 4, 5 and 6.

</details>

Exercise 3. In Drill 2, how many beats does the first note of bar 2 last?

<details class="solution" markdown>
<summary>Answer</summary>

One and a half beats (a dotted quarter). Bar 2 is a dotted quarter, an eighth and a quarter: 1.5 + 0.5 + 1 = 3 beats.

</details>

### Practice routine (30 minutes)

| Minutes | What to do | Tempo | Listen for |
|---|---|---|---|
| 5 | Warm-up: clap eighth notes against a metronome, then rests on beats 2 and 4 | 66 bpm | Claps exactly on the click |
| 15 | New skill: clap and count Drills 1, 2 and 3, then play each on one piano key, with no stops | 66 bpm; 3/8 = 50 | The beat staying steady after a rest |
| 5 | Reading: the [Reading drill](../practice/reading-drill.md), looking at the rhythm of each line first | Untimed | Naming the hard bar before you start |
| 5 | Guitar: 6/8 strumming pattern 3 on Em | 3/8 = 50 | A steady hand |

Repeat this routine on at least three days before you check the boxes below.

- [ ] You can clap each drill once at sight, counting aloud, with no stops.
- [ ] You can name the hard bar of a new line in under 10 seconds.

## Common mistakes

Reading the notes before the time signature. A rhythm in 6/8 and a rhythm in 3/4 can look alike on the page.

Rushing after a rest. Count the rest out loud.

Looking only at the next note. Look one bar ahead.

## Key terms

- **Rhythm-first reading**: scanning for the beat and the hard bar before the pitches.
- **Rhythm drill**: a line of notes on one pitch, to clap at sight.
