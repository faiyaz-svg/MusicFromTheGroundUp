# Legato pedalling

<span class="tag tag-piano">Piano</span>

After this lesson you can change the damper pedal right after each new chord, so the sound stays connected and does not blur, and you can hear when a change is late or early.

Before this: [The damper pedal](01-damper-pedal.md), [Legato, staccato and dynamics](../02-two-hands-and-first-chords/07-legato-staccato-and-dynamics.md). You need: your piano.

## The idea

### Sound first: the blur

Play the first example with the pedal held down for all four bars. The harmony changes from C to F to G, but the old notes keep ringing under the new ones, and the whole thing smears.

```abc
X:1
M:4/4
L:1/4
Q:1/4=60
K:C
%%score {RH LH}
V:RH clef=treble
V:LH clef=bass
[V:RH] !mp!"_Ped."E G c G | F A c A | G B d B | [Ec]4 "_*"|]
[V:LH] C,2 G,,2 | F,,2 C,2 | G,,2 D,2 | C,4 |]
```

Now play the same notes, but lift the pedal at each bar line and press again, as in the next example. Each chord is clean, but between the lift and the press there is a small gap of silence, and the music sounds chopped.

```abc
X:1
M:4/4
L:1/4
Q:1/4=60
K:C
%%score {RH LH}
V:RH clef=treble
V:LH clef=bass
[V:RH] !mp!"_Ped."E G c G "_*" | "_Ped."F A c A "_*" | "_Ped."G B d B "_*" | "_Ped."[Ec]4 "_*"|]
[V:LH] C,2 G,,2 | F,,2 C,2 | G,,2 D,2 | C,4 |]
```

Neither is what a pianist wants. The first is joined but muddy, and the second is clean but broken.

### Legato pedalling

**Legato pedalling** joins the chords and keeps them clean. You change the pedal right after you play the new chord, not at the same moment.

1. Play the first chord, then press the pedal (lesson 1).
2. Play the next chord. The old notes are still ringing.
3. Right after the new keys go down, lift the pedal and press it again in one quick motion, like a heel tap.
4. The lift cuts off the old chord, and the new press catches the new notes. The listener hears no gap.

Because your foot moves right after your hands, not together with them, this is also called **syncopated pedalling**: the pedal change falls off the beat that the hands play on. The mark in this book is `*Ped.` under the first note of the new chord. It tells you the change comes right after that note.

```abc
X:1
M:4/4
L:1/4
Q:1/4=60
K:C
%%score {RH LH}
V:RH clef=treble
V:LH clef=bass
[V:RH] !mp!E G c G | F A c A | G B d B | [Ec]4 |]
[V:LH] "_Ped."C,2 G,,2 | "_*Ped."F,,2 C,2 | "_*Ped."G,,2 D,2 | "_*Ped."C,4 "_*"|]
```

Listen for a connected sound with no old chord left under the new one. If you hear the old chord for a moment, you changed late; if you hear a small gap, you changed early or lifted too long.

### Listening for blur

Your ear is the only check, because the foot gives no signal. After each change ask: do I hear only the new chord? You can test it by playing a chord change very slowly and listening to the moment of the change. A late change leaves an old chord ringing for a beat. An early change cuts the new chord before it is played, so the new notes are not held at all.

### When to change

Change the pedal whenever the harmony changes, that is, when the chord notes change. That might be every bar, every two beats, or once in four bars. In a melody that moves by step and does not change chord, notes that do not belong to the chord also blur when held. Change on those notes as well.

Common mistake at step 3: lifting and pressing slowly, so there is a gap. The change is quick. Practise the motion on a held chord first.

## Try it

Exercise 1. A bar has the chord C on beats 1 and 2 and the chord G on beats 3 and 4. Where does the pedal change?

<details class="solution" markdown>
<summary>Answer</summary>

On beat 3, right after you play the G chord.

</details>

Exercise 2. You hear the old chord under the new one for about a beat. Was the change too early or too late?

<details class="solution" markdown>
<summary>Answer</summary>

Too late (or missed). The lift came after the new chord had already sounded for a while, or never came.

</details>

Exercise 3. Why is the pedal change not made at exactly the same moment as the new chord?

<details class="solution" markdown>
<summary>Answer</summary>

If you change at the same moment, the lift cuts the old chord but the press can come before the new notes are struck, and the new notes are not caught. Changing right after the new chord catches them.

</details>

### Practice routine (30 minutes)

| Minutes | What to do | Tempo | Listen for |
|---|---|---|---|
| 5 | Warm-up: hold a C chord (C4, E4, G4) with the pedal, practise the change motion on it 10 times, lift and press quickly | 60 bpm | No gap in the sound |
| 15 | New skill: the third example, left hand alone with the pedal changes, then right hand alone, then together | 50 bpm, then 60 | Only the new chord sounding after each change |
| 5 | Reading: play the blur example and the legato example one after the other and say which bars differ | Untimed | The old chord disappearing |
| 5 | Guitar: C, G and Dm, one strum per bar | 60 bpm | Every string ringing |

Repeat this routine on at least three days.

</div>

## Common mistakes

Changing on the beat with the hands. The new notes are not caught. Move the foot right after the keys.

Holding the pedal through a melody that moves by step. Notes outside the chord pile up. Change more often.

Lifting for too long. A gap between chords breaks the legato. The lift and press are one quick tap.

## Film yourself

Film your foot and hands together and look at the timing of each change.

Watch: [Piano Lesson on Legato Pedalling](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=F_d8oask2VU) by Pianist Magazine (free, on YouTube).

Check that:

- the foot taps down after the keys, not with them,
- the heel stays on the floor during every change,
- the foot never leaves the pedal.

## Key terms

- **Legato pedalling**: changing the pedal right after the new chord is played, so the sound stays connected and clean.
- **Syncopated pedalling**: another name for legato pedalling; the foot moves off the beat the hands play on.
- **Blur**: old and new notes sounding together because the pedal was held through a change.
