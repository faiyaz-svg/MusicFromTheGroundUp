# Alberti bass and waltz bass

<span class="tag tag-piano">Piano</span>

After this lesson you can play an Alberti bass and a waltz bass in the left hand under a right-hand melody, and choose a left-hand pattern to suit the time signature and mood.

Before this: [Hands together](../02-two-hands-and-first-chords/03-hands-together.md), [Ties, dots and eighth notes](../02-two-hands-and-first-chords/05-ties-dots-and-eighth-notes.md), [Triads and arpeggios on piano](../04-intervals-and-triads/05-triads-and-arpeggios-on-piano.md), [Legato pedalling](02-legato-pedalling.md). You need: your piano.

## The idea

### Sound first: two ways to accompany

Listen to the same C chord played two ways under a right-hand note. In the first the left hand rocks through the notes of the chord. In the second it plays a low note and then two short chords, like a dance step.

```abc
X:1
M:4/4
L:1/8
Q:1/4=60
K:C
%%score {RH LH}
V:RH clef=treble
V:LH clef=bass
[V:RH] !mp![Ec]8 | [Ec]8 |]
[V:LH] C,G,E,G, C,G,E,G, | C,G,E,G, C,G,E,G, |]
```

```abc
X:1
M:3/4
L:1/4
Q:1/4=120
K:C
%%score {RH LH}
V:RH clef=treble
V:LH clef=bass
[V:RH] !mp![Ec]3 | [Ec]3 |]
[V:LH] C, [E,G,] [E,G,] | C, [E,G,] [E,G,] |]
```

The first pattern is an **Alberti bass**. The second is a **waltz bass**, also called oom-pah-pah.

### The Alberti bass

An **Alberti bass** is a left-hand pattern that breaks a chord into the order lowest, highest, middle, highest. For a C chord (C, E, G) that is C, G, E, G. It is played in eighth notes. In 4/4, one chord fills a bar: the four notes are played twice around.

Use left-hand fingers 5, 1, 3, 1. On C that is C3 (5), G3 (1), E3 (3), G3 (1). The pattern is the same on every triad, so you learn one hand shape and move it:

| Chord | Notes in order | Fingers |
|---|---|---|
| C major | C3, G3, E3, G3 | 5, 1, 3, 1 |
| F major | F2, C3, A2, C3 | 5, 1, 3, 1 |
| G major | G2, D3, B2, D3 | 5, 1, 3, 1 |

Play it lightly and evenly. The pattern is accompaniment: it must stay quieter than the right hand (lesson 9 covers that in detail).

Common mistake: pressing the top note harder every time it comes back. The pattern then sounds like a bump. Keep all four notes at the same soft level.

### The waltz bass

A **waltz bass** is for 3/4. The left hand plays a low single note on beat 1 and a chord on beats 2 and 3. The low note is the root of the chord, and the chord is the upper two or three notes. It has an "oom-pah-pah" feel because the low note is heavy and the chords are light.

On C in the left hand: C3 with finger 5 on beat 1, then E3 and G3 together (fingers 3 and 1) on beats 2 and 3. Keep the wrist loose and jump from the low note to the chord by moving the whole hand, not by stretching the fingers.

### Choosing a pattern

Pick the pattern from the time signature, the speed and the mood.

| Situation | Pattern | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 4/4, flowing and gentle | Alberti bass in eighth notes | A steady stream under the melody |
| 3/4, a dance | Waltz bass | Beat 1 heavy, beats 2 and 3 light |
| Slow, quiet, spacious | One long note or open fifth with the pedal | Leaves room (lesson 5) |
| Lively, march-like | Low note and chord on alternate beats | A strong, short pulse |

The mood comes from the pattern as much as the notes. The same three chords sound like a lullaby with an Alberti bass and like a dance with a waltz bass.

## Hear and see

Here is a short original piece for the Alberti bass in 4/4, called Window Seat. The right hand has a slow tune. Listen for the left hand staying under the melody and never getting louder than it.

```abc
X:1
T:Window Seat
C:Original, for this book
M:4/4
L:1/8
Q:1/4=66
K:C
%%score {RH LH}
V:RH clef=treble
V:LH clef=bass
[V:RH] !mp!E4 G4 | A4 c4 | B4 G4 | c8 |
[V:LH] !p!C,G,E,G, C,G,E,G, | F,,C,A,,C, F,,C,A,,C, | G,,D,B,,D, G,,D,B,,D, | C,G,E,G, C,G,E,G, |]
```

Now a short waltz in 3/4, called Slow Carousel. The left hand is oom-pah-pah and the right hand floats above it.

```abc
X:1
T:Slow Carousel
C:Original, for this book
M:3/4
L:1/4
Q:1/4=112
K:C
%%score {RH LH}
V:RH clef=treble
V:LH clef=bass
[V:RH] !mp!E2 G | c2 B | A2 F | G3 |
[V:LH] !p!C, [E,G,] [E,G,] | G,, [B,,D,] [B,,D,] | F,, [A,,C,] [A,,C,] | G,, [B,,D,] [B,,D,] |]
```

## Try it

Exercise 1. Write the Alberti bass notes for an F chord (F, A, C), starting on F2.

<details class="solution" markdown>
<summary>Answer</summary>

F2, C3, A2, C3. Lowest, highest, middle, highest.

</details>

Exercise 2. In a waltz bass, which beat gets the low note?

<details class="solution" markdown>
<summary>Answer</summary>

Beat 1. Beats 2 and 3 get the chord.

</details>

Exercise 3. A slow, quiet piece in 4/4 needs a left-hand pattern. Which would you choose, and why?

<details class="solution" markdown>
<summary>Answer</summary>

A held low note or an open fifth with the pedal, or a slow Alberti bass. Busy patterns would crowd a slow, quiet tune.

</details>

## Common mistakes

Letting the left hand grow louder than the melody. The pattern is accompaniment. Play it softly.

Leaning on the top note of the Alberti figure. Keep the four notes even.

Stretching to reach the waltz chord. Move the whole hand from the low note to the chord.

## Key terms

- **Alberti bass**: a left-hand pattern that plays a chord as lowest, highest, middle, highest.
- **Waltz bass**: a left-hand pattern in 3/4: a low note on beat 1, a chord on beats 2 and 3.
