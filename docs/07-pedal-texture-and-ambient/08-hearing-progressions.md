# Hearing chord progressions

<span class="tag tag-ear">Ear</span>

After this lesson you can follow the bass line of a short chord progression, tell the home chord I from the chord V built on the fifth note of the key, and hear whether a progression comes home or stops away from home.

Before this: [The major scale](../03-the-major-scale-and-keys/02-the-major-scale.md), [Singing the scale by number](../03-the-major-scale-and-keys/04-singing-the-scale-by-number.md), [Chords C, G and D minor](../03-the-major-scale-and-keys/06-chords-c-g-and-d-minor.md), [Triads](../04-intervals-and-triads/04-triads.md). You need: your piano or guitar and your ears.

## The idea

### Sound first: three chords

A **chord progression** is a series of chords played one after another. Play this one, with the play bar, and sing the lowest note of each chord along: C, G, C.

```abc
X:1
M:4/4
L:1/4
Q:1/4=70
K:C
%%score {RH LH}
V:RH clef=treble
V:LH clef=bass
[V:RH] "C"[Ec]4 | "G"[GBd]4 | "C"[Ec]4 |]
[V:LH] C,4 | G,,4 | C,4 |]
```

The first and last chords sound settled, and the middle one sounds like it is leaning toward them. That is the main thing to hear in this lesson.

### Hearing the bass

The **bass note** of a chord is its lowest note. In a simple chord the bass is usually the root, and the bass notes together make a melody of their own. If you can hear the bass moving, you can hear the chords change, even without naming them.

Sing or hum the lowest note of each chord in the example above. It moves C, G, C. Between C and G the bass leaps down, and between G and C it leaps back up. These leaps between the roots of I and V are the strongest moves a bass can make.

### I and V

In a major key, number the notes of the scale 1 to 7. The **I chord** is the chord built on note 1, the home note (the tonic). In C major it is C. The **V chord** is the chord built on note 5. In C major it is G. Stage 9 names all the chords this way, in [Roman numerals](../09-harmony/01-roman-numerals.md); here we only need I and V, and later IV (built on note 4, F in C major).

I sounds like home: the music can rest there. V sounds like the music is on its way back, an unfinished sentence. When V is followed by I, the music comes home.

### Does it come home?

A progression **comes home** when its last chord is I and the harmony settles there. If it ends on V, the music is left hanging, like a question. Both are useful: a hanging end sets up the next phrase.

Listen to these two. The first ends on I, and the second ends on V.

```abc
X:1
M:4/4
L:1/4
Q:1/4=70
K:C
%%score {RH LH}
V:RH clef=treble
V:LH clef=bass
[V:RH] [Ec]4 | [Fc]4 | [GBd]4 | [Ec]4 |]
[V:LH] C,4 | F,,4 | G,,4 | C,4 |]
```

```abc
X:1
M:4/4
L:1/4
Q:1/4=70
K:C
%%score {RH LH}
V:RH clef=treble
V:LH clef=bass
[V:RH] [Ec]4 | [Ec]4 | [Fc]4 | [GBd]4 |]
[V:LH] C,4 | C,4 | F,,4 | G,,4 |]
```

Sing the bass of each. The first goes C, F, G, C, ending on home. The second goes C, C, F, G, ending on G, away from home.

## Hear and see

### Self-test

Close your eyes, or look away from the music, and listen to each example three times. For each one, write down the bass notes you hear, and say if it comes home or stops away from home. Then open the answer.

Test 1:

```abc
X:1
M:4/4
L:1/4
Q:1/4=70
K:C
%%score {RH LH}
V:RH clef=treble
V:LH clef=bass
[V:RH] [Ec]4 | [GBd]4 | [Ec]4 | [Ec]4 |]
[V:LH] C,4 | G,,4 | C,4 | C,4 |]
```

<details class="solution" markdown>
<summary>Answer</summary>

Bass: C, G, C, C (I, V, I, I). It comes home.

</details>

## Try it

Exercise 1. In C major, which chord is I and which is V?

<details class="solution" markdown>
<summary>Answer</summary>

I is C and V is G.

</details>

Exercise 2. A progression has the bass notes G, C, G, C in the key of C. Does it come home?

<details class="solution" markdown>
<summary>Answer</summary>

Yes. It ends on C, the I chord.

</details>

Exercise 3. Play I and V in D major on your piano or guitar. What are the chords?

<details class="solution" markdown>
<summary>Answer</summary>

I is D (D, F#, A). V is A (A, C#, E).

</details>

## Common mistakes

Listening to the top notes instead of the bass. The bass shows the chord. Sing the lowest note.

Expecting every progression to come home. Some end on V on purpose.

Guessing from the notation. Cover the page and use your ears.

## Key terms

- **Chord progression**: a series of chords played one after another.
- **Bass note**: the lowest note of a chord.
- **I chord**: the chord built on note 1 of the scale; it sounds like home.
- **V chord**: the chord built on note 5 of the scale; it leads back to I.
