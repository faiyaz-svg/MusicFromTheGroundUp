# Tchaikovsky: Old French Song

<span class="tag tag-piano">Piano</span>

After this lesson you can play Tchaikovsky's Old French Song, a slow melody in G minor over a moving left hand, with a singing right hand, a quiet left hand and a pedal that changes with the harmony.

## The idea

### The piece

Old French Song is No. 16 of the Album for the Young, Op. 39, a set of short piano pieces by Pyotr Ilyich Tchaikovsky (1840 to 1893). It is in G minor, in 2/4, marked Moderato assai (quite moderate). ### The key

The key signature has two flats, Bb and Eb. That is G minor. G minor's relative major is Bb major, and the two keys share this key signature. The piece mostly uses the notes of the G natural minor scale: G, A, Bb, C, D, Eb, F. A raised seventh, F#, appears in the dominant chord (bars 7, 15, 20, 24 and 31), the same raised 7th you met in harmonic minor.

### The form, by bar

| Bars | What happens | Dynamics |
|---|---|---|
| Pickup, 1 to 8 | The main tune, built on stepwise phrases rising G A Bb C D | p |
| 9 to 16 | The same tune again | pp |
| 17 to 24 | A middle section: the left hand plays short staccato broken chords, the tune climbs and swells | p, crescendo in bars 20 and 21, mf in bar 22, diminuendo in bar 23 |
| 25 to 32 | The main tune returns and ends on a G minor chord in bar 32 | p |

Bar 8 holds the last note (G) of the tune and also carries the short D that is the pickup of the next statement.

### What the hands do

The right hand plays the tune. It moves mostly by step, so it is a good piece to practise legato, the smooth connected touch from stage 2, and a singing tone.

The left hand does more than the usual accompaniment. In bars 1 to 6 it holds a low G while a small line moves above it, so you play two lines in the left hand. Bar 7 is a D7 chord (the left hand spreads D, F#, C), the chord that leads home to G minor in bar 8, which is why the end of that phrase feels like a sentence closing.

In bars 17 to 19 the left hand plays short broken chords, C minor shapes (C, G, C, Eb), detached while the right hand sings legato. This is the hardest part of the piece, because the hands touch the keys in different ways at the same time.

### Hard spots

1. Bars 1 to 6, left hand: holding the low G while the notes above move. Hold the G with finger 5 and play the moving line with the other fingers. Find the fingering that fits your hand and write it in pencil.
2. Bar 6 (and 14, 30): the figure d, e, d in sixteenth notes in the right hand, an ornament-like turn. Count it slowly: "1 e & a" in sixteenths, then speed it up.
3. Bars 17 to 19: right hand legato, left hand staccato. Practise each alone, then together at a slow tempo.
4. Bars 20 to 23: the crescendo to mf and the diminuendo back to p. Change the loudness smoothly.
5. Bar 32: the final chord. Hold it, then lift the pedal slowly.

### Pedalling

The score as published has no pedal marks, so these are suggestions. Use the legato pedalling of lesson 2.

1. Bars 1 to 6: pedal lightly and change about once per bar, right after the left hand moves. If the stepwise tune blurs, change more often.
2. Bar 7 to 8: change on bar 7 (the D7 chord) and again on bar 8 (G minor).
3. Bars 17 to 19: little or no pedal. The staccato left hand should sound dry.
4. Bars 20 to 23: a light legato pedal, changing with the harmony, so the crescendo grows.
5. Bar 32: hold the pedal for the final chord and lift slowly.

If you can use the sostenuto pedal on your piano, it can hold the low G in bars 1 to 6 so you need less finger work. Try that only after you can play the piece with the damper pedal alone.

### Voicing

Use the work from lesson 9. The right-hand tune is mf or p, the left-hand moving line is quieter, and the held G is the quietest. In bar 22 the tune and the left hand are in the same range, so check the balance again by recording yourself.

Exercise 1. What is the key of Old French Song, and how many flats are in the key signature?

<details class="solution" markdown>

</details>

Exercise 2. Which bars are the middle section, and what is new in the left hand?

<details class="solution" markdown>
<summary>Answer</summary>

Bars 17 to 24. The left hand plays short staccato broken chords.

</details>

Exercise 3. Why do you not use the pedal in bars 17 to 19?

<details class="solution" markdown>
<summary>Answer</summary>

The left hand is staccato, and pedal would make it ring and lose the dry, short sound.

</details>

### Practice routine (30 minutes)

| Minutes | What to do | Tempo | Listen for |
|---|---|---|---|
| 5 | Warm-up: G natural minor scale, one octave, each hand alone | 60 bpm | Even notes, Bb and Eb correct |
| 15 | New skill: learn the piece in 4-bar sections (pickup to bar 4, bars 5 to 8, and so on), right hand alone, left hand alone, then together; use the excerpts for the hard spots | 50 bpm, then 60, then 70 | A singing right hand over a quiet left hand |
| 5 | Reading: the first excerpt, hands together, then the second excerpt | 60 bpm | Staccato in the left hand, legato in the right |
| 5 | Guitar: Dsus2, Dsus4 and D, one strum per bar | 60 bpm | Every string ringing |

Repeat this routine on at least ten days. A whole piece takes more than three.

- [ ] You can play bars 1 to 8 hands together at 60 bpm, three times in a row with no stops.
- [ ] You can play bars 17 to 19 with staccato in the left hand and legato in the right, at 60 bpm, three times in a row.
- [ ] You can play the whole piece at 60 bpm without stopping, even with a few small slips.
- [ ] You can record yourself and hear the right hand louder than the left in every bar.

## Common mistakes

Playing the held left-hand G as loudly as the tune. Keep it quiet.

Pedalling through bars 17 to 19. The staccato disappears. Leave the pedal up.

Speeding up the sixteenth-note figure in bar 6. Slow it down until it is even.

- the right hand stays smooth,
- the foot changes the pedal right after the harmony changes.

## Key terms

- **Moderato assai**: a tempo mark meaning moderate speed, with plenty of that moderation.
- **Staccato**: short and detached.
- **Pickup**: a short note before the first full bar.
