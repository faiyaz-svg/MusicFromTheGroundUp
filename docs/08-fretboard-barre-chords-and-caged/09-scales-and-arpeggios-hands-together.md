# Scales and arpeggios hands together

<span class="tag tag-piano">Piano</span>

After this lesson you can play a two-octave major scale with both hands together in parallel and in contrary motion, play a two-octave arpeggio, and use a set of tempo goals to know how fast to go.

Before this: [All twelve major scales](08-all-major-scales.md), [The G and D major scales](../03-the-major-scale-and-keys/08-g-and-d-major-scales.md), [Triads and arpeggios on piano](../04-intervals-and-triads/05-triads-and-arpeggios-on-piano.md). You need: your piano and a metronome.

## The idea

### Two octaves, two hands, an octave apart

For a two-octave scale, the right hand uses the one-octave pattern twice. In C major the right hand plays 1 2 3 1 2 3 4, then the thumb passes under again: 1 2 3 1 2 3 4 5. The left hand ascending plays 5 4 3 2 1 3 2 1 and then 4 3 2 1 3 2 1. Look at the second octave: the left hand starts again with finger 4 after the thumb, not with finger 5.

In **parallel motion** both hands travel in the same direction, an octave apart. The right hand starts on C4 and the left hand on C3. Listen first, then play it hands separately, then together.

```abc
X:1
M:4/4
L:1/4
Q:1/4=60
K:C
%%score {RH LH}
V:RH clef=treble
V:LH clef=bass
[V:RH] !1!C !2!D !3!E !1!F | !2!G !3!A !4!B !1!c | !2!d !3!e !1!f !2!g | !3!a !4!b !5!c'2 |]
[V:LH] !5!C, !4!D, !3!E, !2!F, | !1!G, !3!A, !2!B, !1!C | !4!D !3!E !2!F !1!G | !3!A !2!B !1!c2 |]
```

The thumbs pass under at different moments: the right thumb on the 4th, 8th and 11th notes of the scale, the left thumb on the 5th, 8th and 12th. Only at the 8th note do both thumbs cross at once. This is the hard part of hands-together scales. Go slowly enough that each thumb arrives on time.

Common mistake: speeding up in the middle, where the thumbs pass. Count steadily "1 2 3 4" and keep the pulse the same through the crossing.

### Contrary motion

In **contrary motion** the hands move in opposite directions: they start on the same note and move apart, or start apart and come together. The fingers are a mirror: the same finger plays in both hands at the same time. Start both thumbs on middle C. The right hand climbs and the left hand descends.

```abc
X:1
M:4/4
L:1/4
Q:1/4=60
K:C
%%score {RH LH}
V:RH clef=treble
V:LH clef=bass
[V:RH] !1!C !2!D !3!E !1!F | !2!G !3!A !4!B !1!c | !2!d !3!e !1!f !2!g | !3!a !4!b !5!c'2 |]
[V:LH] !1!C !2!B, !3!A, !1!G, | !2!F, !3!E, !4!D, !1!C, | !2!B,, !3!A,, !1!G,, !2!F,, | !3!E,, !4!D,, !5!C,,2 |]
```

The mirror makes this easier than it looks: the right hand and the left hand use the same finger number on every note, so the hands do the same thing at the same time. The two pinkies finish on C6 and C2, together.

### Arpeggios

An **arpeggio** is a chord played one note at a time. The C major arpeggio uses C, E and G. For two octaves the right hand plays C E G c e g c' with the fingers 1 2 3 1 2 3 5, and the left hand plays C E G c e g c with 5 4 2 1 4 2 1. Other fingerings are used too; this is one common choice, and a teacher may give you another.

```abc
X:1
M:4/4
L:1/4
Q:1/4=60
K:C
%%score {RH LH}
V:RH clef=treble
V:LH clef=bass
[V:RH] !1!C !2!E !3!G !1!c | !2!e !3!g !5!c'2 | !3!g !2!e !1!c !3!G | !2!E !1!C3 |]
[V:LH] !5!C, !4!E, !2!G, !1!C | !4!E !2!G !1!c2 | !2!G !4!E !1!C !2!G, | !4!E, !5!C,3 |]
```

Keep the hand quiet and the wrist level. In an arpeggio the thumb moves under the hand to the next note, and the hand moves up with it. Do not twist the wrist for the thumb.

### Tempo goals

These tempo goals are set for one note per click. Move to the next one only when you can play the scale three times in a row with no stops and even notes.

| Goal | Scales | Tempo |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | C, G and D major, two octaves, hands together | 60 bpm |
| 2 | The same, plus F and A major | 72 bpm |
| 3 | The same scales, parallel and contrary | 84 bpm |
| 4 | All twelve major scales, two octaves, hands together | 72 bpm |
| 5 | C, G, D and F major | 96 bpm |
| 6 | C, G, D and F major, in eighth notes (two notes per click) | 60 bpm |

Eighth notes at 60 bpm are two notes per second, which is the same speed as quarter notes at 120. Aim at goals 1 to 3 in this lesson; goals 4 to 6 are for the weeks after.

## Try it

Exercise 1. In the left-hand C major scale going up two octaves, which notes does the thumb play?

<details class="solution" markdown>
<summary>Answer</summary>

G3, C4, G4 and C5 (finger 1 in the sequence 5 4 3 2 1 3 2 1 4 3 2 1 3 2 1: the 5th, 8th, 12th and 15th notes).

</details>

Exercise 2. Why is contrary motion often easier than parallel motion?

<details class="solution" markdown>
<summary>Answer</summary>

The hands use the same finger on every note, so the crossings happen at the same time in both hands. In parallel motion, the thumbs cross at different places.

</details>

Exercise 3. Which notes does the right hand play in a one-octave C major arpeggio, and with which fingers?

<details class="solution" markdown>
<summary>Answer</summary>

C, E, G, C with fingers 1, 2, 3, 5.

</details>

| Minutes | What to do | Tempo | Listen for |

| 5 | Warm-up: one-octave scales in C, G and D, hands together in parallel | 72 bpm | Both thumbs arriving on the click |
| 15 | New skill: two-octave C major, parallel (5 minutes), contrary (5 minutes), arpeggio (5 minutes); hands separately first each day, then together | 60 bpm | Even notes through the thumb crossings |
| 5 | Sight reading: the first line of the [reading drill](../practice/reading-drill.md) | 50 bpm | Keep going after a wrong note |
| 5 | Guitar: C major in the five CAGED shapes, one strum each | 60 bpm | Clean strings |

## Common mistakes

Rushing the second octave. Set the metronome and keep the same tempo from the first note to the last.

Lifting the shoulders as the hand climbs. Let the arms follow the hands up the keyboard and stay loose.

Practising only the scales you like. Rotate through the keys so every scale is in reach.

## Film yourself

Record 30 seconds of a two-octave scale from the front. Check:

- Is the wrist level through the thumb crossings?
- Do the elbows follow the hands up and down the keys?
- Do the thumbs arrive on the click, in both hands?
- Are the shoulders relaxed?

## Key terms

- **Parallel motion**: both hands moving in the same direction.
- **Contrary motion**: the hands moving in opposite directions.
- **Arpeggio**: the notes of a chord played one after another.
