## The idea

### Where the melody is

In most pieces so far the melody was in the right hand. Here the left hand plays the tune: a bouncing line that starts on F and climbs through the notes of the F chord. The right hand plays short chords in the gaps. In bar 1 the left hand starts notes on eighths 1, 4, 5 and 8, and the right hand plays chords on eighths 2, 3, 6 and 7, so the hands take turns. In bar 2 the left hand runs in eighths for the first half, so the right-hand chords on eighths 2 and 3 sound with left-hand notes.

The first four bars are the model for the rest. Listen, then look at the hands.

```abc
X:1
M:4/4
L:1/8
Q:1/4=70
K:F
%%score {RH LH}
V:RH clef=treble
V:LH clef=bass
[V:RH] z | z[CFA] [CFA] z2 [FAc] [FAc]z | z[FBd] [Bd] z2 [FAc] [FAc]z | z[B,CE] [B,CE]2 z[CFA] [CFA]2 | z[CG] z[FG=B] z[EGc] [EGc]2 |]
[V:LH] !f!C, | F,3 A, C3 F, | B,D FD C3 A, | B,G, C,B, A,F, C,A, | E,2 D,2 C,2 z2 |]
```

The right-hand chords follow the harmony of the tune: an F chord in bar 1, a Bb chord in bar 2, a C-based chord in bar 3, and a cadence in bar 4 that stops on a C chord. You can think of the right hand as the strumming of a guitar and the left hand as the singer.

### The sections of the piece

| Bars | What happens |
|---|---|
| Pickup and 1 to 4 | The tune in the left hand over short chords. Bar 4 stops on a C chord |
| 5 to 8 | The same four bars again, with a different chord in bar 7 |
| 9 to 12 | The melody moves to the right hand, with accents; the left hand plays a line under it |
| 13 to 14 | A closing phrase in the left hand over chords, landing on F in bar 14 |
| 15 to 20 | Bars 9 to 14 again; bar 20 is the last bar and ends on an F chord |

In bars 9 and 10 the right hand has the tune (Bb, A, G, and then accented Bb and short chords) and the left hand plays the same line a tenth lower. In bar 11 both hands play the same notes, an octave apart. In bar 12 the right hand has three melody notes with chords between them.

### The hard spots

1. The left-hand leaps in bar 3 (and again in bars 7 and 13): the line jumps between Bb, G, C, A and F, with a drop of a fifth from G to C and a rise of a seventh from C to Bb. This is the hardest place in the piece for the left hand. Practise it alone, slowly, looking at the next key before you jump.
2. The change of job in the right hand. Bars 1 to 8 are chords; bars 9 to 12 are melody with inner notes. The accents in bars 9, 10, 15 and 16 are the sign of the change.
3. The short, light chords. They are eighth notes, and each hand must sound only when it is its turn. A late right-hand chord makes a smear.
4. Bars 14 and 20, where the left hand plays two-note chords and lands on F.

### Voicing: the tune louder than the chords

To keep the tune on top, play the left hand with more arm weight and the right-hand chords light and quick. Practise the right hand alone at piano (p, soft), and the left hand alone at forte (f), then bring them together. The chords should sound like a gentle accompaniment, and the tune should be the loudest thing you hear. The score has no pedal marks; play it without the pedal at first.

## Try it

Exercise 1. How many flats are in the key signature, and what are they?

<details class="solution" markdown>
<summary>Answer</summary>

One flat, Bb. F major has one flat.

</details>

Exercise 2. In bar 1, on which eighths of the bar does the right hand play, and on which does the left hand start a note?

<details class="solution" markdown>
<summary>Answer</summary>

The right hand plays on eighths 2, 3, 6 and 7. The left hand starts notes on eighths 1, 4, 5 and 8 (F held for three eighths, A, C held for three eighths, F).

</details>

Exercise 3. Play this left-hand line from bars 2 and 3 at 60 bpm. Which two jumps in bar 3 are the biggest?


<details class="solution" markdown>
<summary>Answer</summary>

</details>

### Practice routine (30 minutes)

| Minutes | What to do | Tempo | Listen for |
|---|---|---|---|
| 5 | Warm-up: F major scale, hands separately, then the F major triad in the right hand | 72 bpm | Even touch |
| 15 | New skill: learn the piece in sections of 2 to 4 bars: left hand alone, right hand alone, hands together. Day 1: bars 1 to 4. Day 2: bars 5 to 8. Day 3: bars 9 to 12. Day 4: bars 13 to 14. Day 5: bars 15 to 20. Then join the sections | 50 bpm, then 60 | The left hand louder than the right |
| 5 | Sight reading: the first line of the [reading drill](../practice/reading-drill.md) | 50 bpm | Keep going after a wrong note |
| 5 | Guitar: Bm and F# minor barre chords, one strum each, with rests | 60 bpm | Every string ringing |

Repeat this routine on at least five days, and move up the metronome by 10 bpm when a section is clean.

## Common mistakes

Playing the chords as loud as the tune. The tune is the point; keep the right hand light and short.

Learning the piece from the first bar every day. The hard sections are later, so start some days at bar 9 or 13.

Letting a right-hand chord arrive early, on top of a left-hand note. In bar 1 the chords fill the gaps (eighths 2, 3, 6 and 7); count "1 & 2 & 3 & 4 &" aloud and place them.

## Key terms

- **Pickup**: one or more notes before the first full bar.
- **Forte (f)**: loud.
- **Accent**: a mark that tells you to play a note louder than the others.
