# Sight-reading hands together

<span class="tag tag-reading">Reading</span>

After this lesson you can scan a short two-hand piece and play it at a steady pace on the first try, reading the left hand as chords and patterns, and looking ahead instead of stopping.

Before this: [Sight-reading basics](../02-two-hands-and-first-chords/09-sight-reading-basics.md), [Hands together](../02-two-hands-and-first-chords/03-hands-together.md), [Reading by intervals](../04-intervals-and-triads/10-reading-by-intervals.md), [Sight-reading in new keys](../05-minor-keys-and-chord-charts/12-sight-reading-in-new-keys.md). You need: your piano and a metronome.

## The idea

### Two lines at once

Reading both hands at once is harder than reading one, because your eyes must take in two staves while your hands play. The fix is not to read every note of both hands. You read the right hand as a melody, which moves by steps and skips, and you read the left hand as a pattern: a held note, a chord, or a broken chord. Once you see the pattern, the left hand takes little attention, and your eyes stay free for the next bar.

### The 15-second scan

Before you play a new piece, take 15 seconds and ask five questions.

1. What is the key signature, and so which notes are sharp or flat?
2. What is the time signature, and what is the counting?
3. What are the highest and lowest notes in each hand, and where does each hand start?
4. What is the left-hand pattern? A held note, a chord on each beat, or a broken chord?
5. Where is the hardest spot (a skip, an accidental, a change of position)?

Then choose a tempo you can keep without stopping, usually about half of the tempo you would like. Count one bar aloud, and play.

### Reading the left hand as a pattern

A block chord in the left hand looks like three notes stacked on the staff, with every note on a line or every note on a space. Read the bottom note and the shape, as you read intervals: a stack of two skips is a triad. A broken chord is the same notes spread out: read it as the chord and move your hand through it. Whole notes and half notes in the left hand are only a bass line: read the first note and its length, and look back to the right hand.

Here are three reading examples. In each, scan first and play second at 50 bpm. The first has a bass line, the second has a bass note with chords, and the third has a broken chord in the left hand.

Example 1 has a bass line under a stepwise melody in C major.

```abc
L:1/4
Q:1/4=50

%%score {RH LH}
```

```abc
[V:LH] G,, [B,D] [B,D] | G,, [B,D] [B,D] | C, [E,G,] [E,G,] | G,,3 |]
```

Example 3 is in F major (one flat, Bb). The left hand plays a broken chord in eighth notes while the right hand holds half notes.

```abc
X:1
M:4/4
L:1/8
Q:1/4=50
K:F
%%score {RH LH}
V:RH clef=treble
V:LH clef=bass
[V:RH] c4 A4 | G4 E4 | F4 A4 | F8 |]
[V:LH] F,A,CA, F,A,CA, | C,E,G,E, C,E,G,E, | F,A,CA, F,A,CA, | [F,A,C]8 |]
```

### Looking ahead and not stopping

The skill that most changes your reading is looking one beat ahead of where you are playing. Your eyes read the next notes while your fingers play the current ones. If you make a mistake, do not go back. Keep the beat and play the next correct note, because stopping teaches your hands to stop. After the piece, go back to the one bar that went wrong and fix it by itself.

Common mistake: looking at the keys on every note. Find your hand position at the start of each line, and then keep your eyes on the page. Your fingers know the distance between neighbouring keys.

### Practice routine (30 minutes)

| Minutes | What to do | Tempo | Listen for |
|---|---|---|---|
| 5 | Warm-up: C major scale, two octaves, hands together | 60 bpm | Both thumbs on the click |
| 15 | New skill: for each of the three examples above, do the 15-second scan out loud, then play once without stopping; then play it a second time with the pattern in mind. Then do the same with a new line from the [reading drill](../practice/reading-drill.md) | 50 bpm | The beat never stopping |
| 5 | Reading: choose one bar of Example 3 and read the left hand alone, naming the chord and then the notes | Untimed | Chord named before the notes |
| 5 | Guitar: C major in the A and E shapes, one strum each | 60 bpm | Clean strings |

Repeat this routine on at least three days, with new reading lines each day.

## Common mistakes

Stopping at a wrong note. Keep the beat and play the next note.

Choosing too fast a tempo. Pick a tempo at which the hardest bar is still possible.

Reading every left-hand note one at a time. See the chord or pattern first.

## Key terms

- **Scan**: looking over a piece before playing, to find the key, time, range, patterns and hard spots.
- **Block chord**: the notes of a chord played together.
- **Broken chord**: the notes of a chord played one after another.
