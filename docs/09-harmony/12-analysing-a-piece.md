# Analysing a piece

<span class="tag tag-theory">Theory</span>

After this lesson you can analyse a short piece: find its key and time signature, mark its phrases, write the Roman numeral of each chord, name the cadences and spot the non-chord tones.

Before this: [Roman numerals](01-roman-numerals.md), [The chords of a key](02-diatonic-chords.md), [Cadences](03-cadences.md), [Non-chord tones](09-non-chord-tones.md), [Minuet in G](../03-the-major-scale-and-keys/10-minuet-in-g.md). You need: your piano, a pencil and a copy of the score.

## The idea

### Why analyse

To analyse a piece is to take it apart and say how it is built. You learn it faster, because you remember chords and phrases instead of single notes. You also read the next piece in the same key more easily, since the same patterns come back. This lesson gives one method and applies it to a piece you know.

### The six steps

Do the steps in this order. Write each answer on the score in pencil.

1. Key. Read the key signature, then check the last chord. If the piece ends on a chord that sounds like home, that chord names the key. Accidentals that appear often (like the G# in A minor) show a harmonic minor.
2. Time and phrases. Read the time signature, then count the bars. Draw a line at each place where the music pauses or comes to rest. Phrases are usually 2 or 4 bars.
3. Chords. In each bar, look at the lowest note and the notes that are held or repeated. Name the chord that fits them.
4. Numerals. Write the Roman numeral of each chord in the key.
5. Cadences. Look at the last two chords of each phrase and name the cadence.
6. Non-chord tones. Mark the notes that are not in the bar's chord and name the type.

Common mistake at step 3: naming a chord from the melody alone. Look at the bass note first, then at the other notes.

### A worked example: the Minuet in G, bars 1 to 8

Here are the first 8 bars of the Minuet in G, without the ornaments. Listen first, then follow the steps.

```abc
X:1
T:Minuet in G, bars 1 to 8
C:Christian Petzold
M:3/4
L:1/8
Q:1/4=80
K:G
%%score {RH LH}
V:RH clef=treble
V:LH clef=bass
[V:RH] d2 GA Bc | d2 G2 G2 | e2 cd ef | g2 G2 G2 |
[V:LH] [G,B,D]4 A,2 | B,6 | C6 | B,6 |
[V:RH] c2 dc BA | B2 cB AG | F2 GA BG | A6 |]
[V:LH] A,6 | G,6 | D2 B,2 G,2 | D2 D,C B,A, |]
```

Step 1. The key signature has one sharp (F#), and the music ends on a G chord in the full piece, so the key is G major.

Step 2. The time signature is 3/4. The 8 bars fall into two phrases of 4 bars: bars 1 to 4 and bars 5 to 8.

Steps 3 and 4. The bass notes and chords are in this table, in G major (G, A, B, C, D, E, F#).

| Bar | Bass | Chord | Numeral |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | G | G: G, B, D | I |
| 2 | B | G: G, B, D | I |
| 3 | C | C: C, E, G | IV |
| 4 | B | G: G, B, D | I |
| 5 | A | Am: A, C, E | ii |
| 6 | G | G: G, B, D | I |
| 7 | G, B, D | G: G, B, D | I |
| 8 | D | D: D, F#, A | V |

In bars 2 and 4 the bass is B, not G, so the chord is G with B in the bass (G/B, an inversion, as in stage 4). It is still the chord I. The progression is I, I, IV, I, ii, I, I, V.

Step 5. Bar 8 ends the second phrase on V, so it is a half cadence (I to V, from bar 7 to bar 8). Nothing in the first phrase closes it fully: it ends on I with the bass on B, not on the root.

Step 6. Some melody notes are not in the bar's chord. In bar 1, over G (G, B, D), the A is a passing tone between G and B, and the C is a passing tone between B and the D of bar 2. In bar 5, over Am (A, C, E), the D between two Cs is an upper neighbour tone, and the B between C and A is a passing tone. In bar 7, over G, the F# between two Gs is a lower neighbour tone.

The piece comes home at the end of the second half with D in bar 15 and G in bar 16, which is a perfect cadence (V to I). You can check this in the full score in the Minuet lesson.

## Try it

Exercise 1. Analyse this original piece, "Evening Steps", in A minor. Cover the answer, press play, and work through the six steps. The score has no chord symbols.

```abc
X:1
T:Evening Steps
C:Written for this book
M:4/4
L:1/4
Q:1/4=66
K:Am
%%score {RH LH}
V:RH clef=treble
V:LH clef=bass
[V:RH] A c e c | f e d2 | c B A2 | ^G B e2 |
[V:LH] A,,2 [C,E,]2 | D,2 [F,A,]2 | A,,2 [C,E,]2 | E,2 [^G,B,]2 |
[V:RH] c A F A | d e f d | ^g a ^g e | c2 A2 |]
[V:LH] F,,2 [A,,C,]2 | D,2 [F,A,]2 | E,2 [^G,B,D]2 | A,,2 [C,E,]2 |]
```

<details class="solution" markdown>
<summary>Answer</summary>

Key: A minor. There are no sharps or flats in the key signature, the piece ends on Am, and the G# in bars 4 and 7 is the raised 7th of the harmonic minor. Time: 4/4. Phrases: bars 1 to 4 and bars 5 to 8.

Chords: bar 1 Am (i), bar 2 Dm (iv), bar 3 Am (i), bar 4 E (V), bar 5 F (VI), bar 6 Dm (iv), bar 7 E7 (V7), bar 8 Am (i). The notes of each chord are Am: A, C, E. Dm: D, F, A. E: E, G#, B. F: F, A, C. E7: E, G#, B, D.

Cadences: bar 3 to bar 4 ends the first phrase on V, a half cadence. Bar 7 to bar 8 is V7 to i in root position, with the melody ending on A, a perfect cadence.

Non-chord tones: bar 2, the E is a passing tone between F and D. Bar 3, the B is a passing tone between C and A. Bar 6, the E is a passing tone between D and F. Bar 7, the A is an upper neighbour tone between two G#s.

</details>

### Practice routine (30 minutes)

| Minutes | What to do | Tempo | Listen for |
|---|---|---|---|
| 5 | Warm-up: A harmonic minor scale and the A minor triad, hands separately | 60 bpm | The raised G# |
| 15 | New skill: play "Evening Steps" hands together, then play it with chord roots only in the left hand and say each numeral aloud | 50 bpm, then 66 | The chords changing on beat 1 |
| 5 | Ear training: play the half cadence and the perfect cadence at the end of each phrase of "Evening Steps", and say which is open and which is finished | Untimed | Open against finished |
| 5 | Guitar: play the chords of "Evening Steps" (Am, Dm, Am, E, F, Dm, E7, Am), four strums per bar | 60 bpm | The same pull toward Am |

Repeat this routine on at least three days.

## Common mistakes

Finding the key from the first chord only. Many pieces start away from home. Check the key signature and the last chord.

Naming a chord from the melody notes. The melody has non-chord tones; the bass and the held notes show the chord.

Writing numerals without checking the key. In A minor, the chord on F is VI, and in C major it is IV.

## Key terms

- **Analysis**: taking a piece apart to say its key, phrases, chords, cadences and non-chord tones.
