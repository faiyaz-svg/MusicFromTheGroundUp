# Sight-reading classical pieces

<span class="tag tag-reading">Reading</span>

After this lesson you can scan a classical piece in under a minute, spot Alberti bass patterns and scale runs as patterns instead of single notes, and read short classical-style pieces on piano and guitar at a steady beat.

Before this: [Sight-reading basics](../02-two-hands-and-first-chords/09-sight-reading-basics.md), [Sight-reading in new keys](../05-minor-keys-and-chord-charts/12-sight-reading-in-new-keys.md), [Alberti bass and waltz bass](../07-pedal-texture-and-ambient/04-alberti-and-waltz-bass.md), [Sight-reading rhythms](../06-meter-terms-and-rhythm/11-sight-reading-rhythms.md). You need: your piano, your guitar and a metronome.

## The idea

### Classical music is made of patterns

The pieces in this stage repeat the same shapes again and again: a scale going up, a chord broken into notes, the same bar with one note changed. A reader who sees each note alone reads slowly. A reader who sees the shape reads a whole bar at once. This lesson names the common shapes so you can spot them.

| Pattern | What it looks like | Where you saw it |
|---|---|---|
| Scale run | Notes next to each other going up or down, eight in a row | Clementi, bars 8 and 12 |
| Broken chord | A chord's notes in order, up or down, such as C, E, G | Clementi, bar 1 |
| Alberti bass | The left hand plays low, high, middle, high on one chord | Left hands in many sonatinas |
| Held bass and run | One long note under a run | Carcassi, bars 1 to 8 |
| Repeated bar | The same bar again, sometimes one octave higher | Giuliani Study 3, bars 9 to 15 |

### A one-minute scan

Before you play a classical piece, spend a minute on these steps.

1. Find the key signature, then the time signature.
2. Find the tempo word and the dynamics.
3. Mark the repeats and the endings.
4. Find the hard bar: the one with the most accidentals, the biggest leap or the fastest notes.
5. Circle every sharp, flat and natural sign.
6. Name the first and last note of each run, so you know where it starts and stops.

Then play it at a tempo where you can keep going, and do not stop for wrong notes. Common mistake at step 4: skipping the scan and finding the hard bar when you are in it.

### Reading a left-hand pattern

In an Alberti bass, read the chord once and then the pattern: low, high, middle, high, again. If the chord stays for a bar, the left hand has nothing new to read until the chord changes. Write the chord names above the bars in pencil, and read those.

### Reading a scale run

Name the first and last note and the direction. Check the key signature for sharps or flats on the way, and play the middle notes by step. Do not read the middle notes one by one.

## Hear and see

Example 1: piano, Alberti bass in C. The left hand plays low, high, middle, high, and the right hand plays long notes. The chords are C, F, G and C. Read the chord, then the pattern.

```abc
X:1
M:4/4
L:1/8
Q:1/4=70
K:C
%%score {RH LH}
V:RH clef=treble
V:LH clef=bass
V:RH
E4 G4 | A4 F4 | G4 B4 | c8 |]
V:LH
C,G,E,G, C,G,E,G, | F,CA,C F,CA,C | G,DB,D G,DB,D | [C,E,G,]8 |]
```

Example 2: piano, scale runs. Bar 1 goes up the scale, bar 3 comes down it. Name the first and last notes before you play.

```abc
X:1
M:4/4
L:1/8
Q:1/4=70
K:C
%%score {RH LH}
V:RH clef=treble
V:LH clef=bass
V:RH
C D E F G A B c | d4 G4 | c B A G F E D C | C8 |]
V:LH
C,8 | G,,8 | C,8 | [C,,C,]8 |]
```

Example 3: guitar, the same shape on four chords. The bar is the same pattern each time: the bass, then the notes above it. Read the first bar, then look for what changes.

```abc
%%opts tab=guitar sound=nylon
X:1
M:4/4
L:1/8
Q:1/4=60
K:C clef=treble-8
"C"C G c e c e c G | "Am"A, E A c A c A E | "Dm"D A d f d f d A | "G"G, D G B G B G D |]
```

## Try it

Exercise 1. In Example 2, what are the first and last notes of the run in bar 1, and the first and last notes of the run in bar 3?

<details class="solution" markdown>
<summary>Answer</summary>

Bar 1 runs up from C to the C an octave higher. Bar 3 runs down from that C back to the C below.

</details>

Exercise 2. In Example 3, how does the bass change from bar to bar?

<details class="solution" markdown>
<summary>Answer</summary>

The bass notes are C, A, D and G, one for each chord, and the pattern above them stays the same.

</details>

Exercise 3. In Example 1, what do the three notes in the left hand of the first bar spell?

<details class="solution" markdown>
<summary>Answer</summary>

C major: C, E, G.

</details>

### Practice routine (30 minutes)

| Minutes | What to do | Tempo | Listen for |
|---|---|---|---|
| 5 | Warm-up: C major scale hands together, then A minor scale hands together | 60 bpm | Even notes |
| 10 | Piano reading: Examples 1 and 2, one minute of scanning each, then play without stopping | 60 bpm, then 70 | Keeping the beat when you slip |
| 10 | Guitar reading: Example 3, then read the next four unseen bars of any piece in this stage | 60 bpm | Reading the pattern, not the notes |
| 5 | The scan drill: take any piece from this stage, set a timer for one minute, scan it, and say its key, time, tempo word and hard bar | Untimed | Finding the hard bar first |

Repeat this routine on at least three days, with a new piece for the scan drill each day.

</div>

- [ ] You can scan a piece in under one minute and say its key, time signature, tempo word, repeats and hard bar.

## Common mistakes

Reading every note. A run is a first note, a last note and a direction, so read it as one shape.

Stopping for a wrong note. Keep the beat and come back to the bar on the next pass.

Skipping the scan. Most mistakes come from a sharp or a repeat you did not see before you started.

## Key terms

- **Pattern**: a shape of notes that repeats, such as a scale run or a broken chord.
- **Scale run**: a quick series of notes that moves by step in one direction.
- **Broken chord**: the notes of a chord played one after another.
