# Pedal points and ostinatos

<span class="tag tag-piano">Piano</span>

After this lesson you can play a pedal point and an ostinato on the piano, say how each builds tension or colour, and write the chord names for chords over a held bass note.

Before this: [Cadences](../09-harmony/03-cadences.md), [Inversions](../04-intervals-and-triads/07-inversions.md), [Sus and add9 chords](../07-pedal-texture-and-ambient/06-sus-and-add9-chords.md), [Aeolian loops](04-aeolian-loops.md). You need: your piano.

## The idea

### A bass note that does not move

Hold a low G with your left hand. With your right hand play C, then F, then Dm, then G7, over the same low G. The first three chords do not fit the G in the usual way, and the tension grows. When the G7 arrives, the G finally belongs, and the music is ready to go home to C.

A **pedal point** is a note, usually in the bass, that is held or repeated while the chords above it change. The note is the **pedal** (the word comes from the organ, where the foot holds a bass note down). It has nothing to do with the damper pedal under your right foot, though you may use that foot too.

When the pedal is the tonic (C in C major), it is a tonic pedal, and it calms the harmony. When the pedal is the dominant (G in C major), it is a dominant pedal, and it builds tension that is released when the music goes to the tonic.

### Chord names over a pedal

A slash chord (stage 4) names the chord on top and the bass note after the slash. C/G is a C chord over a G bass. Over a pedal the chords often have odd names, because the bass note is not the root.

| Chord | Notes, bass first | Right hand over the G |
|---|---|---|
| C/G | G, C, E | C, E, G |
| F/G | G, F, A, C | F, A, C |
| Dm/G | G, D, F, A | D, F, A |
| G7 | G, B, D, F | B, D, F |

G7: G, B, D, F. By the fourth bar the right hand has B, D and F over the G, which is a plain G7, the tension chord that pulls to C.

```abc
X:1
M:4/4
L:1/4
Q:1/4=60
K:C
%%score {RH LH}
V:RH clef=treble
V:LH clef=bass
[V:RH] !p!"C/G"[ceg]4 | "F/G"[cfa]4 | !mf!"Dm/G"[dfa]4 | !f!"G7"[dfb]4 | !mf!"C"[ceg]4 |]
[V:LH] [G,,G,]4- | [G,,G,]4- | [G,,G,]4- | [G,,G,]4 | [C,,C,]4 |]
```

In the example the tie keeps the G in the left hand for four bars. Bars 1 to 3 sound away from home, bar 4 sounds ready to go home, and bar 5 is home. Play the left hand loud enough that the G stays in your ear, then add a crescendo through bars 2 to 4.

### A pattern that does not change

An **ostinato** is a short pattern, rhythmic, melodic or both, that repeats many times while other parts change. The word is Italian for "stubborn". You already know some ostinatos: the broken-chord left hand of stage 5 repeats the same shape and the chord under it changes.

In the example below the right hand repeats the same four-note figure, E C A C, in every bar. The left hand moves under it with chords Am, F, C and G. The ostinato notes stay the same, and each bass note gives them a different meaning. Over Am they make the chord. Over F they make Fmaj7 (F, A, C, E). Over C the A makes it C6. Over G they have no B, so the C hangs over the G like the sus4 chords from stage 7.

```abc
X:1
M:4/4
L:1/8
Q:1/4=60
K:C
%%score {RH LH}
V:RH clef=treble
V:LH clef=bass
[V:RH] !p!"Am"e c A c e c A c | "F"e c A c e c A c | "C"e c A c e c A c | "G"e c A c e c A c |]
[V:LH] [A,,E,]8 | [F,,C,]8 | [C,,G,,]8 | [G,,D,]8 |]
```

### Pedal point or ostinato

A pedal point stays on one bass note and the harmony above changes. An ostinato stays on one pattern and the harmony underneath may change. A piece can use both at once: a dominant pedal in the bass with a repeated figure above it, and a crescendo, which is a common way to build tension before a big theme.

## Try it

Exercise 1. Which is a pedal point and which is an ostinato: (a) G held in the bass for four bars while chords change above, (b) a four-note right-hand figure repeated in every bar?

<details class="solution" markdown>
<summary>Answer</summary>

(a) is a pedal point. (b) is an ostinato.

</details>

Exercise 2. Build a dominant pedal in G major. Which bass note do you hold?

<details class="solution" markdown>
<summary>Answer</summary>

D, the 5th note of the G major scale: G, A, B, C, D, E, F#, G. The pedal ends when the music goes to a G chord.

</details>

Exercise 3. In the ostinato example, which bar has the chord Fmaj7, and which notes of the right-hand figure are in it?

<details class="solution" markdown>
<summary>Answer</summary>

Bar 2. Fmaj7: F, A, C, E. The figure's notes E, C and A are all in the chord.

</details>

## Common mistakes

Letting the held note die. The bass note must keep sounding. Play it firmly, or use the damper pedal, as in stage 7, and change the pedal only when the harmony above is clear.

Playing the ostinato louder than the changing part. The pattern is a background. Keep it even and quiet.

Confusing a pedal point with the damper pedal. A pedal point is a note in the music, written with notes. The damper pedal is a foot action, written as Ped.

## Key terms

- **Pedal point**: a note held or repeated, usually in the bass, while the chords above change.
- **Tonic pedal**: a pedal point on the tonic, which calms the harmony.
- **Dominant pedal**: a pedal point on the dominant, which builds tension that releases when the music goes to the tonic.
- **Ostinato**: a short pattern repeated many times while other parts change.
