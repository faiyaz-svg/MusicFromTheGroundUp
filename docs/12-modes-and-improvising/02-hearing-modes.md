# Hearing the modes

<span class="tag tag-ear">Ear</span>

After this lesson you can hear the characteristic note of Dorian, Phrygian, Lydian, Mixolydian and Aeolian over a drone, and tell which mode a short melody is in, at least 5 times out of 6.

Before this: [The modes](01-modes.md), [Singing the scale by number](../03-the-major-scale-and-keys/04-singing-the-scale-by-number.md), [Hearing major and minor](../05-minor-keys-and-chord-charts/08-hearing-major-and-minor.md). You need: your piano, and headphones if you can.

## The idea

### One note against the drone

In lesson 1 you saw each mode as a pattern of steps. Your ear does not count steps. It hears one note against the home note, and that note's distance from home gives the mode its colour. With C as the drone, the characteristic notes are these.

| Mode on C | Characteristic note | Distance from the drone | What it sounds like |
|---|---|---|---|
| Dorian | A (natural 6) | C up to A is a major 6th | A lift inside a minor sound |
| Phrygian | Db (b2) | C up to Db is a minor 2nd | A tight rub that wants to fall back to C |
| Lydian | F# (#4) | C up to F# is an augmented 4th | Floating, leaning up toward G |
| Mixolydian | Bb (b7) | C up to Bb is a minor 7th | Open and relaxed, with no pull to finish |
| Aeolian | Ab (b6) | C up to Ab is a minor 6th | Heavy, sinking toward G |

Ionian has no altered note. It is the reference: if none of these colours is there, and the 3rd is major, you are hearing major.

### Three questions in order

Use these questions on any short melody over a drone. They sort the seven modes into the one you hear.

1. Where is home? Hum along with the drone until one note feels like rest. Everything else is measured from it.
2. Is the 3rd major or minor? Compare it with the major and minor 3rds you learned in stage 4. This splits the modes in two: major 3rd means Ionian, Lydian or Mixolydian, and minor 3rd means Dorian, Phrygian or Aeolian (Locrian too, but it is rarely used as a home).
3. Listen for the one altered note. In the major group, a raised 4th means Lydian and a lowered 7th means Mixolydian. In the minor group, a natural 6 means Dorian, a lowered 2nd means Phrygian, and a lowered 6th with no other change means Aeolian.

Common mistake at step 3: waiting for the whole scale. The altered note usually appears in the first two bars of a melody, because a good tune uses it early. Listen for the note that sounds like a colour, not a step.

### Pairs help

The hardest pair is Dorian and Aeolian, because both have a minor 3rd. Play them back to back and listen only for the 6th: Dorian's A sounds brighter and Aeolian's Ab sounds darker. The next pair is major and Lydian: the F# in Lydian sounds like a note that wants to go up, and the F natural in major does not.

## Try it

Exercise 1: the self-test. Cover the notation with a card so you listen without reading. Play the six examples above in this order: Example 4, Example 2, Example 6, Example 1, Example 5, Example 3. Listen to each twice, then write down the mode you hear for items 1 to 6 before you open the answers.

<details class="solution" markdown>
<summary>Answers</summary>

Item 1: Mixolydian (b7, Bb). Item 2: Phrygian (b2, Db). Item 3: Ionian (no altered note, major 3rd). Item 4: Dorian (natural 6, A). Item 5: Aeolian (b6, Ab). Item 6: Lydian (#4, F#).

</details>

Score yourself out of 6. Repeat with the examples in a new order on a different day, because the second time you would otherwise remember the order rather than the sound. The [Practice tools](../practice/index.md) page has more ear quizzes.

Exercise 2. Play C and Ab, then C and A, one after the other. Which interval is wider, and which mode does each belong to in this lesson?

<details class="solution" markdown>
<summary>Answer</summary>

C up to Ab is a minor 6th (8 half steps), the Aeolian colour. C up to A is a major 6th (9 half steps), the Dorian colour.

</details>

## Common mistakes

Listening for the whole scale. Listen for one note against the drone instead, because it shows up in the first bars.

Guessing from mood alone. Moods are a tendency (Phrygian is often dark, Lydian often bright), and a slow Lydian tune can sound sad. Confirm with the 3rd and the altered note.

Forgetting the drone. Without a held home note, the ear picks a different home and the answers change. Keep the left hand down.

## Key terms

- **Drone**: a held note or open fifth that tells the ear where home is.
- **Altered note**: a scale degree a half step above or below its place in the major scale, such as the #4 or b7.
