# Improvise over film progressions

<span class="tag tag-improvise">Improvising</span>

After this lesson you can improvise with the A minor pentatonic scale over two film-style chord loops (Am F G and Am F C G), on guitar or piano, landing on a chord note when the chord changes.

Before this: [The five pentatonic boxes](09-pentatonic-boxes.md), [The minor pentatonic scale](08-minor-pentatonic.md), [Improvise in Dorian](05-improvise-in-dorian.md), [Chord symbols and chord charts](../05-minor-keys-and-chord-charts/04-chord-symbols-and-chord-charts.md). You need: your guitar or your piano (or both), and a way to record yourself.

## The idea

### Two loops

Film, game and anime music often repeats a short chord loop in a minor key. The loops here have four bars, one chord per bar. They are described in [Aeolian loops](../11-film-game-and-anime-harmony/04-aeolian-loops.md), which names the chords with Roman numerals. In A minor, the chords are these.

| Loop | Chords | Numerals |
|---|---|---|
| A | Am, F, G, G | i, VI, VII, VII |
| B | Am, F, C, G | i, VI, III, VII |

The chord spellings: Am: A, C, E. F: F, A, C. G: G, B, D. C: C, E, G. All the chords come from the A natural minor scale.

### The notes allowed

The A minor pentatonic scale: A, C, D, E, G. These five notes are in the A natural minor scale, so they fit every chord in both loops. On guitar, play box 1 (frets 5 to 8) and the box next to it, box 2, if you want more room. On piano, play A, C, D, E and G in any octave between A3 and A5.

Not every note is equally restful over every chord. The table shows which notes of the scale belong to each chord. Those are the best notes to land on when a chord changes.

| Chord | Scale notes that are in the chord |
|---|---|
| Am | A, C, E |
| F | A, C |
| G | G, D |
| C | C, E, G |

The scale has no F and no B, so the F chord's root and the G chord's third are never played. That is fine: the chords supply them. What you play is the colour on top.

### Three rules for the first try

1. Use only A, C, D, E and G, and begin and end on A.
2. On the first beat of each new bar, play a note that belongs to that chord (see the table).
3. Say each idea twice with a rest between, and change the ending the second time.

### Steps for the first try

1. Play Loop A (below) and listen to the chord changes. Hum A as the home note.
2. Play only the chord notes from the table, one per bar, on the first beat.
3. Add one more scale note per bar, between the chord notes.
4. Play a motif of three notes in bar 1 and move it to each new chord in the next bars.
5. Switch to Loop B and repeat.

Common mistake at step 2: playing the chord note late. Place it on the first beat when the chord changes, and it will sound right. The ear checks the first beat of each new bar.

## Hear and see

Loop A is four bars of Am, F, G and G. The right hand holds the triad and the left hand plays the root and the fifth. Loop it with the play bar.

```abc
X:1
M:4/4
L:1/4
Q:1/4=72
K:Am
%%score {RH LH}
V:RH clef=treble
V:LH clef=bass
[V:RH] "Am"[A c e]4 | "F"[A c f]4 | "G"[B d g]4 | "G"[B d g]4 |]
[V:LH] [A,,E,]4 | [F,,C,]4 | [G,,D,]4 | [G,,D,]4 |]
```

Loop B is four bars of Am, F, C and G.

```abc
X:1
M:4/4
L:1/4
Q:1/4=72
K:Am
%%score {RH LH}
V:RH clef=treble
V:LH clef=bass
[V:RH] "Am"[A c e]4 | "F"[A c f]4 | "C"[G c e]4 | "G"[G B d]4 |]
[V:LH] [A,,E,]4 | [F,,C,]4 | [C,,G,,]4 | [G,,D,]4 |]
```

Here is an example improvisation over Loop B, played twice, as guitar notes in box 1 and box 2. The chord names are above the staff. Bars 1 to 4 state the idea, bars 5 to 8 move it up, and bar 9 comes home to A. Each bar starts on a chord note: A, C or E over Am and F, then E over C, then D over G.

```abc
%%opts tab=guitar
X:1
M:4/4
L:1/8
Q:1/4=72
K:Am clef=treble-8
"Am"A, C E2 D C A,2 | "F"C2 A, C D2 C A, | "C"E2 G A2 G E2 | "G"D2 E G2 E D2 |
"Am"c2 A E c2 A2 | "F"A2 c d2 c A2 | "C"e2 g e c2 e2 | "G"d3 c A2 G2 |
"Am"A,8 |]
```

On piano, use the same note names with your right hand anywhere between A3 and A5. The guitar staff is written an octave above the sound, so the letters match but the pitches sit an octave lower on the guitar.

Record yourself for one minute over a loop. Listen for the first beat of each bar, and ask whether you landed on a chord note.

## Try it

Exercise 1. Which notes of the A minor pentatonic scale are in the C chord?

<details class="solution" markdown>
<summary>Answer</summary>

C, E and G. The C chord is C, E, G, and all three are in the scale.

</details>

Exercise 2. Over the F chord in Loop A, which two scale notes are chord notes?

<details class="solution" markdown>
<summary>Answer</summary>

A and C. The F chord is F, A, C, and the scale has no F.

</details>

Exercise 3. Make up a one-bar idea for the G bar of Loop A that starts on a chord note, using only the five scale notes.

<details class="solution" markdown>
<summary>Answer</summary>

Many answers work. One: D, E, G, E. It starts on D, a note of the G chord (G, B, D), and uses only scale notes.

</details>

### Practice routine (30 minutes)

| Minutes | What to do | Tempo | Listen for |
|---|---|---|---|
| 5 | Warm-up: box 1 up and down on guitar, or A minor pentatonic up and down on piano | 66 bpm | Even notes |
| 15 | New skill: improvise over Loop A for 5 minutes, then Loop B for 5 minutes, then play the example and change two bars | 72 bpm, then 66 | A chord note on the first beat of each bar |
| 5 | Ear: play lesson 2 Examples 1 and 5 and name the modes | Untimed | The 6th |
| 5 | Other instrument: play the loop's chords (guitar strums or piano triads) | 72 bpm | A steady beat |

Repeat this routine on at least three days before you check the boxes below. Improvise something new each time.

### Move on when

- [ ] You can improvise 8 bars over Loop A with only the five notes, starting and ending on A, with no stops.
- [ ] You can improvise 8 bars over Loop B and play a chord note on the first beat of at least 6 of the 8 bars.
- [ ] You can record one minute over a loop and point to one idea that came back.
- [ ] You can name the notes of the A minor pentatonic scale that belong to each chord of Loop B.

## Common mistakes

Playing the same note on every chord. Move the idea to the chord, or use the table, so the note changes with the chord.

Running out of ideas. Say less. One idea played twice, with a rest, fills four bars.

Speeding up. The loop is the clock, so keep with it, and slow it with the tempo slider if you need to.

## Key terms

- **Chord loop**: a short chord progression that repeats, such as four bars of Am, F, C and G.
- **Chord note**: a note that belongs to the chord that is playing.
