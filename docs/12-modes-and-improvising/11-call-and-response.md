# Call and response

<span class="tag tag-improvise">Improvising</span>

After this lesson you can play a short question phrase and an answer phrase over a chord loop, with the question ending away from home and the answer ending on A.

Before this: [Improvise over film progressions](10-improvise-over-film-progressions.md), [Improvise in Dorian](05-improvise-in-dorian.md), [The five pentatonic boxes](09-pentatonic-boxes.md). You need: your piano or your guitar, and a way to record yourself.

## The idea

### Questions and answers

When you talk, a question rises or hangs, and an answer settles. Music does the same. A **call** (also called a question phrase) is a short phrase that stops on a note that feels unfinished. A **response** (an answer phrase) answers it and stops on a note that feels finished. Together they make a **call and response**. The home note, A in this lesson, is the finished sound, so the call ends on any other note and the response ends on A.

This is the easiest way to give an improvisation a shape. You do not need new ideas: the response can start the same way as the call and change only the ending.

### The notes allowed

The A minor pentatonic scale again: A, C, D, E, G. On guitar, use boxes 1 and 2. On piano, use any octave between A3 and A5.

### The backing loop

Four bars at 72 bpm over Am, F, G and Am. Bars 1 and 2 are the call, and bars 3 and 4 are the response. In the first loop the book plays a call for you and leaves the answer to you.

```abc
X:1
M:4/4
L:1/4
Q:1/4=72
K:Am
%%score {RH LH}
V:RH clef=treble
V:LH clef=bass
[V:RH] "Am"z E A c | "F"c2 z2 | "G"z4 | "Am"z4 |]
[V:LH] [A,,E,]4 | [F,,C,]4 | [G,,D,]4 | [A,,E,]4 |]
```

The call ends on C, which is not A, so it sounds unfinished. In the second loop, the book plays an eight-bar version with two calls (bars 1 and 2, and bars 5 and 6) and leaves the answers (bars 3 and 4, and bars 7 and 8) empty.

```abc
X:1
M:4/4
L:1/4
Q:1/4=72
K:Am
%%score {RH LH}
V:RH clef=treble
V:LH clef=bass
[V:RH] "Am"z E A c | "F"c2 z2 | "G"z4 | "Am"z4 |
[V:LH] [A,,E,]4 | [F,,C,]4 | [G,,D,]4 | [A,,E,]4 |
[V:RH] "Am"z c e g | "F"e2 z2 | "G"z4 | "Am"z4 |]
[V:LH] [A,,E,]4 | [F,,C,]4 | [G,,D,]4 | [A,,E,]4 |]
```

### Three rules for the first try

1. Use only A, C, D, E and G.
2. End the call on a note that is not A (C, D, E or G), and end the answer on A.
3. Start the answer with the same first two or three notes as the call, then change the rest.

### Steps for the first try

1. Play the first loop and listen to the book's call. Hum a possible answer.
2. Play only the last note of your answer, an A, in bar 4. Hold it.
3. Add two or three notes before it, starting like the call.
4. Put a rest at the end of the call, as the book does, so the question hangs.
5. Switch to the second loop and answer both calls, with different answers.
6. Then turn the loop off, or use the chord loop from lesson 10, and play both the call and the response yourself.

Common mistake at step 3: ending the answer on a note that is not A. Test it by singing: if the phrase sounds like it could keep going, the last note is not home.

## Hear and see

Here is the first loop with one possible answer written out. The answer starts like the call (a rest, then E and A) and then falls to the A that finishes it.

```abc

M:4/4
L:1/4
Q:1/4=72
K:Am
%%score {RH LH}
V:RH clef=treble
V:LH clef=bass

```

On guitar, the same call and answer in box 1 look like this. The call ends on C at fret 5 of string 3 and the answer ends on A at fret 7 of string 4. The fretboard figure from lesson 9 shows the positions.

Record yourself playing the second loop with your own answers. Listen for whether each answer sounds like it finishes.

## Try it

Exercise 1. In the example, which note ends the call, and which note ends the answer?

<details class="solution" markdown>
<summary>Answer</summary>

The call ends on C (bar 2). The answer ends on A (bar 4).

</details>

Exercise 2. Why does ending on A sound finished?

<details class="solution" markdown>
<summary>Answer</summary>

A is the home note of A minor, the note the ear hears as the place to rest. Every other note leans toward it.

</details>

Exercise 3. Write a call of four notes that ends on E, using only the five scale notes.

<details class="solution" markdown>
<summary>Answer</summary>

Many answers work. One: A, C, D, E. It uses only scale notes and stops on E, which is not home.

</details>

## Common mistakes

Ending both phrases on A. Then the call has no question in it. Pick another note for the call.

Answering with unrelated notes. Start like the call, so the ear hears it as an answer.

Rushing the rest. The rest at the end of the call is the pause before the answer. Count it.

## Key terms

- **Call** (question phrase): a short phrase that ends on a note that feels unfinished.
- **Response** (answer phrase): a phrase that answers a call and ends on the home note.
- **Call and response**: a call followed by a response.
