# Variations on a theme

<span class="tag tag-improvise">Improvising</span>

After this lesson you can play a four-bar theme and five variations of it (rhythm, register, mode, ornament and harmony), and make a variation of your own.

Before this: [Call and response](11-call-and-response.md), [Improvise in Dorian](05-improvise-in-dorian.md), [Improvise over film progressions](10-improvise-over-film-progressions.md), [The modes](01-modes.md). You need: your piano (or your guitar for the melody), and a way to record yourself.

## The idea

### Change one thing

A **theme** is a short melody you can recognize. A **variation** is the theme played again with one thing changed, so the listener hears both what stayed and what is new. Variations are a way to improvise without inventing from nothing: you start from a tune you know and change it. Change one thing at a time. If you change everything, the theme disappears.

Five things are easy to change.

1. Rhythm: keep the notes, change how long they last.
2. Register: play the melody higher or lower.
3. Mode: change one or two notes to move to a different mode.
4. Ornament: add small notes around the main ones.
5. Harmony: keep the melody, change the chords under it.

### The theme

Lamplight is an original theme for this book: four bars in A minor, with the chords Am, G, F and Am in the left hand. The melody falls from E to A in steps. Learn it first, and play it over the loop.

```abc
X:1
T:Lamplight (theme)
C:Original, for this book
M:4/4
L:1/4
Q:1/4=72
K:Am

[V:LH] [A,,E,]4 | [G,,D,]4 | [F,,C,]4 | [A,,E,]4 |]
```

### Five variations

Variation 1 changes the rhythm. The notes are the same (bar 1 repeats the C), but each bar uses a dotted rhythm, a long note and a short one. Listen for the lilt.

```abc
X:1
M:4/4
L:1/4
Q:1/4=72
K:Am
%%score {RH LH}
V:RH clef=treble
V:LH clef=bass
[V:RH] "Am"e3/ c/ c2 | "G"d3/ c/ B2 | "F"c3/ A/ F3/ E/ | "Am"A4 |]
[V:LH] [A,,E,]4 | [G,,D,]4 | [F,,C,]4 | [A,,E,]4 |]
```

Variation 2 changes the register. The melody is an octave higher and the left hand stays where it is, so the gap between the hands is wider and the sound is lighter.

```abc
X:1
M:4/4
L:1/4
Q:1/4=72
K:Am
%%score {RH LH}
V:RH clef=treble
V:LH clef=bass
[V:RH] "Am"e'2 c'2 | "G"d' c' b2 | "F"c' a f e | "Am"a4 |]
[V:LH] [A,,E,]4 | [G,,D,]4 | [F,,C,]4 | [A,,E,]4 |]
```

Variation 3 changes the mode. The F becomes F#, which turns A Aeolian into A Dorian, and the F chord becomes a D chord (D, F#, A). The natural 6 (F#) is the Dorian colour from lesson 5.

```abc
X:1
M:4/4
L:1/4
Q:1/4=72
K:Am
%%score {RH LH}
V:RH clef=treble
V:LH clef=bass
[V:RH] "Am"e2 c2 | "G"d c B2 | "D"c A ^F E | "Am"A4 |]
[V:LH] [A,,E,]4 | [G,,D,]4 | [D,,A,,]4 | [A,,E,]4 |]
```

Variation 4 adds ornaments. The small notes before the main notes are grace notes: quick notes played right before the main note. Keep them light.

```abc
X:1
M:4/4
L:1/4
Q:1/4=72
K:Am
%%score {RH LH}
V:RH clef=treble
V:LH clef=bass
[V:RH] "Am"{f}e2 c2 | "G"d c {c}B2 | "F"c A {G}F E | "Am"{B}A4 |]
[V:LH] [A,,E,]4 | [G,,D,]4 | [F,,C,]4 | [A,,E,]4 |]
```

Variation 5 changes the harmony. The melody stays the same, and the chords become C, Em, Dm and Am (C: C, E, G; Em: E, G, B; Dm: D, F, A). The tune now sounds as if it leans toward C major.

```abc
X:1
M:4/4
L:1/4
Q:1/4=72
K:Am
%%score {RH LH}
V:RH clef=treble
V:LH clef=bass
[V:RH] "C"e2 c2 | "Em"d c B2 | "Dm"c A F E | "Am"A4 |]
[V:LH] [C,,G,,]4 | [E,,B,,]4 | [D,,A,,]4 | [A,,E,]4 |]
```

### Notes allowed and rules for your first variation

Notes allowed: the theme's notes (E, C, D, B, A, F) and the notes of A minor, plus F# if you try the mode change. Three rules:

1. Keep the theme's first note (E) and last note (A).
2. Change only one of the five things.
3. Play the plain theme once, then your variation, and see whether you can hear both.

### Steps

1. Play the theme over the loop until it needs no thought.
2. Pick one thing, such as rhythm, and play Variation 1 from the page.
3. Then change your own: make a new rhythm for bar 1 only.
4. Do the same for the other four things on other days.
5. Combine two once each is comfortable, such as high register with ornaments.

Common mistake at step 3: changing the notes along with the rhythm. If the melody becomes a new tune, go back and keep the notes.

## Hear and see

Play Variations 1 and 3 one after the other and listen to what each one changes. Variation 1 keeps every note and moves the beats. Variation 3 keeps the rhythm and changes the colour. Then play the theme and Variation 5 and listen to the same melody over two sets of chords.

Record your own variation and play the theme before it. Ask the listener (or yourself the next day) which thing you changed.

## Try it

Exercise 1. In Variation 3, which chord replaces F, and why?

<details class="solution" markdown>
<summary>Answer</summary>

D, which is D, F#, A. The melody note F became F#, and D is the chord that contains it.

</details>

Exercise 2. Which of the five changes is Variation 2?

<details class="solution" markdown>
<summary>Answer</summary>

Register: the same notes, an octave higher.

</details>

Exercise 3. Make a rhythm variation of bar 1 only: keep E and C, and change how long each lasts.

<details class="solution" markdown>
<summary>Answer</summary>

Many answers work. One: E for 3 beats and C for 1 beat. Another: two quick notes, E and E, then C for 2 beats.

</details>

### Practice routine (30 minutes)

| Minutes | What to do | Tempo | Listen for |
|---|---|---|---|
| 5 | Warm-up: the theme, hands together | 72 bpm | A clear, even melody |
| 15 | New skill: Variations 1 to 5, one at a time, then make your own variation of one thing and play it after the theme | 72 bpm, then 66 | What stayed the same and what changed |
| 5 | Ear: play the theme in A Aeolian and A Dorian and say which is which | Untimed | The F against F# |
| 5 | Guitar: play the melody in box 1, or strum Am, G, F and Am | 72 bpm | A steady beat |

Repeat this routine on at least three days before you check the boxes below.

## Common mistakes

Changing too much. If the theme is no longer there, undo one change.

Forgetting to play the theme first. The variation only means something after the listener has heard what it varies.

Staying on one kind of change. Try rhythm, register, mode, ornament and harmony in turn.

## Key terms

- **Theme**: a short melody that is played, then varied.
- **Variation**: the theme played again with one thing changed.
- **Grace note**: a quick small note played right before a main note.
