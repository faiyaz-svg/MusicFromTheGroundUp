# The alto and tenor clefs

<span class="tag tag-theory">Theory</span> <span class="tag tag-optional">Optional</span>

After this lesson you can name the notes on the alto and tenor clefs, and play a short melody written in each.

Before this: [The treble staff and C position](../01-first-sounds/09-treble-staff-and-c-position.md), [The bass clef and the grand staff](../02-two-hands-and-first-chords/01-bass-clef-and-grand-staff.md). You need: your piano.

This lesson is optional. You will not meet these clefs in this book's pieces. They matter if you later read orchestral parts, old editions or theory exams that include them.

## The idea

### C clefs

The treble and bass clefs mark where G4 and F3 sit. A third kind of clef marks where middle C (C4) sits. It is called the **C clef**, and the sign is two curves and a vertical bar. The point where the two curves meet is on the line for middle C. Two positions are common.

The **alto clef** puts middle C on the middle line (line 3). Viola music is written in it. The **tenor clef** puts middle C on line 4, counted from the bottom. Cello, bassoon and trombone parts switch to it for high notes.

Play these two examples. Both are the same notes, and both start on middle C.

```abc
X:1
M:4/4
L:1/4
Q:1/4=70
K:C clef=alto
C D E F | G A B c |]
```

```abc
X:1
M:4/4
L:1/4
Q:1/4=70
K:C clef=tenor
C D E F | G A B c |]
```

Look at where the first note sits. In the first example it is on the middle line, and in the second it is on line 4.

### Names of the lines and spaces

| Clef | Lines, bottom to top | Spaces, bottom to top |
|---|---|---|
| Alto | F3, A3, C4, E4, G4 | G3, B3, D4, F4 |
| Tenor | D3, F3, A3, C4, E4 | E3, G3, B3, D4 |

Each row follows the same rule as every staff: going up from a line to the next space is the next letter of the alphabet.

### How to read a C clef

1. Find the C clef's middle C first. It is the line the two curves surround.
2. Count lines and spaces up or down from it. One step up the staff is one letter up.
3. Read the key signature by letter. The sharps and flats sit on different lines than in treble clef, so name each one from middle C instead of copying a treble pattern.

## Common mistakes

Reading by treble-clef habit. Find middle C first, then count.

Mixing up the two C clefs. The curves point to the line: line 3 is alto, line 4 is tenor.

## Key terms

- **C clef**: a clef that marks the line for middle C.
- **Alto clef**: the C clef with middle C on the middle line.
- **Tenor clef**: the C clef with middle C on line 4.
