# Reading drill

The drill writes a new four-bar melody in 4/4 each time you press "New melody". It uses quarter notes and half notes, mostly moves by step, and ends on the tonic, the home note of the key. Because it never repeats exactly, you cannot memorize it. You have to read.

Read the melody first. Find the first note, then read the shape: which way it moves and how far. When you can, play it on the instrument. Then press play to check yourself, and slow the tempo slider down if you need to.

If a note stops you, turn on "Show note names" for a moment, then turn it off and read again.

## Five minutes a day

1. Pick the level you can almost play.
2. Play one melody at 60 bpm without stopping, even if you make mistakes.
3. Check it with the play bar and listen for the notes you missed.
4. Press "New melody" and do three more.
5. Move to the next level when you can play three melodies in a row with no stops.

Look at the whole bar before you play it, so your eyes are ahead of your hands.

Levels 1 and 2 go with the lessons on [the treble staff and C position](../01-first-sounds/09-treble-staff-and-c-position.md) and the bass clef and the grand staff<!-- link: ../02-two-hands-and-first-chords/01-bass-clef-and-grand-staff.md -->.
