# Common mistakes

The mistakes listed at the end of each lesson, collected in reading order. Skim the ones for the stage you are in before a practice session, and come back to this page when something keeps going wrong.

## Stage 1: First sounds

### [How to practise](../01-first-sounds/01-how-to-practise.md)

Playing too fast. A tempo where you stumble practises the stumble. Slow the metronome until three clean in a row is easy.

Skipping the metronome because it feels strict. The click is the thing that shows you when you rush or drag, so keep it on for every exercise that gives a bpm.

Practising once for a long time instead of often for a short time. If you miss a day, play the next day at the normal 30 minutes. Do not try to catch up with an hour.

### [Meet your piano](../01-first-sounds/02-meet-your-piano.md)

Using the pedals as you play. The pedals blur notes together, and a beginner does not yet hear when that helps. Leave your feet flat on the floor until stage 7.

Keeping the volume so low that soft and loud are hard to tell apart. Set the volume so a loud note is comfortable and a soft note is still easy to hear.

Playing with touch sensitivity switched off. Check it in the settings, because an even volume hides how hard you are pressing.

### [Meet your guitar](../01-first-sounds/03-meet-your-guitar.md)

Calling string 6 the "first string" because it is at the top as you look down. In this book string 1 is the thinnest, as in tab and in every chord chart.

Twisting a tuner a long way with no tuner to check. A string can snap or go very sharp. Wait for lesson 6.

Leaving the guitar by a radiator or in the car. Heat and dry air crack the top and lift the bridge.

### [Pitch and the musical alphabet](../01-first-sounds/04-pitch-and-the-musical-alphabet.md)

Looking for C by counting from the left end of the piano. Use the group of two black keys, because it works anywhere on the keyboard.

Mixing up the groups of two and three. Say "C is by the two" aloud for a week.

Thinking a higher octave number means a bigger or thicker note. It only means higher in pitch.

### [Sitting at the piano and finger numbers](../01-first-sounds/05-sitting-and-finger-numbers.md)

Flat fingers. If the last joint of a finger collapses, the finger plays on its nail side and the sound is thin. Curve the fingers and play from the pad.

Lifted shoulders. Tight shoulders pass tension to the wrists. Take a breath and let your shoulders drop before each exercise.

Pressing with the fingers only, instead of letting the arm help. If the fourth and fifth fingers feel weak, let more of the arm's weight go into them.

### [Holding the guitar, tuning and the pick](../01-first-sounds/06-holding-tuning-and-the-pick.md)

Pressing with the flat of the finger. A flat finger touches the next string and mutes it. Curve the finger and press with the tip.

Holding the pick too tight. A tight grip makes a harsh sound and tires your thumb. Hold it firmly enough that the pick does not slip.

Tuning down to the note. If you pass the note, loosen a bit below it and tighten again. The string settles more firmly when it is tuned up.

### [Beat, tempo and note values](../01-first-sounds/07-beat-tempo-and-note-values.md)

Rushing the held notes. Half and whole notes feel long, so beginners cut them short. Keep counting aloud until the next note.

Clapping every beat instead of every note. Clap only where a note starts.

Counting faster when the music gets harder. The count must stay on the click. If you are speeding up, slow the metronome and start again.

### [Bars, time signatures and rests](../01-first-sounds/08-bars-time-signatures-and-rests.md)

Skipping the rest. A rest is a beat you count and do not play. If you drop it, the next note comes early.

Mixing up the time signature numbers. The top is how many beats, the bottom is the kind of note that gets one beat.

Counting 1 2 3 4 in a 3/4 bar. Count the beats the time signature gives you, so that the "1" lands on the first beat of every bar.

### [The treble staff and C position](../01-first-sounds/09-treble-staff-and-c-position.md)

Reading by counting up from the bottom line every time. Learn the five notes by their places (bottom line, first space, second line) until you see them at once.

Forgetting the ledger line. Middle C has a line of its own. If a note sits below the staff with a short line through it, it is C4.

Looking at the hand. The point of C position is that the fingers know their keys. Keep your eyes on the page.

### [First melodies on piano](../01-first-sounds/10-first-melodies-on-piano.md)

Moving the hand out of C position. If you find yourself reaching, check that your thumb is still on middle C. The tune never leaves the five keys.

Rushing the long notes. Half notes need two beats. Count them aloud until the length is automatic.

Playing the rest as a short note. In a rest, lift your fingers and sit still. Count "1 2" of silence in bar 4 of Morning in C.

### [Reading guitar tab](../01-first-sounds/11-reading-tab.md)

Reading the tab upside down. The top line is string 1, the thinnest string, not string 6. Say "top line, thin string" until it is automatic.

Reading the number as a finger instead of a fret. In tab, 3 means fret 3, not the third finger. The finger rule in this lesson only happens to match.

Ignoring the rhythm. The numbers show where to play, not how long. Count from the notes above the tab.

### [Chord diagrams and your first chord](../01-first-sounds/12-chord-diagrams-and-e-minor.md)

Muting strings with the flat of a finger. Curve the fingers until all six strings ring.

Strumming only some of the strings. Start the stroke above string 6 and end below string 1, so none are missed.

Stopping the strumming arm to wait for the next beat. Keep the arm moving; strum on the beat and let it swing back.
