# Common mistakes

The mistakes listed at the end of each lesson, collected in reading order. Skim the ones for the stage you are in before a practice session, and come back to this page when something keeps going wrong.

## Stage 1: First sounds

### [How to practise](../01-first-sounds/01-how-to-practise.md)

Playing too fast. A tempo where you stumble practises the stumble. Slow the metronome until three clean in a row is easy.

Skipping the metronome because it feels strict. The click is the thing that shows you when you rush or drag, so keep it on for every exercise that gives a bpm.

Practising once for a long time instead of often for a short time. If you miss a day, play the next day at the normal 30 minutes. Do not try to catch up with an hour.

### [Meet your piano](../01-first-sounds/02-meet-your-piano.md)

Using the pedals as you play. The pedals blur notes together, and a beginner does not yet hear when that helps. Leave your feet flat on the floor until stage 7.

Keeping the volume so low that soft and loud are hard to tell apart. Set the volume so a loud note is comfortable and a soft note is still easy to hear.

Playing with touch sensitivity switched off. Check it in the settings, because an even volume hides how hard you are pressing.

### [Meet your guitar](../01-first-sounds/03-meet-your-guitar.md)

Calling string 6 the "first string" because it is at the top as you look down. In this book string 1 is the thinnest, as in tab and in every chord chart.

Twisting a tuner a long way with no tuner to check. A string can snap or go very sharp. Wait for lesson 6.

Leaving the guitar by a radiator or in the car. Heat and dry air crack the top and lift the bridge.

### [Pitch and the musical alphabet](../01-first-sounds/04-pitch-and-the-musical-alphabet.md)

Looking for C by counting from the left end of the piano. Use the group of two black keys, because it works anywhere on the keyboard.

Mixing up the groups of two and three. Say "C is by the two" aloud for a week.

Thinking a higher octave number means a bigger or thicker note. It only means higher in pitch.

### [Sitting at the piano and finger numbers](../01-first-sounds/05-sitting-and-finger-numbers.md)

Flat fingers. If the last joint of a finger collapses, the finger plays on its nail side and the sound is thin. Curve the fingers and play from the pad.

Lifted shoulders. Tight shoulders pass tension to the wrists. Take a breath and let your shoulders drop before each exercise.

Pressing with the fingers only, instead of letting the arm help. If the fourth and fifth fingers feel weak, let more of the arm's weight go into them.

### [Holding the guitar, tuning and the pick](../01-first-sounds/06-holding-tuning-and-the-pick.md)

Pressing with the flat of the finger. A flat finger touches the next string and mutes it. Curve the finger and press with the tip.

Holding the pick too tight. A tight grip makes a harsh sound and tires your thumb. Hold it firmly enough that the pick does not slip.

Tuning down to the note. If you pass the note, loosen a bit below it and tighten again. The string settles more firmly when it is tuned up.

### [Beat, tempo and note values](../01-first-sounds/07-beat-tempo-and-note-values.md)

Rushing the held notes. Half and whole notes feel long, so beginners cut them short. Keep counting aloud until the next note.

Clapping every beat instead of every note. Clap only where a note starts.

Counting faster when the music gets harder. The count must stay on the click. If you are speeding up, slow the metronome and start again.

### [Bars, time signatures and rests](../01-first-sounds/08-bars-time-signatures-and-rests.md)

Skipping the rest. A rest is a beat you count and do not play. If you drop it, the next note comes early.

Mixing up the time signature numbers. The top is how many beats, the bottom is the kind of note that gets one beat.

Counting 1 2 3 4 in a 3/4 bar. Count the beats the time signature gives you, so that the "1" lands on the first beat of every bar.

### [The treble staff and C position](../01-first-sounds/09-treble-staff-and-c-position.md)

Reading by counting up from the bottom line every time. Learn the five notes by their places (bottom line, first space, second line) until you see them at once.

Forgetting the ledger line. Middle C has a line of its own. If a note sits below the staff with a short line through it, it is C4.

Looking at the hand. The point of C position is that the fingers know their keys. Keep your eyes on the page.

### [First melodies on piano](../01-first-sounds/10-first-melodies-on-piano.md)

Moving the hand out of C position. If you find yourself reaching, check that your thumb is still on middle C. The tune never leaves the five keys.

Rushing the long notes. Half notes need two beats. Count them aloud until the length is automatic.

Playing the rest as a short note. In a rest, lift your fingers and sit still. Count "1 2" of silence in bar 4 of Morning in C.

### [Reading guitar tab](../01-first-sounds/11-reading-tab.md)

Reading the tab upside down. The top line is string 1, the thinnest string, not string 6. Say "top line, thin string" until it is automatic.

Reading the number as a finger instead of a fret. In tab, 3 means fret 3, not the third finger. The finger rule in this lesson only happens to match.

Ignoring the rhythm. The numbers show where to play, not how long. Count from the notes above the tab.

### [Chord diagrams and your first chord](../01-first-sounds/12-chord-diagrams-and-e-minor.md)

Muting strings with the flat of a finger. Curve the fingers until all six strings ring.

Strumming only some of the strings. Start the stroke above string 6 and end below string 1, so none are missed.

Stopping the strumming arm to wait for the next beat. Keep the arm moving; strum on the beat and let it swing back.


## Stage 2: Two hands and first chords

### [The bass clef and the grand staff](../02-two-hands-and-first-chords/01-bass-clef-and-grand-staff.md)

Reading a bass-clef note as if it were in the treble clef. The lines have different names, so the same position gives a different note. Check the clef before the first note.

Forgetting that the left hand's notes get lower toward the left of the keyboard. Higher on the page is still higher on the piano, even in the bass staff.

Counting up from the bottom line for every note. Use the landmarks (F3 between the dots, middle C) and step from the nearest one.

### [Left hand in C position](../02-two-hands-and-first-chords/02-left-hand-c-position.md)

Mixing up the finger order. The left little finger is on C3 and the thumb is on G3, so the numbers fall as the notes rise. Say "5 4 3 2 1" up the keys until it is automatic.

Letting the thumb lie flat on the key. Play on the side of the tip of the thumb, with the thumb slightly curved.

Reading the bass clef as the treble clef. Check the two dots at the start of the line before you name the first note.

### [Hands together](../02-two-hands-and-first-chords/03-hands-together.md)

Skipping the hands-separate step. Hands together is slower to learn when each hand is still uncertain.

Letting the held left-hand key come up early. Keep the finger on the key until the bar is over.

Looking down at the left hand. Your left hand knows C position now. Keep your eyes on the page.

### [The E and A chords](../02-two-hands-and-first-chords/04-chords-e-and-a.md)

Moving one finger at a time in a change. Lift and place the whole shape together, except where a finger is an anchor.

Looking for the notes while the beat goes by. Stop the strumming, place the shape without hurrying, then restart the count.

Gripping the neck hard. Press only enough for a clear tone; if your hand aches, stop, shake it out, and see a teacher or a doctor if the pain returns.

### [Ties, dots and eighth notes](../02-two-hands-and-first-chords/05-ties-dots-and-eighth-notes.md)

Playing the second note of a tie. Hold the first note and don't play the second.

Rushing the eighth note after a dotted quarter. It is short, but it still waits for the last "&". Count aloud.

Forgetting that the dot is half of the note's own value. A dotted half is 3 beats and a dotted quarter is 1.5 beats; other dotted notes come in later stages.

### [The A minor and D chords](../02-two-hands-and-first-chords/06-chords-a-minor-and-d.md)

Striking the strings the chord doesn't use. Place the pick on the first string you want before the stroke starts.

Muting string 1 in D with the finger on string 2. The three fingers are close together. Arch them more.

Changing the fingers one at a time. Lift and place the shape together, as you did for E and A.

### [Legato, staccato and dynamics](../02-two-hands-and-first-chords/07-legato-staccato-and-dynamics.md)

Playing softly by playing hesitantly. Soft playing needs the same steady rhythm and an even tone; use less speed in the key, not a slower beat.

Making staccato notes too short. Aim for about half the written length, and keep the count steady in the silence.

Using the volume knob for dynamics. The knob changes everything at once. The change should come from your touch.

### [Strumming with down strokes](../02-two-hands-and-first-chords/08-strumming-down-strokes.md)

Stopping the arm to wait for the chord. Keep the arm moving and miss a strum rather than the beat.

Gripping the pick. A tight grip makes a harsh sound and a tired hand. Hold it firmly enough that it doesn't slip, and no tighter.

Speeding up as the music goes on. Practise with the metronome until your strums land on its clicks.

### [Sight-reading basics](../02-two-hands-and-first-chords/09-sight-reading-basics.md)

Playing too fast. Pick a tempo at which you can read everything, and play at that tempo even if it feels slow.

Looking at the hands. Your eyes belong on the page. Learn the position by feel.

Stopping to fix mistakes. This turns a reading exercise into a practice exercise. Fix the line afterwards, in a separate pass.

### [G and F positions](../02-two-hands-and-first-chords/10-g-and-f-positions.md)

Playing C position notes by habit. Check that the thumb (or the left little finger) is on the first note of the line before you start.

Looking at the hand to find the new position. Practise by feel: find the black key groups with your fingertips and then the white key.

Playing a line a position too high or too low. Say the first note aloud before you place the hand.

### [One-minute chord changes](../02-two-hands-and-first-chords/11-one-minute-changes.md)

Moving one finger at a time. Move the whole shape together.

Lifting the fingers high off the strings. A small lift is faster than a big one.

Practising for too long. One-minute changes are an exercise, not a workout. Stop after the 15 minutes in the routine.

### [First chord pieces](../02-two-hands-and-first-chords/12-first-chord-pieces.md)

Reading the melody notes as chords. The notes on the staff are for the melody player; you read the symbols above them.

Rushing the strums to catch up after a change. Hold the beat; play the strum you missed as a rest.

Forgetting the starting string: A starts on string 5, D starts on string 4, and Em and E use all six.


## Stage 3: The major scale and keys

### [Whole steps, half steps, sharps and flats](../03-the-major-scale-and-keys/01-whole-and-half-steps.md)

Calling every move to the next white key a whole step. E to F and B to C are half steps. Check for a black key between the two.

Forgetting that a sharp lasts to the end of the bar. If the same letter appears again in that bar, it is still sharp.

Counting the starting note as step one. A whole step from C is D, one move of two half steps, and the count starts after C.

### [The major scale](../03-the-major-scale-and-keys/02-the-major-scale.md)

Writing A# instead of Bb in the F major scale. Each letter appears once, so after A comes a B-letter, and it is Bb.

Counting the half step as a whole step. Check each step on the keyboard, not by sound alone.

Forgetting that the scale ends on the tonic again. Eight notes make the scale, with the first and last both the tonic.

### [The C major scale and thumb-under](../03-the-major-scale-and-keys/03-c-major-scale-and-thumb-under.md)

Reaching the thumb late. The thumb should start to move while finger 3 is still playing.

Twisting the wrist. Keep the wrist level, and move the elbow and the arm along the keyboard.

Playing louder after the thumb. The thumb is a strong finger, so it hits harder. Listen for equal volume.

If a hand, wrist or arm aches, stop and rest. If it keeps happening, see a teacher or a doctor.

### [Singing the scale by number](../03-the-major-scale-and-keys/04-singing-the-scale-by-number.md)

Singing too high. Start in the middle of your voice, and pick a drone that suits it.

Drifting flat on the way down. Play the drone again and re-match it every second time.

Singing the notes as letter names. The numbers are the tool, and they carry over to every key.

### [Notes on strings 6 and 5](../03-the-major-scale-and-keys/05-notes-on-strings-6-and-5.md)

Forgetting the single-fret gaps. E to F and B to C are one fret apart, and all the other natural notes are two frets apart.

Counting from fret 1 as zero. Fret 0 is the open string, and the first fret is one half step above it.

Tuning while the string is above the note, then stopping. Go below the note and come up to it, so the string stays in tune.

### [The C, G and D minor chords](../03-the-major-scale-and-keys/06-chords-c-g-and-d-minor.md)

Muting string 1 with the C index finger. Arch the finger so only string 2 is pressed.

Strumming the strings marked x. Start the C strum on string 5, and the Dm strum on string 4.

Lifting all the fingers high for a change. Keep them close to the strings.

### [Key signatures with sharps](../03-the-major-scale-and-keys/07-sharp-key-signatures.md)

Playing F natural in G major because no sharp sign is in front of the note. The signature covers the whole piece.

Putting the second sharp on the wrong line or space. Learn the shape from the figure: F on the top line, then C in the third space.

Naming the last sharp as the key. The key is a half step above it.

### [The G and D major scales](../03-the-major-scale-and-keys/08-g-and-d-major-scales.md)

Using the wrong finger on the black keys. Check the figures: in G major the right hand plays F# with finger 4, and in D major it plays F# with finger 3 and C# with finger 4.

Tipping the hand to reach a black key. Keep the wrist level and let the finger reach.

Rushing the second octave. Keep one note per beat and the same volume.

### [Flat keys and the circle of fifths](../03-the-major-scale-and-keys/09-flat-keys-and-the-circle-of-fifths.md)

Naming the last flat as the key. The key is the second-to-last flat.

Writing A# for Bb in a flat key. A scale uses each letter once, so F major has Bb and no A#.

Going the wrong way round the circle. Clockwise adds sharps and anticlockwise adds flats.

### [Minuet in G](../03-the-major-scale-and-keys/10-minuet-in-g.md)

Learning the right hand fast and the left hand never. Give both hands the same number of repeats.

Starting every practice session at the beginning. Vary where you start.

Playing the three beats evenly. A minuet has a lean on beat 1.

### [Reading notation on guitar](../03-the-major-scale-and-keys/11-reading-notation-on-guitar.md)

Reading the notes as piano notes. The guitar sounds an octave lower than the staff shows.

Reading only the tab. Use the staff for the rhythm.

Mixing up the strings at the ledger lines. Count down from middle C on its ledger line.

### [First tunes by ear](../03-the-major-scale-and-keys/12-first-tunes-by-ear.md)

Looking for the first note by name. Look for its number: is it the tonic, or a leap above it?

Trying the whole tune at once. Work one phrase at a time.

Giving up on the key. If the tune does not sound right in C, hum the last note and try another tonic.


## Stage 4: Intervals and triads

### [Interval numbers](../04-intervals-and-triads/01-interval-numbers.md)

Counting from zero, so that C to E becomes a "2nd". Count the lower note as 1: C is 1, D is 2, E is 3.

Counting keys on the piano instead of letter names. The black keys between do not count in the number. C to E has one white key between, but it is a 3rd.

Changing the number when a sharp or flat appears. C to E and C to Eb are both 3rds. Only the quality changes.

### [Interval qualities](../04-intervals-and-triads/02-interval-qualities.md)

Mixing up the number and the size. The number counts letters. The quality comes from the half steps. Do both counts every time.

Calling every 3rd "major" because it is the one you hear most. Check the half steps: 4 is major, 3 is minor.

Writing the wrong letter for the upper note. A minor 3rd above D is F, not E#, because a 3rd must use the third letter (D, E, F). Choose the letter first, then add the sharp or flat.

### [Hearing intervals](../04-intervals-and-triads/03-hearing-intervals.md)

Reading the staff while you listen. The notation gives the answer away, so cover it and trust your ear first.

Trusting a tune from memory without checking it. Play the first two notes on the piano; if they are not the interval you expected, choose another tune for that interval.

Practising only going up. Do some of each direction, because many tunes begin with a downward move.

### [Triads](../04-intervals-and-triads/04-triads.md)

Using the wrong letters. A triad on F uses the letters F, A and C. F minor is F, Ab, C and never F, G#, C. Choose the letters first (every other letter), then add the sharps and flats.

Thinking the 5th decides major or minor. Both use a perfect 5th. The 3rd decides, and the 5th only changes in diminished and augmented triads.

Mixing up the symbols. A capital letter alone (C) is a major triad. A small m (Cm) is minor. Write dim and aug in full.

### [Triads and arpeggios on piano](../04-intervals-and-triads/05-triads-and-arpeggios-on-piano.md)

Playing a rolled chord by accident. The three notes must start together. Drop the arm's weight into the keys at the same moment.

Tense fingers on the 3rd and 5th. Keep the wrist level and the hand relaxed, or the 5th finger will tire.

Changing fingers in the arpeggio. Use 1, 2, 3, 5 up and 5, 3, 2, 1 down for the right hand. Use the same fingers every time, so the pattern becomes automatic.

### [Open seventh chords](../04-intervals-and-triads/06-open-seventh-chords.md)

Strumming string 6 in A7, D7 or B7. Those shapes mute string 6 (and string 5 for D7). Start the strum on the right string and aim the pick.

Treating A7 as the same chord as A. The A chord has no G. A7 adds the note G, and the 7 in each chord name means a minor 7th above the root.

Letting a fretting finger touch an open string. Check each string by plucking it alone.

### [Inversions](../04-intervals-and-triads/07-inversions.md)

Naming an inversion by the top note. Only the lowest note counts. E, G, C and G, C, E are different inversions.

Thinking an inversion is a different chord. It has the same three letters. Only the bass note moves.

Playing a slash chord with the root in the bass. In C/E the lowest note must be E.

### [Inversions on piano](../04-intervals-and-triads/08-inversions-on-piano.md)

Jumping the whole hand to the root position of each chord. Look for the shape that keeps the hand near middle C.

Using the wrong fingering in the F and G shapes. Fingers 1, 2 and 5 keep the hand relaxed. Fingers 1, 3 and 5 would stretch the middle finger.

Playing the left-hand root above middle C. Keep it in the octave below, so the left hand stays under the right.

### [Eighth-note strumming](../04-intervals-and-triads/09-eighth-note-strumming.md)

Stopping the hand on the missed strokes. The hand moves down and up all the time. Only the pick touching the strings changes.

Strumming the up stroke across all six strings. Keep it light and aim for the top three or four strings.

Gripping the pick and the neck too hard. A tight grip makes the strum choppy and tires the hand. If your wrist or forearm hurts, stop and rest, and see a teacher or doctor if the pain returns.

### [Reading by intervals](../04-intervals-and-triads/10-reading-by-intervals.md)

Counting up from the bottom line each time. Count from the nearest landmark, and from the note before it when the move is a step or a skip.

Reading the names but ignoring the direction. If the notes go up the page, the hand goes up the keyboard. Check the direction before you check the letter.

Looking down at the hands. Keep your eyes on the page. If you must look, glance at the keys and return at once to the same bar.

### [Easy classical pieces: a musette and a minuet](../04-intervals-and-triads/11-easy-classical-pieces.md)

Playing through the piece from the start every time. The first bars become smooth and the later bars stay weak. Start sometimes from section C or D.

Practising at full speed. Mistakes you play fast become habits. Use half speed until three plays in a row are clean.

Skipping the survey. A leap or a shift you did not see coming causes the stop. Two minutes of looking saves ten minutes of repeats.

### [Hearing chord quality](../04-intervals-and-triads/12-hearing-chord-quality.md)

Listening only to the top note. In an inverted chord the top note can be the root, the 3rd or the 5th. Listen to the whole chord.

Calling every dark chord diminished. A minor chord is dark but settled. A diminished chord is tense and sounds pinched.

Guessing from the notation. In the self-tests, the staff gives the answer away. Cover it first.


## Stage 5: Minor keys and chord charts

### [The natural minor scale](../05-minor-keys-and-chord-charts/01-natural-minor.md)

Calling A minor "C major starting on A" and then playing it like C major. The notes are the same, but the home note is A. End every phrase on A, and the ear hears A as home.

Forgetting the flat in D natural minor. The scale has Bb. Say "D minor, one flat" before you play.

Mixing up the number of the lowered notes. The 3rd, 6th and 7th are lowered compared with the major scale on the same first note.

### [Harmonic and melodic minor](../05-minor-keys-and-chord-charts/02-harmonic-and-melodic-minor.md)

Playing melodic minor with the raised notes on the way down. Going up F# and G#; going down F and G, the natural notes.

Putting G# in the key signature. A minor has no sharps in the signature, so the G# is an accidental that you write each time it appears in a bar.

Hearing the harmonic minor step as a mistake. The step from F to G# is three half steps wide and sounds wide. It is the shape of the scale.

### [The A minor and D minor scales](../05-minor-keys-and-chord-charts/03-a-and-d-minor-scales.md)

Using finger 4 or 5 on the Bb in D minor. The long fingers sit better on black keys. Use finger 3 in both hands.

Mixing up the forms. In the melodic form, playing the raised notes on the way down. Down is natural.

Forgetting the natural sign in D melodic minor. Going up, the B is natural, so the music has a natural sign in front of it. Without it you play Bb.

### [Chord symbols and chord charts](../05-minor-keys-and-chord-charts/04-chord-symbols-and-chord-charts.md)

Stopping the strumming arm at a bar line to change the chord. Keep the arm moving; the fingers move, the arm does not stop.

Reading the repeat bar sign as an empty bar. It means play the last bar again.

Treating a slash chord as a different chord. D/F# is still a D chord. Only the lowest note differs.

### [Relative and parallel keys](../05-minor-keys-and-chord-charts/05-relative-and-parallel-keys.md)

Mixing up relative and parallel. Relative keys share a signature; parallel keys share a tonic.

Counting a minor 3rd up when you need to go down. For the relative minor of a major key, count down: C down a minor 3rd is A.

Treating the signature as the answer. One sharp could be G major or E minor. Find the tonic from the music.

### [Chords from a lead sheet on piano](../05-minor-keys-and-chord-charts/06-chords-from-a-lead-sheet-on-piano.md)

Playing the melody and chords at the same volume. The melody must be louder. Play the chords with a softer touch.

Looking at your hands at each change. Learn the shape of each triad with the eyes on the page.

Holding the left-hand chord down after the change. Release it exactly on beat 1 as the new chord starts, or the two chords blur.

### [The capo and moving shapes](../05-minor-keys-and-chord-charts/07-capo-and-moving-shapes.md)

Counting frets from the nut, not from the capo. With the capo on fret 2, the shape's first fret is fret 3 in absolute terms.

Reading shape names as sounding chords. The chart says G; the room hears A.

Squeezing the capo too hard. The strings go sharp and the clamp tires your hand. Use the least pressure that stops the buzz.

### [Hearing major and minor](../05-minor-keys-and-chord-charts/08-hearing-major-and-minor.md)

Deciding by speed or volume. The test is the 3rd, not whether the music sounds sad or happy.

Not finding the home note. A melody that starts on its 3rd or 5th can fool you. Wait for where it comes to rest.

Guessing from the first chord only. A progression can start on a minor chord and end major. Listen to the last chord.

### [Schumann: Melody and Soldiers' March](../05-minor-keys-and-chord-charts/09-schumann-album-for-the-young.md)

Playing every note at the same volume. Choose the melody and play the rest softer.

Making the eighth after a dot too long. Say "long, short" aloud and clap it before you play.

Learning the whole piece at once. Learn two to four bars at a time, hands separately, and join them.

### [Fingerpicking basics](../05-minor-keys-and-chord-charts/10-fingerpicking-basics.md)

Letting the thumb play louder than the fingers. Practise p alone with a light touch, then p and i together.

Using the same finger for two strings. Keep i, m and a each on their own string.

Letting go of the chord shape between notes. Press the whole chord down for the whole bar.

### [Broken-chord accompaniment](../05-minor-keys-and-chord-charts/11-broken-chord-accompaniment.md)

Accenting the thumb. The note on the top of the pattern is often too loud. Keep all eight notes the same volume.

Looking at the left hand and losing the melody. Learn the left-hand pattern until you can play it while reading the right hand.

Leaving the chord late. Change to the next chord's root exactly on beat 1.

### [Sight-reading in new keys](../05-minor-keys-and-chord-charts/12-sight-reading-in-new-keys.md)

Reading the key signature and then forgetting it. Circle the changed notes first.

Missing an accidental that came earlier in the bar. Accidentals hold to the bar line, so look back along the bar.

Stopping at a wrong note. Keep the beat and play the next note correctly; fixing it later is what practice is for.

### [Muting and sixteenth-note strumming](../05-minor-keys-and-chord-charts/13-muting-and-sixteenth-strumming.md)

Stopping the arm on the misses. The arm moves on every sixteenth, even when the pick misses the strings.

Strumming too hard. At this speed the arm must be loose, with the wrist doing most of the work.

Pressing a palm mute too hard. The note loses its pitch. Rest lightly.


## Stage 6: Meter, terms and rhythm

### [Compound meter](../06-meter-terms-and-rhythm/01-compound-meter.md)

Counting six equal beats. The count has six numbers but only two beats, so press harder on 1 and 4.

Thinking 6/8 is 3/4 played fast. The total length is the same, but 6/8 groups the notes in threes. Hear the example bars side by side to feel it.

Reading the dotted quarter note as two beats. It holds one beat of three eighth notes.

### [Triplets and syncopation](../06-meter-terms-and-rhythm/02-triplets-and-syncopation.md)

Rushing the triplets into a "1 and a 2 and a" lilt. The three notes of a triplet are equally spaced; say "trip let" evenly with a slow metronome.

Playing the tied note again. After a tie there is no second attack. Hold the first note through the full length of both.

Putting the clap on the beat. In syncopation the clap lands off the beat; the foot stays on it.

### [Tempo and dynamics terms](../06-meter-terms-and-rhythm/03-tempo-and-dynamics-terms.md)

Treating the bpm ranges as exact. They are a rough guide, and the piece, the instrument and the player affect the speed. Use them to set a metronome, then listen.

Slowing down at crescendos or speeding up at diminuendos by habit. Dynamics and tempo are separate. Keep the pulse steady unless a word says otherwise.

Playing every f at the same loudness. A piece with p, mf, f and ff needs four levels you can tell apart. Choose the softest and loudest first, then fit the others between.

### [Articulation and repeat signs](../06-meter-terms-and-rhythm/04-articulation-and-repeat-signs.md)

Playing both endings. The first ending is played once only. On the repeat, skip it and go to the second.

Treating a fermata as a fixed length. The player chooses it, and the music after it begins on a clear count.

Confusing tenuto with legato. Tenuto is one note held to its full length. Legato joins notes together smoothly.

### [6/8 on piano](../06-meter-terms-and-rhythm/05-six-eight-on-piano.md)

Pressing the chord as hard as the bass. The chord is a lighter push on count 4.

Moving the right hand when the left changes chords. The right hand stays in C position for this piece.

Playing three even beats per bar. 6/8 has two beats. Keep the lean on 1 and 4.

### [6/8 strumming](../06-meter-terms-and-rhythm/06-six-eight-strumming.md)

Stopping the hand on missed counts. Keep swinging and miss the strings.

Strumming all six counts equally. Counts 1 and 4 need the extra weight.

Hearing 6/8 as 3/4. If you feel three beats, say the beat count aloud: 1 and 4 only.

### [Fingerpicking patterns](../06-meter-terms-and-rhythm/07-fingerpicking-patterns.md)

Plucking with the fingers flat. Curl the fingertip so the string is pulled across the pad and nail.

Lifting the fretting hand between bars. Keep fingers on the strings until the new chord is ready.

Rushing the first bar. Count three bars aloud before you start.

### [Burgmüller: two easy studies](../06-meter-terms-and-rhythm/08-burgmuller-easy-studies.md)

Starting with the easier hand and leaving the harder one for last. Learn the harder hand alone at half tempo, then add the other.

Playing the left-hand chords as loudly as the melody. In No. 3 the chords are a quiet cushion. Keep them below the right hand.

Skipping the clef change. When the left hand moves into the treble clef, say "treble clef" aloud before the notes.

### [Rhythm by ear](../06-meter-terms-and-rhythm/09-rhythm-by-ear.md)

Writing the notes before the beat is settled. Always count the beat first.

Forgetting rests. A gap between notes is not always a rest: a long note sounds until the next one. Listen for silence, for gaps as well as new starts.

Tapping the rhythm as even beats. Rhythm is long and short, so exaggerate the long notes by holding your hand down.

### [Hammer-ons and pull-offs](../06-meter-terms-and-rhythm/10-hammer-ons-and-pull-offs.md)

Picking the second note. The point is the fretting hand makes it sound.

Hammering too far behind the fret. The note buzzes. Land close behind the fret wire.

Pressing the strings so hard the tune slows down. The tap is quick; relax the hand between taps.

### [Sight-reading rhythms](../06-meter-terms-and-rhythm/11-sight-reading-rhythms.md)

Reading the notes before the time signature. A rhythm in 6/8 and a rhythm in 3/4 can look alike on the page.

Rushing after a rest. Count the rest out loud.

Looking only at the next note. Look one bar ahead.

### [Slides and bends](../06-meter-terms-and-rhythm/12-slides-and-bends.md)

Bending with one finger only. Put fingers 1 and 2 behind it so the push is shared.

Bending too far. A half step is small; compare it with the fret above.

Letting the thumb slip over the top of the neck. Keep it behind the neck for a bend, as for every other note.


## Stage 7: Pedal, texture and ambient

### [The damper pedal](../07-pedal-texture-and-ambient/01-damper-pedal.md)

Pressing the pedal before the chord. Old notes get caught. Press right after the keys go down.

Holding the pedal for a whole page because it sounds nice. Notes from different chords pile up. Lift when the harmony changes.

Pedalling with the toe in the air and the heel up. The foot has no pivot, and the pedal clunks. Keep the heel down.

### [Legato pedalling](../07-pedal-texture-and-ambient/02-legato-pedalling.md)

Changing on the beat with the hands. The new notes are not caught. Move the foot right after the keys.

Holding the pedal through a melody that moves by step. Notes outside the chord pile up. Change more often.

Lifting for too long. A gap between chords breaks the legato. The lift and press are one quick tap.

### [Half pedal, una corda and sostenuto](../07-pedal-texture-and-ambient/03-half-pedal-una-corda-and-sostenuto.md)

Pressing the sostenuto pedal before the keys. It holds nothing. Press it while the keys are down.

Using the left pedal as a loud and soft switch. It changes the tone too. Use the dynamic marks for loudness and the pedal for colour.

Expecting the same results on every digital piano. They differ. Use your manual.

### [Alberti bass and waltz bass](../07-pedal-texture-and-ambient/04-alberti-and-waltz-bass.md)

Letting the left hand grow louder than the melody. The pattern is accompaniment. Play it softly.

Leaning on the top note of the Alberti figure. Keep the four notes even.

Stretching to reach the waltz chord. Move the whole hand from the low note to the chord.

### [Ambient piano](../07-pedal-texture-and-ambient/05-ambient-piano.md)

Playing the melody louder when the notes get high. Keep p or pp all the way.

Changing the pedal on every melody note. The chord has not changed, so the pedal stays.

Rushing the rests. Count them.

### [Sus and add9 chords](../07-pedal-texture-and-ambient/06-sus-and-add9-chords.md)

Muting the open strings with the underside of a finger. Arch the fingers.

Playing the sus chords with all six strings. Dsus2 and Dsus4 use only strings 4 to 1; the A chords use strings 5 to 1.

Treating a sus chord as a mistake. It is a different sound, and it often leads back to the plain chord.

### [Power chords](../07-pedal-texture-and-ambient/07-power-chords.md)

Letting strings 3 to 1 ring. They add a third and sound muddy. Mute them with the underside of finger 1.

Pressing the palm too hard on the muted eighths. The notes die. Rest the hand lightly.

Rushing the change. Count the beat out loud and move early.

### [Hearing chord progressions](../07-pedal-texture-and-ambient/08-hearing-progressions.md)

Listening to the top notes instead of the bass. The bass shows the chord. Sing the lowest note.

Expecting every progression to come home. Some end on V on purpose.

Guessing from the notation. Cover the page and use your ears.

### [Voicing the melody](../07-pedal-texture-and-ambient/09-voicing-the-melody.md)

Playing everything at one loudness. The tune disappears. Decide which layer sings.

Making the left hand quiet by lifting it. The pattern gets uneven. Press gently with fingers close to the keys.

Pressing all five fingers equally in a chord. Put the weight on the melody finger.

### [Tchaikovsky: Old French Song](../07-pedal-texture-and-ambient/10-tchaikovsky-album-for-the-young.md)

Playing the held left-hand G as loudly as the tune. Keep it quiet.

Pedalling through bars 17 to 19. The staccato disappears. Leave the pedal up.

Speeding up the sixteenth-note figure in bar 6. Slow it down until it is even.

### [Travis picking](../07-pedal-texture-and-ambient/11-travis-picking.md)

Letting the thumb speed up or drop out when the fingers play. The thumb is the clock. Practise it alone first.

Plucking with a bent, tense wrist. The hand tires. Keep the wrist relaxed.

Hitting the thumb strings with the fingers. Check which string each finger plays.

If a hand, wrist or arm aches, stop and rest. If it keeps happening, see a teacher or a doctor.

### [Ambient guitar arpeggios](../07-pedal-texture-and-ambient/12-ambient-guitar-arpeggios.md)

Playing too loud. Ambient music is soft. Keep the pick-hand movement small.

Muting a string with a fretting finger. Arch the fingers.

Rushing the changes. Keep the count and let a change be late rather than stopping.

If a hand, wrist or arm aches, stop and rest. If it keeps happening, see a teacher or a doctor.


## Stage 8: Fretboard, barre chords and CAGED

### [Every note on the fretboard](../08-fretboard-barre-chords-and-caged/01-fretboard-all-notes.md)

Forgetting that string 2 is different. Strings 3 and 2 are a major 3rd apart, not a perfect 4th, so the octave rule changes from 2 frets to 3 frets there.

Learning flats and sharps before the naturals. Do the naturals first; the sharps are the frets in between.

Naming a note by counting from the nut every time. Use the fret markers and the octave shapes.

### [E-shape barre chords](../08-fretboard-barre-chords-and-caged/02-e-shape-barre-chords.md)

Placing finger 1 on top of the fret wire. The barre goes close behind it, as for a single note.

Pressing with the soft pad of the finger. The strings sink into the gaps in the skin and buzz. Roll toward the bony edge.

Practising for twenty minutes straight. The hand tires and form gets worse. Short blocks with rests build strength faster and safely.

### [A-shape barre chords](../08-fretboard-barre-chords-and-caged/03-a-shape-barre-chords.md)

Strumming string 6 in an A-shape chord. The open low E clashes with the chord. Start your strum on string 5.

Letting the barre finger sag in the middle. The strings under the bend buzz. Press with the bony edge and keep the finger straight.

Playing every chord as a barre chord. The open chords you know sound fuller and are easier; use a barre when a chord has no open shape.

### [Barre chord pieces](../08-fretboard-barre-chords-and-caged/04-barre-chord-pieces.md)

Stopping the beat at a hard change. Keep the pulse going and let one chord be a little muted, then fix it slowly. A steady beat matters more than a clean chord.

Pressing the barre harder at the change. Press only as hard as the chord needs to ring; the squeeze tires the hand.

Starting the whole piece at full speed. Learn each four-bar section slowly and add the metronome.

### [The CAGED chord shapes](../08-fretboard-barre-chords-and-caged/05-caged-chords.md)

Forgetting that open strings become fretted. The moved A shape needs the barre because the open strings 5 and 1 now sit at the fret.

Playing a string that does not belong. A moved shape can leave a string out; the diagram shows an x for it.

Skipping the rests. These shapes stretch the hand more than the earlier barre chords. Stop if anything hurts.

### [CAGED scale shapes](../08-fretboard-barre-chords-and-caged/06-caged-scale-shapes.md)

Playing a block with the wrong fingers. Plan the fingering of each string once, then keep it the same every time.

Treating the blocks as separate. They overlap, so a note near the edge of one block is also in the next.

Practising a block faster than the metronome. Slow practice is how the shape becomes automatic.

### [Reading higher up the neck](../08-fretboard-barre-chords-and-caged/07-fretboard-reading.md)

Reading the tab and ignoring the staff. In this lesson the staff is the only guide, and you decide the string and fret. That is the skill.

Staying in one position when the music goes out of it. Stretching one finger past its fret hurts after a while; shift instead.

Looking at the hand on every note. Learn the map first, look at the page, and glance at the neck only at shifts.

### [All twelve major scales](../08-fretboard-barre-chords-and-caged/08-all-major-scales.md)

Playing the thumb on a black key. It twists the wrist; use the starting fingers in the table.

Using the right-hand fingering for the left hand. The hands differ in B, F and the group 4 scales, so check both columns.

Raising the tempo before the scale is even. A fast uneven scale practises the unevenness.

### [Scales and arpeggios hands together](../08-fretboard-barre-chords-and-caged/09-scales-and-arpeggios-hands-together.md)

Rushing the second octave. Set the metronome and keep the same tempo from the first note to the last.

Lifting the shoulders as the hand climbs. Let the arms follow the hands up the keyboard and stay loose.

Practising only the scales you like. Rotate through the keys so every scale is in reach.

### [Schumann: The Happy Farmer](../08-fretboard-barre-chords-and-caged/10-the-happy-farmer.md)

Playing the chords as loud as the tune. The tune is the point; keep the right hand light and short.

Learning the piece from the first bar every day. The hard sections are later, so start some days at bar 9 or 13.

Letting a right-hand chord arrive early, on top of a left-hand note. In bar 1 the chords fill the gaps (eighths 2, 3, 6 and 7); count "1 & 2 & 3 & 4 &" aloud and place them.

### [Sight-reading hands together](../08-fretboard-barre-chords-and-caged/11-sight-reading-hands-together.md)

Stopping at a wrong note. Keep the beat and play the next note.

Choosing too fast a tempo. Pick a tempo at which the hardest bar is still possible.

Reading every left-hand note one at a time. See the chord or pattern first.

### [Hearing intervals 2](../08-fretboard-barre-chords-and-caged/12-hearing-intervals-2.md)

Confusing a major 7th with an octave. A major 7th sounds nearly right and wants to move; the octave is calm.

Naming by the quality alone. Always count the letters first (C up to A is a 6th), then listen for major or minor.

Ignoring the lower note when singing. Sing from the lower note each time.


## Stage 9: Harmony

### [Roman numerals](../09-harmony/01-roman-numerals.md)

Writing the numeral from the chord symbol's letter. The numeral counts scale notes from the key's tonic. In G major, D is V, not IV, because D is the 5th note of the G scale.

Using the wrong case. Dm in C major is ii (lower case) because it is a minor chord. Writing II means D major, which is not in C major.

Forgetting that minor keys change. The natural minor v is Em, and the V in the harmonic minor is E major. Both appear in music, so check the chord symbol.

### [The chords of a key](../09-harmony/02-diatonic-chords.md)

Thinking the numerals are fixed chords. IV is F in C major and C in G major. Always say the key first.

Treating vi as a different kind of chord from I. It is a minor chord, but it shares two notes with I and does the same job (home), with a darker colour.

Playing the dominant long and the tonic short. Hold the V for the full bar and let the I ring, so you can hear the arrival.

### [Cadences](../09-harmony/03-cadences.md)

Calling every V to I a perfect cadence. If the V is not a real chord change at the end of a phrase, it is only part of a progression. A cadence sits at the end of a phrase.

Confusing the half cadence with the perfect cadence because both involve V. In a half cadence V is the last chord. In a perfect cadence V comes before the last chord.

Forgetting the raised 7th in minor. A perfect cadence in A minor needs E major (with G#), not Em.

### [Seventh chords](../09-harmony/04-seventh-chords.md)

Writing a major 7th as a flat 7th. Cmaj7 has B natural. C7 has Bb. The note names show the difference.

Calling every seventh chord a dominant 7th. The dominant 7th is the one built on the 5th note of the scale, with a major triad and a minor 7th. The others have their own names.

Playing the 7th as loud as the root. Let the 7th be the softest note, so the chord colour is clear.

### [Seventh chords on piano](../09-harmony/05-seventh-chords-on-piano.md)

Playing the chord with finger 4 instead of 5. Finger 4 is weak and short. The span from the root to the 7th is easier with finger 5 on the top note.

Hitting the keys one after another. Press all four down together and listen for a clean block.

Playing V7 and I at the same volume. Let the I chord be a little softer, as the music relaxes.

### [Major 7 and minor 7 barre shapes](../09-harmony/06-maj7-and-min7-barre-shapes.md)

Strumming string 6. The shapes have no string 6 note, and a low open E ruins the chord. Mute it or skip it.

A thumb that sits too low on the neck. Keep it behind the neck near the middle, so the barre has strength.

Squeezing too hard. A barre needs the right place for the finger, not extra force. Stop if your hand hurts, and see a teacher or a doctor if it keeps happening.

### [Cadences at the piano](../09-harmony/07-cadences-at-the-piano.md)

Lifting the whole hand between chords. Keep the hand in one place and move the fingers that need to move.

Forgetting the key signature in the new key. In F major the IV chord has Bb, and in D major the V chord has C#.

Playing the left-hand root with the wrong octave. If the left hand sits too high it muddies the right hand. Stay in the bass clef.

### [Minor-key progressions on guitar](../09-harmony/08-minor-key-progressions.md)

Playing Em where the music has E. The two chords differ by one note, and the minor v has no leading tone. Check the chord symbol.

Letting the barre F buzz. Press with the side of the index finger and lay it flat on strings 1 and 2.

Strumming too hard on the first beat. A small accent is enough to show the bar.

### [Non-chord tones](../09-harmony/09-non-chord-tones.md)

Calling every note outside the chord a passing tone. Check how it arrives and where it goes: a neighbour returns to the note it left, and an escape tone leaps away.

Missing the tie in a suspension. The note must be held from the old chord. A note that is only repeated is not a suspension.

Labelling by the beat alone. Appoggiaturas and suspensions are on strong beats. Passing and neighbour tones are usually on weak beats, but they can fall on strong ones too.

### [Chopin: Prelude in A major](../09-harmony/10-chopin-prelude-in-a-major.md)

Rushing the long-short figure. Count the dotted eighth as three sixteenths and the next note as one.

Pedalling through the bass change. The pedal from the old bass sustains the E into the A. Lift the pedal as the bass key goes down.

Playing the chords louder than the melody. The melody is the top note.

### [Hearing chord progressions 2](../09-harmony/11-hearing-progressions-2.md)

Listening to the top note instead of the bass. The top note often stays the same across several chords. Follow the lowest note.

Guessing from the notation. In the self-tests the staff gives the answer away, so cover it first.

Calling every dark chord vi. Check the bass number: vi has bass 6, ii has bass 2 and iii has bass 3.

### [Analysing a piece](../09-harmony/12-analysing-a-piece.md)

Finding the key from the first chord only. Many pieces start away from home. Check the key signature and the last chord.

Naming a chord from the melody notes. The melody has non-chord tones; the bass and the held notes show the chord.

Writing numerals without checking the key. In A minor, the chord on F is VI, and in C major it is IV.


## Stage 10: Classical repertoire

### [Fingerstyle posture and studies](../10-classical-repertoire/01-fingerstyle-posture-and-studies.md)

Playing the same finger twice in a row on a melody. It sounds uneven and slows you down. Alternate i and m.

Plucking by lifting the string away from the guitar. The string should be pulled across and released. Curl the whole finger toward the palm.

Squeezing the neck because the strings feel stiff. Press with only enough force to stop the buzz, and keep the thumb relaxed behind the neck.

### [Giuliani: right-hand studies](../10-classical-repertoire/02-giuliani-right-hand-studies.md)

Pressing the bass notes harder than the upper notes. The thumb is strong, so soften it, or the melody disappears.

Letting a bass note ring over a rest. Steel strings ring longer than nylon, so touch the string to stop it when the score shows a rest.

Practising from bar 1 every time. Start at the hard spot, then go back one bar.

### [Carulli: a waltz](../10-classical-repertoire/03-carulli-study.md)

Playing the waltz too slowly because each eighth note gets a big beat. Feel one bar as one dance step, "ONE two three", with the weight on the 1.

Playing the bass as loud as the chords. The bass is on the beat only. Pluck it softly and let the chords sing above it.

Forgetting to stop the bass on a rest. On steel strings, touch the string with a finger to cut it.

### [Sor: two lessons from Opus 60](../10-classical-repertoire/04-sor-op-60.md)

Playing the high and low notes with the same weight. The bass note should be quieter and short, and the tune notes should sing.

Reading a high note as part of a new melody. In the jumping bars, the low and high notes belong to two lines that happen to share one staff.

Missing the accidental. A sharp stays until the bar line, and a natural sign cancels it. Circle each one first.

### [Carcassi: a study from Opus 60](../10-classical-repertoire/05-carcassi-op-60.md)

Letting the run speed up in the middle of the bar. Count 1 & 2 & 3 & 4 & against the metronome and keep each eighth note the same length.

Dropping the held bass note early. Steel strings keep ringing, but a bass note can fade if the finger lifts. Keep the left-hand finger down for the full length.

Playing the sharps late. Mark each one in pencil first, and learn the shape before the speed.

### [Clementi: Sonatina in C, first movement](../10-classical-repertoire/06-clementi-sonatina-op-36-1.md)

Counting four beats in a bar. In 2/2 the count is "1 2", with half notes as the beats.

Tightening the wrist in bars 20 and 21. The octave jump is a small bounce from a loose arm, not a stretch.

Rushing the runs. Play each run at the speed of the slowest bar, and keep the left hand quiet.

### [Burgmüller: Arabesque](../10-classical-repertoire/07-burgmuller-arabesque.md)

Playing the left hand chords too long. They are staccato, short and light, and a heavy left hand covers the right.

Dropping the first note of a slurred group. Lift the hand at the end of each slur, and start the next group with a fresh, small movement.

Taking the wrong ending. Mark the first and second endings in pencil before you play.

### [Burgmüller: Ballade](../10-classical-repertoire/08-burgmuller-ballade.md)

Playing every chord at the same volume. The piece starts piano, builds to forte, and the return is pianissimo. Mark each dynamic in pencil.

Counting three beats in a bar. In 3/8 at this speed there is one beat, so count "1 2 3" in eighth notes, fast.

Missing the naturals in the left hand. B natural and A natural cancel flats from the key signature. Circle them.

### [The Minuet in G on guitar](../10-classical-repertoire/09-bach-minuet-for-guitar.md)

Letting the bass ring over a change of harmony. Touch the string with a finger to stop it.

Playing the high bars out of tune because the fretting hand is far from the body. Press close behind the fret, and move the whole hand with the fingers.

Rushing through the repeats. Count the repeat as a full pass.

### [Sight-reading classical pieces](../10-classical-repertoire/10-sight-reading-classical.md)

Reading every note. A run is a first note, a last note and a direction, so read it as one shape.

Stopping for a wrong note. Keep the beat and come back to the bar on the next pass.

Skipping the scan. Most mistakes come from a sharp or a repeat you did not see before you started.

### [The eras of classical music](../10-classical-repertoire/11-music-eras.md)

Treating the dates as hard borders. A composer born in 1810 can write in a Romantic style and also use older forms.

Mixing up the era and the style. "Classical music" in everyday speech means all of it, while the Classical era is only 1750 to 1820.

Judging an era by one piece. Listen for the features in several pieces first.

### [Aguado: a little piece](../10-classical-repertoire/12-aguado-study.md)

Playing the dotted sixteenth note too long. The thirty-second note is very short, so count it carefully.

Forgetting the new key signature at bar 17. Say "one flat" before the first bar of the F major part.

Reading all three voices at once. Start with the top voice, then the bass, then the middle.


## Stage 11: Film, game and anime harmony

### [The royal road progression](../11-film-game-and-anime-harmony/01-the-royal-road.md)

Starting on the wrong chord. The royal road starts on IV, not on I. In C major the first chord is F, not C.

Reading iii and vi as major. Lower-case numerals are minor chords: Em and Am in C major. Play them as minor or the progression loses its bittersweet sound.

Rushing the sevenths. Playing four notes in the right hand makes the chords heavy. Use the three-note upper triad from the last example.

### [The royal road on piano](../11-film-game-and-anime-harmony/02-royal-road-on-piano.md)

Playing the melody and chords at the same volume. Make the melody the loudest part, as in stage 7: keep the left hand quieter.

Forgetting the F# in G major. The key signature covers it, but the notes of the D and Bm chords sit in the middle of the staff and the sharp is easy to lose. Say "F sharp" under your breath at the Bm.

Stretching for the tenth with a tight hand. If your left hand hurts or cannot reach, play the root and fifth only, or stop and rest. Pain is a stop sign.

### [The royal road on guitar](../11-film-game-and-anime-harmony/03-royal-road-on-guitar.md)

Strumming string 6 on Fmaj7. The shape starts on string 4, so aim the pick there.

Letting the pattern speed up. The eight notes are even eighths. Count "1 & 2 & 3 & 4 &" out loud at 60 bpm.

Squeezing the barre. Press only as hard as needed. If your hand hurts, stop, shake it out, and see a teacher if the pain returns.

### [Aeolian loops](../11-film-game-and-anime-harmony/04-aeolian-loops.md)

Changing the G to G7 or the E to E major at the end of the loop. That adds a leading tone and a pull, and the loop turns into an ordinary minor cadence. Keep the VII a plain major triad.

Counting the numerals from the wrong scale. VI and VII in a minor key are chords on the 6th and 7th notes of the minor scale, not the major one.

Slow F changes on the guitar. Place finger 1 on the first string early while the other chord is still playing.

### [Borrowed chords and the Mario cadence](../11-film-game-and-anime-harmony/05-borrowed-chords-and-the-mario-cadence.md)

Calling Ab, Bb, C the same as VI VII I. In C major, VI would be Am and VII would be Bdim. The flat signs matter.

Playing Bb as B. The borrowed chord has a flatted root. Read the flat sign before you move.

Using the fan name as a technical term. Write bVI bVII I when you talk to a teacher or write a score.

### [The minor iv and the Picardy third](../11-film-game-and-anime-harmony/06-minor-iv-and-the-picardy-third.md)

Writing Fm as IV. The numeral must be lower case, iv, because the chord is minor.

Raising the third in the wrong place. The Picardy third is only the last chord. If the C# appears earlier in a bar, the key changes to A major.

Muting the barre. For Fm, press finger 1 across all six strings evenly. If string 3 buzzes, move the pressure closer to the fret.

### [Pedal points and ostinatos](../11-film-game-and-anime-harmony/07-pedal-points-and-ostinatos.md)

Letting the held note die. The bass note must keep sounding. Play it firmly, or use the damper pedal, as in stage 7, and change the pedal only when the harmony above is clear.

Playing the ostinato louder than the changing part. The pattern is a background. Keep it even and quiet.

Confusing a pedal point with the damper pedal. A pedal point is a note in the music, written with notes. The damper pedal is a foot action, written as Ped.

### [An arpeggio prelude](../11-film-game-and-anime-harmony/08-arpeggio-prelude.md)

Rushing the eighths in the easy part. The first section is the place to set the speed. Keep the metronome on.

Pedalling over a chord change. The old chord keeps ringing under the new one. Press the new bass note, then change the pedal.

Playing every note at full strength. The texture is a wash. Play lightly, from the fingertips, and let the dynamics come from the whole bar.

### [Chromatic mediants](../11-film-game-and-anime-harmony/09-chromatic-mediants.md)

Moving every finger. One note stays. Find it first and keep it down.

Calling any chord from outside the key a chromatic mediant. It must be a third from the tonic and have the same quality.

## Stage 12: Modes and improvising

### [The modes](../12-modes-and-improvising/01-modes.md)

Thinking a mode uses new notes. It does not. A mode is the same seven notes with a different home, which changes the order of whole and half steps.

Playing the right keys with no home note. Without a drone, bass note or chord, the notes lean toward the nearest major or minor key. Hold the first note of the mode.

Mixing up parallel and white-key versions. D Dorian on the white keys has no sharps or flats, but C Dorian has Eb and Bb. The mood is the same; the notes are not.

### [Hearing the modes](../12-modes-and-improvising/02-hearing-modes.md)

Listening for the whole scale. Listen for one note against the drone instead, because it shows up in the first bars.

Guessing from mood alone. Moods are a tendency (Phrygian is often dark, Lydian often bright), and a slow Lydian tune can sound sad. Confirm with the 3rd and the altered note.

Forgetting the drone. Without a held home note, the ear picks a different home and the answers change. Keep the left hand down.

### [Lydian and the sound of wonder](../12-modes-and-improvising/03-lydian-wonder.md)

Playing the B flat from habit. The key of F major has a Bb, but F Lydian does not. If the sound feels ordinary, check for it.

Letting the left hand lead. The fifths are the background. Play them softer than the melody and let them ring.

Treating G as a different key. The G chord belongs to F Lydian. The tune stays in F, and the chord adds the B colour.

### [Left-hand patterns for improvising](../12-modes-and-improvising/04-left-hand-patterns-for-improvising.md)

Playing the pattern too loudly. The right hand needs to be heard over it, so keep the left hand at p.

Changing the pattern while you improvise. The left hand is the backdrop. Pick one pattern and stay with it until the right hand is comfortable.

Holding the pedal through the chord change. If you use the pedal in the vamp, change it as the left hand moves from D to G, or the two chords will blur.

### [Improvise in Dorian](../12-modes-and-improvising/05-improvise-in-dorian.md)

Filling every beat. A phrase needs rests to be heard. Count a beat of silence after each idea.

Stopping at a wrong note. Keep the beat, play the next note, and use the odd note as a motif if it sounds interesting.

Wandering without a home. Begin and end on D, and come back to D after every few bars. That is how the ear knows the mode.

### [Improvise in Lydian](../12-modes-and-improvising/06-improvise-in-lydian.md)

Going back to F major by habit. The Bb is always the wrong note in this lesson. Keep your fourth finger off the black key.

Playing the same phrase every time. Change the first note or the rhythm each time you loop.

Ignoring the chord. When the loop moves to G, aim for a note of that chord at the start of the bar, and then walk to the next.

### [Ambient improvising](../12-modes-and-improvising/07-ambient-improvising.md)

Playing too much. If you cannot hear the chord, you are playing too much. Take notes away until you can.

Playing too loud. Use p or pp, so the melody sits on the chord.

Holding the pedal across a chord change. Change it right after the new low note, or the old and new chords blur.

### [The minor pentatonic scale](../12-modes-and-improvising/08-minor-pentatonic.md)

Letting the hand drift. Your index finger should stay at fret 5 for the whole box. If it moves, slow down.

Stopping at a wrong note. If a note sounds odd, move to the next note in the box and keep the beat.

Playing without the loop. The scale is a set of notes. The loop makes it a sound, so play along with it.

### [The five pentatonic boxes](../12-modes-and-improvising/09-pentatonic-boxes.md)

Learning the shapes without the root. Find the A notes first, or you will not know where home is.

Bending too fast. Take the bend slowly and aim for a pitch you can hear against the fretted note.

Gripping too hard on slides. Press lightly while the finger is moving, and press firmly when it lands.

### [Improvise over film progressions](../12-modes-and-improvising/10-improvise-over-film-progressions.md)

Playing the same note on every chord. Move the idea to the chord, or use the table, so the note changes with the chord.

Running out of ideas. Say less. One idea played twice, with a rest, fills four bars.

Speeding up. The loop is the clock, so keep with it, and slow it with the tempo slider if you need to.

### [Call and response](../12-modes-and-improvising/11-call-and-response.md)

Ending both phrases on A. Then the call has no question in it. Pick another note for the call.

Answering with unrelated notes. Start like the call, so the ear hears it as an answer.

Rushing the rest. The rest at the end of the call is the pause before the answer. Count it.

### [Variations on a theme](../12-modes-and-improvising/12-variations-on-a-theme.md)

Changing too much. If the theme is no longer there, undo one change.

Forgetting to play the theme first. The variation only means something after the listener has heard what it varies.

Staying on one kind of change. Try rhythm, register, mode, ornament and harmony in turn.
